## EN2-17 — Active and passive voice — Second Term

**Objectives:** identify who performs an action; change active to passive without changing tense; explain when the actor can be omitted.

**Teaching:** In an active sentence, the subject performs the action. A passive sentence makes the receiver the subject. Use the appropriate form of **be** and the main verb's past participle. Keep the original tense. Add **by** and the actor when that information matters. Not every verb has an object that can become a passive subject.

**Worked example:** “The pupils cleaned the classroom.” The action is past; the receiver is the classroom. Therefore: “The classroom was cleaned by the pupils.” “The prefect checks the register” becomes “The register is checked by the prefect.”

**Practice:** Change (1) The gardener waters the plants. (2) Ada repaired the chair. (3) The team will paint the gate. Explain why “The baby sleeps” cannot be changed in the same way.

**Answers:** (1) The plants are watered by the gardener. (2) The chair was repaired by Ada. (3) The gate will be painted by the team. “Sleeps” has no object here.

**Source:** PASSIVE, British Council. Assess tense, agreement, participle and retained meaning separately.

## EN2-08 — Reflexive and relative pronouns — Second Term

**Objectives:** distinguish a pronoun referring back to the subject from a pronoun introducing a relative clause; use each in a complete sentence.

**Teaching:** Use a reflexive pronoun when the subject and object refer to the same person or group: “I taught myself the song.” Forms include myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves and themselves. A relative pronoun links a noun to more information: “The pupil who won is smiling.” **Who** commonly refers to people, **which** to things, and **whose** to possession. Do not use a reflexive pronoun simply because it sounds formal.

**Practice:** Complete: (1) We prepared the posters ____. (2) The girl ____ bag was lost reported it. (3) This is the desk ____ needs repair. Explain the function of the added word in each sentence.

**Answers:** ourselves; whose; which/that. The first refers back to “we”; the others introduce relative clauses.

**Sources:** REFLEX and RELATIVE. This replaces the blank-page task; it does not invent an Oxford page number.

## EN2-15 — Expository essay — Second Term

**Objectives:** explain a topic clearly; organise an introduction, developed paragraphs and conclusion; distinguish explanation from unsupported opinion.

**Teaching:** An expository essay helps the reader understand a topic or process. State the focus early. Give each body paragraph a clear point, followed by explanation or an example. Arrange a process in its actual sequence. Use connecting words where they clarify relationships. End by bringing the explanation together rather than introducing an unrelated topic.

**Worked plan: How our class keeps its reading corner organised.** Introduction: explain the purpose. Paragraph 1: classify books. Paragraph 2: record borrowing. Paragraph 3: return and check books. Conclusion: explain how the system makes books easy to find.

**Practice:** Write 180–220 words using this plan. Exchange work with a partner, who identifies one clear explanation and one point needing detail.

**Marking:** focus 4; logical organisation 4; developed explanation/examples 6; grammar and spelling 4; relevant conclusion 2. Accept different effective wording.

**Source:** EXPOS, Purdue OWL. The plan, assignment and rubric are newly authored.

## PE-01 — Bookkeeping ethics — Second Term

**Objectives:** explain transparency, accountability and probity; apply them to a simple class-business record.

**Teaching:** A useful financial record must be understandable and honest. Transparency means making relevant information clear. Accountability means being able to explain and justify how money was handled. Probity means acting with integrity. Keep receipts, record the full amount, explain a correction instead of hiding it, and follow the agreed approval procedure. A record that balances can still be dishonest if a transaction was omitted.

**Scenario:** A class receives ₦8,000 for materials. It spends ₦5,500 and holds ₦2,500. A receipt is missing. The treasurer proposes recording only ₦5,000 spent and keeping ₦500.

**Questions:** What should the record show? Which ethical duties are involved? How should the missing receipt be handled?

**Answers:** Record the actual ₦5,500 expense and ₦2,500 balance. Do not retain ₦500 privately. Explain the missing evidence, seek a duplicate or approved supporting record, and report the discrepancy to the responsible adult.

**Source:** ETHICS. The scenario is original. This is basic recordkeeping education, not financial advice.

## PE-02 and PE-03 — Display and types of headings — Second Term

**Objectives:** organise a document visibly; distinguish the main heading from subheadings; apply horizontal and vertical placement.

**Teaching:** Display is the deliberate arrangement of text on a page. A main heading identifies the whole document; subheadings identify its sections. Consistent alignment, spacing and emphasis make the structure easier to follow. Horizontal placement concerns the left-to-right position. Vertical placement concerns position from top to bottom. A visually attractive page must still communicate its purpose accurately.

**Worked task:** Design an A4 notice for a school reading afternoon. Include one main heading, two subheadings, date, time, place and contact person. Produce a left-aligned version and a version with a centred heading. Explain which you prefer and why.

**Assessment:** all required information 5; heading hierarchy 3; readable spacing/alignment 3; consistent style 2; justified choice 2. Several layouts may be correct. Do not mistake retail merchandise displays for document display.

**Source:** DISPLAY, NOUN BED112, Module 2 Unit 1. Two sessions: first create the notice; then compare and improve layouts.

## PE-04 — Proofreading and speed development — Second Term

**Objectives:** carry out stated corrections accurately; distinguish speed from accuracy; improve a repeated typing task.

**Teaching:** A proof mark instructs a correction, such as deleting, inserting, replacing, transposing, changing case or starting a paragraph. Read the mark with the affected text. A fast copy with errors is not a correct copy. Check your work before submitting it.

**Practice:** The teacher supplies a short original notice with six explicit editing instructions: insert a missing word, delete a repeated word, exchange two words, capitalise a name, correct one spelling and begin a new paragraph. Produce a fair copy. Then type a separate 100-word passage twice, recording time and errors each time.

**Class measurement:** For this exercise only, gross words per minute = words typed ÷ minutes. Report the number of uncorrected errors separately; do not hide errors inside a speed score. Compare accuracy first, then speed.

**Assessment:** one mark for each correct edit; explain whether the second timed attempt improved accuracy, speed or both.

**Sources:** PROOF and DISPLAY. A verified visual proof-mark chart still needs to accompany the app version; verbal instructions alone do not assess recognition of all symbols.

## PE-05 — Scale drawing — First Term

**Objectives:** interpret a ratio scale; convert units before calculation; draw and label a simple shape to scale.

**Teaching:** A scale of 1:50 means one unit on the drawing represents fifty of the same units in reality. Drawing length = actual length ÷ 50. Actual length = drawing length × 50. State the units and scale; do not assume a resized screen image can be measured accurately with a ruler.

**Worked example:** A 4 m wall is 400 cm long. At 1:50 its drawing length is 400 ÷ 50 = 8 cm.

**Practice:** (1) Draw a 6 m × 4 m rectangular room at 1:100. (2) A line measures 3.5 cm on a 1:20 drawing. Find the actual length. (3) Explain why measuring an enlarged phone screenshot gives unreliable results.

**Answers:** (1) 6 cm × 4 cm, with scale and dimensions labelled. (2) 70 cm. (3) Screen resizing changes the relationship between displayed size and original drawing size.

**Sources:** SCALE and BLUEPRINT. The numerical examples are original.

## PE-06 — Simple blueprint reading — First Term

**Objectives:** locate title, scale and dimensions; distinguish a plan from an elevation; answer questions using labelled dimensions.

**Teaching:** Begin with the drawing title and revision information so that you know what is represented. Read the scale, units, labels and symbol key. A plan looks down from above; an elevation shows a side. Dimension labels give intended sizes. If a print or image has been resized, its stated dimensions remain more reliable than measuring the display.

**Practice drawing specification:** Draw a plan rectangle labelled room, 5 m × 3 m. Mark a 0.9 m door opening on one long side and a 1.2 m window opening opposite. Add a title, units, scale 1:100 and a key. The learner identifies the door and window, states both room dimensions, and calculates floor area.

**Answers:** 5 m and 3 m; area 15 m²; the openings measure 0.9 m and 1.2 m in reality. At 1:100 they occupy 0.9 cm and 1.2 cm on an accurately printed drawing.

**Source:** BLUEPRINT. The specified drawing must be supplied visually before this becomes a complete app lesson.

## PE-07 — Statistics II: averages — Second Term

**Objectives:** calculate mean, median and mode; explain why they can differ.

**Teaching:** The mean is the sum divided by the number of values. To find the median, put the values in order and find the middle; with an even number of values, average the middle two. The mode is the most frequent value; there may be more than one mode or no unique mode.

**Worked example:** For 2, 4, 4, 5, 10, the sum is 25 and there are five values. Mean = 5; median = 4; mode = 4. The largest value raises the mean.

**Practice:** Find all three averages for (1) 3, 5, 5, 7; (2) 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 7. Explain which values occur most often.

**Answers:** (1) mean 5, median 5, mode 5. (2) mean 3, median 2.5, modes 2 and 3. Award method marks for ordering, summing and identifying frequency.

**Source:** STATS, OpenStax Prealgebra 2e §5.5. Data sets and questions are newly authored.
