## Recommended single route: a Yoruba teacher or performer supplies the passage
Why this one: the school has Yoruba teachers; one source can supply the passage, the checked transcription and translation, provenance and permission for exactly the intended use; no third-party copyright is involved; and it avoids relying on a Scribd copy whose lines are unchecked and whose genre fit is unproven.

**What the supplier must provide (all of it)**
1. **Passage:** one Ọya pípè or oríkì Ọya of at least three lines, written one performed line per line, with full tone marks and under-dots. Words not heard or not certain are marked, not guessed.
2. **Genre label as the supplier knows it** (Ọya pípè, oríkì Ọya, or other) and the occasion it belongs to.
3. **Checked transcription:** a second Yoruba reader who has checked the written lines against the performance or the original page (name and date).
4. **Translation:** named translator, line-aligned English, stated as literal or free; untranslatable words left in Yoruba with a note.
5. **Provenance:** who composed or performed it; for a recording, performer, place, date and occasion; for a written source, title, author, year, edition and page/line range; who collected and transcribed it.
6. **Permission covering the intended use:** the supplier's (and, if different, the performer's or community's) written consent to display these exact lines, with translation and the school's teaching notes, to enrolled students inside the school app, with the credit wording they want, whether excerpting and adding notes is allowed, and who to contact if consent is withdrawn.
7. **Suitability:** the supplier's view that the lines suit JSS3 pupils.

Once supplied, the repository work is already defined: lesson ID `SUP-YO-OYA-TEXT` (First Term), attribution record bound to the exact body hash, tests, and the owner's distribution choice under `YO_LICENSE/OWNER_DECISION.md`. The hash requirement is met by hashing the supplied file/recording or consent letter privately and storing only the SHA-256.
