## Decisions
**D1. Which routes may be tried? (any subset)**
- (a) Ask the rights holder of Olajubu (1977) for permission to use one named iwì egúngún chant.
- (b) Ask the author of Aransi (2024) where the èsà excerpt came from.
- (c) Ask a Yoruba teacher, elder or chanter to supply a short Ṣàngó pípè and Ọya pípè (and iwì egúngún) text, with their consent and a named transcriber.
- (d) Have someone with the full text check Johnson (1921) for printed Yoruba Ṣàngó, Ọya and Egúngún passages.

**D2. Is èsà egúngún acceptable as the scheme's "iwì egúngún" for text study?** The existing genres lesson records NOUN YOR 215 as pairing them; the openly licensed papers examined here do not say so. Yes lets a verified èsà text count; No keeps it open until a source says iwì.

**D3. Optional stand-in.** Kolawole (2023) prints Èṣù pípè lines already checked on 19 October 2026. Teaching them as text study would cover the pípè form but not Ṣàngó or Ọya, and would not close YO-SL-089 or YO-SL-094. Approve as a labelled stand-in, or leave it out.

Until D1 returns a usable passage, YO-SL-089 and YO-SL-094 stay PARTIAL.
