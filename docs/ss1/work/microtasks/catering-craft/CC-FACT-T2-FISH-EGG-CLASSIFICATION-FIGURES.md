## Findings
1. **Egg water content:** composition figures vary with source, egg size and analytical basis. University of Illinois Extension gives approximate edible-portion water values of 88% for white and 48% for yolk. The source's figures should be checked against a named food-composition table before reuse; this note does not replace them with a single universal value.
2. **Egg size classes:** USDA consumer size classes are defined by minimum net weight per dozen, not by a single fixed weight threshold for every egg. USDA lists Jumbo 30 oz/dozen, Extra Large 27 oz, Large 24 oz, Medium 21 oz, Small 18 oz and Peewee 15 oz. The source's repeated 53/63/73 g boundaries do not match that U.S. standard and may reflect another grading system or a transcription error. No local Nigerian standard was established in this review; do not silently replace it with U.S. grades.
3. **Mollusc shell description:** the statement that molluscs have a single shell is overgeneralised. Clams, scallops and oysters are bivalves with a two-part hinged shell. Molluscs include multiple classes, some with one shell, two shells, reduced internal shell or no external shell.
4. **Lean/fatty fish:** FAO describes lean fish as species that store most lipids in the liver, with typical examples including cod, saithe and hake; fatty fish store more lipid in tissues. This is a useful classification principle, but the source's examples must be checked species-by-species. Tuna is not a safe blanket example of lean fish; fat content varies by species and body region.
5. **Dried fish protein:** drying removes water and can raise protein concentration per 100 g of product without creating additional protein. The wording “weighs more protein” is not technically clear and should not be silently changed in the source. A research study reports higher protein per 100 g in dried fish powder as water is removed; composition depends on species and drying method.
6. **“Dressed fish”:** this is retained as the source's wording. It may refer to fish that has been prepared/gutted, but the exact intended meaning requires context and is not inferred here.
