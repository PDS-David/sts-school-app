## Findings
1. **Pasteurisation:** the source's 65°C for 30 minutes is not the standard batch combination in the cited Codex and FDA materials. Codex CXC 57-2004 gives minimum whole-milk conditions with equivalent bactericidal effect of 63°C for 30 minutes (batch) or 72°C for 15 seconds (continuous flow). FDA's Grade A Pasteurized Milk Ordinance gives the same two principal combinations. This is a factual discrepancy, not a claim that these are the only validated processes.
2. **Sterilisation:** a single generic “120°C for up to an hour” statement is not an adequate universal specification. The FSSAI dairy-term definitions distinguish sterilisation in sealed containers from UHT processing and describe product- and process-specific conditions. A corrected teaching statement must distinguish the process and applicable standard rather than substitute one universal time.
3. **UHT:** FSSAI defines UHT treatment as at least 135°C for one second or more in continuous flow, followed by aseptic packing in hermetically sealed containers. This corroborates the source's “at least 135°C for 1 second” figure in that regulatory context; it does not mean every country uses identical legal wording.
4. **Milk storage duration:** no single safe duration can be assigned from the source statement alone. Storage depends on whether milk is pasteurised or UHT, packaging integrity, temperature, date marking and the manufacturer's after-opening instruction. The Hong Kong Centre for Food Safety explicitly advises following the product's after-opening instruction for UHT milk; the Indiana State Board of Animal Health notes shelf life varies with processing, packaging and storage. A fixed number of days should not be taught without a defined product and label.
5. **Digestibility:** the source's broad claims at lines 415–424 are not sufficiently specific to validate as universal statements. Digestibility depends on the food, processing and individual tolerance; no numerical or medical claim is added here.
6. **Fruit freezing:** the source's below −18°C figure is consistent with a common frozen-storage target, but it should be framed as a freezer-storage condition, not a guarantee of indefinite quality or safety.
7. **Apple storage:** the source's 1–4°C range is too broad to present as a universal optimum. USDA commercial-storage tables and ASHRAE guidance show apple storage temperatures vary by cultivar; many varieties use around 0–1°C, while some chilling-sensitive varieties require warmer conditions. A cultivar-specific range is necessary.
