## Findings
1. **Classification:** “game” is a culinary and regulatory category whose meaning depends on jurisdiction, species and context. The source's three groups are a source-specific classification, not a universal zoological taxonomy or a legal permission list.
2. **Eagles and other protected wildlife:** eagle species are subject to species-specific conservation and trade controls; many are listed under CITES. An example in a school source must not be read as authorisation to hunt, trade or serve it. Verify the exact species and applicable national wildlife law before using any example.
3. **Alligator:** the source's single-word example is insufficient to establish which species or legal status is intended. Species, origin, permits and local wildlife law must be checked before any food-service use is discussed.
4. **Fat and digestibility:** the claim that all game is less fatty and more digestible than poultry/meat is overgeneralised. Published comparative data show substantial variation by species and cut; no universal nutritional comparison is supported.
5. **Hanging:** hanging can be used for controlled chilling/ageing of some carcasses, but it is not “essential for all game”. Official wild-game guidance stresses rapid cooling, clean field dressing and temperature control; hanging is conditional on suitable cool conditions and protection from contamination. A warm ambient one-hour or indefinite hanging instruction would be unsafe.
6. **Storage:** game meat is perishable. Official guidance recommends prompt cooling and refrigeration/freezing; specific storage times depend on species, cut, processing and temperature. Do not use the source's blanket statement as a storage schedule.
