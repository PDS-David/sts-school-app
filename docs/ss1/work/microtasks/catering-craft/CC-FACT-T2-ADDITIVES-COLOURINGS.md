## Findings
1. **“Coal tar” terminology:** this is historically associated with some synthetic organic colour additives, but it is not an accurate blanket description of all artificial food colours. FDA notes that certified colour additives are generally synthetic and are now mainly made from raw materials obtained from petroleum or coal; its consumer guidance describes multiple chemical classes and both certified and certification-exempt categories.
2. **Regulatory approval:** in the United States, colour additives must be approved for their intended use and listed in regulations before use in food; some also require batch certification. Approval is specific to permitted food categories, amounts and labelling conditions. This is U.S. law, not a statement of Nigerian or universal legal requirements.
3. **Natural versus synthetic:** “natural” does not automatically mean unrestricted or risk-free, and “synthetic” does not by itself mean prohibited. Regulatory status depends on identity, purity, intended use and jurisdiction.
4. **Flavourings and enhancers:** the source's broad lists do not establish that every listed substance is approved for every food or permitted at any amount. No ingredient is endorsed or given a dosage here.
5. **Local regulatory gap:** before teaching a compliance list for the intended market, check the current rules and permitted lists of the competent national food regulator. This record does not substitute U.S. FDA material for local law.
