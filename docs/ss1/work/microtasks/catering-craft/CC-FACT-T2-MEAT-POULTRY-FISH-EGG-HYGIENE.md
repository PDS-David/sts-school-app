## Findings
1. **Raw poultry washing/rinsing:** CDC states raw chicken is ready to cook and does not need washing; washing can spread germs to hands, surfaces and nearby foods. Food Standards Scotland likewise advises against washing raw chicken/meat. The source's lime/lemon rinse should not be taught as a food-safety control.
2. **Poultry hanging:** the source's one-hour hanging instruction is not a validated food-safety storage step. Perishable raw poultry must be kept cold; USDA FSIS advises prompt refrigeration and a refrigerator at or below 40°F/4.4°C. A time at ambient temperature is not a substitute for controlled chilling.
3. **Live/dressed poultry and offal:** storage depends on product condition, packaging, temperature and time. No generic safe duration can be inferred from the source. Treat raw poultry and offal as perishable foods; keep refrigerated or frozen under applicable local guidance and prevent raw juices contacting ready-to-eat food.
4. **Meat smoking/canning:** phenols and carbonyl compounds can occur in smoke, but naming chemical classes does not establish that any particular home-smoking process is safe or that the compounds alone preserve the food. Canning safety depends on validated process, food acidity, container and pressure/temperature controls; the source's brief statements are not an operational canning recipe.
5. **Papain:** papain is a proteolytic enzyme used as a meat tenderiser; the source's mention does not specify a product, amount or validated process. No dose or application instruction is added.
6. **Egg freshness tests:** shake and float/salt-water tests may indicate changes associated with age/air-cell size, but they do not establish that an egg is free of pathogens or safe to eat. Use date/label, storage and safe handling guidance; do not use a float result as a safety clearance.
7. **Fish handling:** keep raw fish cold and separate from ready-to-eat foods; no fish-specific storage duration is supplied or invented in this record.
