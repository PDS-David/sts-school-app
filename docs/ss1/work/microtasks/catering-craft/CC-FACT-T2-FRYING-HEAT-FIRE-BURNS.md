## Findings
1. **Smoke from frying oil:** smoke is a warning that oil is overheated. The U.S. National Fire Protection Association advises turning off the burner/removing the pan carefully when smoke appears; the Hong Kong Centre for Food Safety advises changing oil when it starts smoking or foaming or develops unusual colour/odour. “Blue haze” is not a reliable universal temperature indicator and should not be taught as a target stage.
2. **Water and hot oil:** wet food can cause hot oil to splatter. Fire services advise drying food before adding it to hot oil. Water must never be poured onto an oil fire; it can cause burning oil to erupt/spread.
3. **Oil-fire response:** NFPA advises sliding a lid over a pan fire only if safe, turning off the burner and leaving the lid in place while the pan cools; never use water. If not safe or fire persists, evacuate and call emergency services. This is general home-fire guidance, not a substitute for local institutional fire procedures or training.
4. **Oil cooling/straining:** the source's instruction to cool oil before straining is directionally consistent with avoiding handling hot oil, but it gives no validated safe cooling method, filtration equipment or temperature. Do not strain hot oil or add operational steps beyond approved workplace procedures.
5. **Burning/charring:** excessive heat can burn food and create fire/burn hazards, but the source gives no measured temperatures or validated cooking endpoints. Do not infer a universal smoke point or oil-decomposition threshold.
6. **Pressure cooker:** line 114 is only a mention; this item supplies no operating, pressure-release or maintenance instructions.
