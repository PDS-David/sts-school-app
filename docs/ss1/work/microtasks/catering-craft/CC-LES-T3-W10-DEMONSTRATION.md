# SS1 Catering Craft — Third Term, Week Ten
## Project-authored equivalent lesson: Demonstration of table laying and setting

**Status:** Draft implementation for review. The source contains only a Week Ten topic heading and no teaching body. This is a project-authored equivalent, not recovered source text and not an observed classroom demonstration.

### Learning objectives
By the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:
1. Identify equipment for a basic place setting.
2. Explain how a setting changes with the menu and service style.
3. Demonstrate a hygienic and safe one-person cover.
4. Inspect and clear a completed setting safely.

### Key terms
- **Table setting:** the planned arrangement of tableware for a meal.
- **Cover:** the complete place setting for one diner.
- **Mise en place:** preparation of the service area and equipment before service.

### Equipment
A stable table; clean tablecloth or placemat; dinner plate; fork; dinner knife; spoon if required by the menu; side plate and side knife if required; clean drinking glass; napkin; tray or service plate; polishing cloth; and inspection checklist.

### Teaching notes
A good setting is clean, stable, orderly and appropriate to the menu. Arrangements vary by establishment, meal and service style; this basic arrangement is not universal. Centre the main plate in front of the chair. Place the fork to the left and the knife to the right with its cutting edge facing the plate. Add a spoon to the right of the knife when required. Place a side plate to the left when required, and position the drinking glass above the knife area where it is stable and reachable. Arrange multiple pieces in order of use, with the first-course implement furthest from the plate. Add only items required by the menu and service plan.

Handle cutlery by the handles and glassware by its stem or lower part. Do not touch eating surfaces or the inside of glasses. Remove chipped, cracked, stained or damaged items from service. Keep clean equipment separate from used equipment. Follow the establishment's instructions where they differ from this basic classroom arrangement.

### Demonstration procedure
1. **Prepare the area:** wash and dry hands; clean and sanitise the table using the approved procedure; check that the table is stable and chairs are safely positioned.
2. **Check the service plan:** identify the meal and menu, then select only the crockery, cutlery, glassware and linen required.
3. **Inspect equipment:** check cleanliness and look for cracks, chips, stains or damage. Set defective items aside for replacement.
4. **Prepare the covering:** spread a clean tablecloth or place a clean placemat as required; align it neatly and ensure it creates no snag or trip hazard.
5. **Place the main plate:** centre it in front of the chair, leaving sufficient space for the diner and service.
6. **Place cutlery:** put the fork left and knife right, blade facing inward. Add a spoon only when required. Arrange multiple pieces in order of use.
7. **Add optional items:** place a side plate left when required; position clean glassware securely above the knife area; add a napkin neatly.
8. **Complete the setting:** check alignment, spacing, cleanliness, stability and suitability for the menu. Keep items safely away from the table edge.
9. **Inspect and reset:** view the setting from the diner's position; replace missing, dirty, damaged or misplaced items. Carry clean items on a clean tray.
10. **Clear down:** separate used items from clean stock, wash and sanitise reusable equipment according to approved procedure, dry and store safely, and clean the work area.

### Safety and hygiene
Keep hands, equipment and work surfaces clean. Do not mix clean and used tableware. Do not reach across another learner or a seated diner with sharp cutlery or glassware. Carry breakable items carefully on a suitable tray and do not overload it. Pick up broken glass only with approved tools, never bare hands. Keep walkways clear and report spills immediately. Follow school or workplace procedures for cleaning chemicals and equipment.

### Learner practical activity
In pairs, prepare one basic cover for a stated menu. One learner lays the cover while the other uses the inspection checklist; then they change roles. Each learner explains why each selected item is needed and identifies one change for a different menu. This is a proposed classroom activity, not a claim that a practical has taken place.

### Assessment checklist
- Hygienic preparation of hands, work area and table — 3 marks.
- Appropriate selection and inspection of equipment — 3 marks.
- Neat table covering and centred main plate — 2 marks.
- Correct and safe cutlery placement — 4 marks.
- Appropriate optional items and stable glassware — 2 marks.
- Final inspection for cleanliness, damage, stability and alignment — 3 marks.
- Safe handling, clean/used separation and clear-down — 3 marks.

**Total: 20 marks.** All safety-critical behaviours must be demonstrated; correct an unsafe act immediately and repeat the step before sign-off.

### Review questions
1. What is meant by a cover?
2. Why should a setting match the menu and service style?
3. Where are the fork and dinner knife placed in a basic setting?
4. Give three checks to make before using crockery or glassware.
5. State two ways to prevent contamination when handling tableware.
