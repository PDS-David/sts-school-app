## Findings
1. **Trolley movement:** the INFLIBNET hospitality text *Styles of Service* also states that a gueridon trolley should be pushed rather than pulled to help control and steer it. This corroborates the source's service convention. It is not a substitute for workplace-specific risk assessment, training or safe load limits.
2. **Hot-plate position:** no authoritative general standard was located requiring hot plates or food warmers to be placed on the left side for every gueridon layout. The position should be treated as a layout convention dependent on trolley design, operator handedness, guest position and service procedure, not a universal safety rule.
3. **Hot plates and food transfer:** hot serving ware and hot liquids create burn/scald hazards. The source gives no equipment specification, safe handling tool, heat rating or detailed procedure. A practical lesson must use the establishment's approved heat-resistant handling equipment and supervised procedure; none is invented here.
4. **Open-flame service:** the INFLIBNET text notes that gueridon cooking can involve naked flames in the dining area and requires adequate precautions. This requires a separately approved fire-risk procedure; the source's short lines are not a complete operational safety standard.

## Disposition
Keep the source's “left-hand side” and push-not-pull wording unchanged. Teach trolley pushing only as a conventional service practice supported by the hospitality reference; do not assert a universal left-side rule. Hot-plate handling and open-flame operation require a local workplace/school safety procedure before practical teaching. No lesson, coverage, correction, answer or practical observation is created here. No tests, importer, database or device runs were performed; testing remains deferred by the owner.
