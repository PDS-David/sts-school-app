## Findings
1. **Serving at the “right temperature”:** this is a qualitative instruction, not a measurable standard. USDA FSIS advises cooked/reheated food be held hot at or above 140°F/60°C in its U.S. guidance. Codex and national food codes may specify different or more detailed controls for particular foods, processes and jurisdictions. The source itself contains no numeric holding temperature.
2. **Hygiene and sanitation duties:** keeping premises, utensils and service materials clean is consistent with general food-hygiene principles. WHO's Five Keys emphasise keeping clean, separating raw and cooked foods, cooking thoroughly, keeping food at safe temperatures and using safe water/raw materials. The source's general statements do not specify a validated cleaning method or chemical concentration.
3. **Cleaning table-laying materials:** the source's requirement for clean tableware is consistent with general hygiene guidance. It does not identify a sanitiser, contact time or chemical concentration; none is invented here.
4. **Housekeeping and safety:** the listed duties are broad job responsibilities. They do not provide an operational checklist, hazard assessment or emergency response. A later practical lesson must use current workplace/school procedures.
5. **Cover/napkin “all precautions” statement:** the phrase is too vague to validate as a specific hygiene control. No extra meaning is assigned to it.
6. **No hidden numerical instruction:** the source does not state a holding temperature, allergen rule, cleaning chemical/concentration or first-aid procedure in the cited passages. Do not infer any from the general wording.
