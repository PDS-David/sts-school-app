## EDE: consonant classification decision

The three valid criteria for classifying consonants are **place of articulation**, **manner of articulation** and **state of the glottis / voicing**. The source's duplicated or inconsistent labels are replaced by these standard headings.

### Eight places of articulation

| Place | Yoruba consonants |
|---|---|
| Bilabial | b, m |
| Labiodental | f |
| Alveolar | t, d, s, n, r, l |
| Palato-alveolar | ṣ, j |
| Palatal | y |
| Velar | k, g |
| Labial-velar | p, gb, w |
| Glottal | h |

### Seven manners of articulation

| Manner | Yoruba consonants |
|---|---|
| Plosive / stop | b, t, d, k, g, p, gb |
| Fricative | f, s, ṣ, h |
| Affricate | j |
| Nasal | m, n |
| Tap / flap | r |
| Lateral | l |
| Approximant | y, w |

### State of the glottis

- **Voiced:** b, d, g, gb, j, m, n, r, l, w, y. The vocal folds vibrate during production.
- **Voiceless:** t, k, p, f, s, ṣ, h. The vocal folds do not vibrate during production.

### Complete consonant inventory and IPA

The eighteen standard graphemes are `b, d, f, g, gb, h, j, k, l, m, n, p, r, s, ṣ, t, w, y`. The orthographic `p` represents the voiceless labial-velar stop /k͡p/, not a plain English /p/; `gb` is /ɡ͡b/; `j` is /d͡ʒ/; `ṣ` is /ʃ/. Keep lowercase `l` distinct from capital `I`, and never replace `ṣ` with a second `s`.

The replacement lesson includes a clean eight-place-by-seven-manner matrix. It restores missing `p`, `b`, `g` and `y`, puts `j` in the affricate row, `ṣ` in the palato-alveolar fricative row, `l` in the lateral row, and `w` in the labial-velar approximant cell. The source's garbled Yoruba glosses for the technical labels are not copied; clear English labels and plain Yoruba explanations are used instead.

## ASA: food, crops and practical dishes

- Correct `Ouje` to **`Oúnjẹ`** and `aranko` to **`ẹranko`**. Use clear crop names: `iṣu`, `àgbàdo`, `gbàgùdà`, `ọ̀gẹ̀dẹ̀`, `ẹ̀wà` and vegetables.
- Replace the incomplete/garbled proverb at line 1103 with an original explanatory sentence: **`Oúnjẹ ń fún ara ní agbára; iṣẹ́ àgbẹ̀ àti iṣẹ́ àwọn olùpèsè oúnjẹ ń mú kí oúnjẹ dé ọjà àti sí ilé.`** Do not attribute it to an elder or present it as a traditional proverb.
- Teach `ìyán` as boiled yam pounded until smooth.
- The source's “àmàlà prepared from plantain” is taught as a **plantain-flour variant** (`àmàlà ìyẹ̀fun ọ̀gẹ̀dẹ̀`), not as the only kind of àmàlà. Explain that yam-flour and cassava-flour versions also exist.
- Teach `àdùn` as a maize snack made from roasted maize flour, palm oil and seasoning; processor recipes vary.
- Add the three practical dishes that the source asks learners to prepare but does not teach:
  - **Gbẹ̀gìrì:** peeled beans are cooked until soft, blended or mashed, then simmered with palm oil and seasoning to make a smooth bean soup.
  - **Ẹ̀kuru:** soaked/dehulled beans are ground with a little water, wrapped in clean leaves or suitable food-safe wrapping, and steamed until firm.
  - **Ìkọ̀kọ̀rẹ̀:** peeled water yam is grated, seasoned and cooked in small portions in a pepper-and-palm-oil sauce with fish or other protein; it is an Ijebu water-yam dish.
- Add explicit food hygiene and kitchen safety: clean hands and utensils, keep raw and cooked foods separate, cook beans thoroughly, and use adult supervision for knives, hot oil and boiling water.
- The source strings `on pese`, `ire-oko`, `omi hiho`, `omorogun`, `egeere`, `eree` are not reconstructed by guess. The replacement uses clear food names and process steps.

## LITIRESO: five elements of literary analysis

Use the following consistent terms:
- **Kókó ọ̀rọ̀:** theme or central idea.
- **Lílò èdè:** language use and literary devices.
- **Àṣà Yorùbá:** cultural practices, values and social context represented in the work.
- **Ẹ̀dá ìtàn:** character; identify the protagonist as the central character.
- **Ibùdó ìtàn:** setting, including place and time.

The replacement lesson includes an original short story, “Tádé àti àpò owó”, and a worked analysis of its theme, language, culture, characters and setting. The story is newly authored; it is not presented as a source text or a traditional tale.
