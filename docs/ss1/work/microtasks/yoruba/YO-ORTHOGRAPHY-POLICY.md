# Yorùbá Orthography Policy for SS1 Teaching Material

Decision status: **Adopted as the working school-writing standard; teacher/subject-review approval remains pending.**

## Governing standard
Use the Revised Official Orthography for the Yorùbá Language approved by the Joint Consultative Committee on Education (JCCE) in June 1974 as the base standard for formal school writing. This is supported by Tèmítọ́pẹ́ Olúmúyìwá's 2013 scholarly discussion of Yoruba writing standards and by F. O. Asahiah's 2014 research on standard Yoruba orthography and tone.

## Letter inventory and under-dots
- Standard alphabet: a, b, d, e, ẹ, f, g, gb, h, i, j, k, l, m, n, o, ọ, p, r, s, ṣ, t, u, w, y.
- Treat **e / ẹ**, **o / ọ**, and **s / ṣ** as distinct letters.
- Use the under-dotted forms **ẹ, ọ, ṣ** wherever the standard lexical form requires them. Do not replace them with e, o or s for convenience.
- Treat **gb** as a single Yorùbá consonant/digraph in alphabet teaching.
- Do not invent under-dots or tone marks where the lexical form does not require them; verify uncertain words against a reliable Yorùbá dictionary or competent speaker.

## Tone marking
- High tone: acute accent (´).
- Low tone: grave accent (`).
- Mid tone: unmarked in ordinary standard orthography.
- Do not add a macron to every mid-tone vowel in ordinary school spelling. A macron may be used in a clearly labelled technical phonology chart, but it is not the standard everyday writing mark.
- Do not use a caron as a standard Yorùbá tone mark.
- Tone marks apply to syllabic nasals where the word requires them (e.g. **ń**, **ǹ**). The tone must be lexically and contextually checked.

## Word division and nasalised pronouns
Use standard word boundaries. Examples include:
- **ta ni**, **kí ni**, **wí pé**, **gẹ́gẹ́ bí**, **nítorí pé**, **nígbà gbogbo**, **jẹ́ kí**, **bí ó tilẹ̀ jẹ́ pé**.
- **fún un**, **nà án**, **dín in**, **pọn ọn**.

Do not join separate particles because they are spoken together. Do not detach a nasalised object pronoun from its standard written form.

## Older and standard spellings
Older spellings may be quoted in a source-history table, but must be labelled as historical/older forms. Use the standard form in learner-facing examples and answers. Examples documented in the 1974 orthography discussion include:
- Aiya → **Àyà**
- aiye → **ayé**
- yio → **yóò**
- ẹiyẹ → **ẹyẹ**
- lailai → **laelae**
- On → **Òun**
- onjẹ → **Òunjẹ**
- ọkọrin → **Ọkùnrin**

Do not infer a spelling correction merely because two words look similar. The pair **Okorin → Okunrin** is not accepted as a standard pair without source recovery and qualified linguistic confirmation.

## Encoding and display
- Store text in Unicode and normalize to NFC before delivery.
- Preserve the precomposed under-dotted letters; tone marks may require combining marks after the under-dot letter. Verify rendered output in the actual font/UI so marks are not clipped or detached.
- Never strip diacritics in a “plain text” fallback if the result is intended as correct Yorùbá teaching material.
- Preserve tone marks and under-dots in answer keys, assessment prompts, tables, headings and search/display strings.

## Quality gate
A Yoruba lesson is not marked student-ready merely because the text contains some diacritics. Every Yoruba lexical item, example, answer and assessment must be checked for:
1. correct under-dotted letter;
2. correct high/low/mid tone;
3. correct syllabic nasal form;
4. correct word division;
5. consistent standard spelling.

When a lexical tone or historical spelling cannot be established from an authoritative reference, record a precise review item instead of guessing.
