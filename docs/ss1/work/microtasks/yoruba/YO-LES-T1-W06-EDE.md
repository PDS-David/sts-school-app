# Yoruba SS1 — First Term, Week 6, EDE: Sílẹ́bù (Syllable)

## Learning objectives

By the end of the lesson, students should be able to:
1. Explain what a syllable is.
2. Identify the three basic syllable structures in Standard Yorùbá: V, CV and N.
3. Separate a word into its syllables and label each syllable's structure.
4. Distinguish a syllabic nasal from a nasal vowel.
5. Explain why an ordinary Yorùbá syllable does not end in a consonant coda.

## 1. Meaning of a syllable

A **syllable** is a unit of pronunciation organised around one sound peak (the nucleus). In Yorùbá, the syllable is also the basic tone-bearing unit: a syllable is pronounced with a tone even when the tone is not written with an accent mark.

A word may contain one syllable or several syllables. When we divide a word into syllables, we show the parts in the order in which they are pronounced. A vertical bar or hyphen can be used to show the division.

Examples:
- `fò` — one syllable.
- `adé` — two syllables: `a | dé`.
- `olùgbàlà` — four syllables: `o | lù | gbà | là`.

Do not confuse a syllable with a letter. A syllable is a unit of pronunciation, not simply one written character.

## 2. The three basic Yorùbá syllable structures

The basic structures taught in this lesson are:

| Symbol | Name | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| V | Vowel syllable | A vowel forms the syllable by itself | `a` in `a | dé` |
| CV | Consonant + vowel | A consonant is followed by a vowel | `dé` in `a | dé`; `fò` |
| N | Syllabic nasal | A nasal consonant forms a syllable by itself | `m` in `Bí | m | bọ́ | là` |

**V** means vowel. **C** means consonant. **N** means a syllabic nasal.

### A. Vowel syllable (V)

A vowel can form a syllable on its own. In the examples below, the vowel is separated from the next syllable:

- `adé` → `a | dé` → `V | CV`
- `èédú` → `è | é | dú` → `V | V | CV`

The first syllable in `adé` is `a`; the second is `dé`. In `èédú`, the three syllables are `è`, `é` and `dú`.

### B. Consonant-plus-vowel syllable (CV)

A consonant followed by a vowel can form one syllable. The consonant is the beginning of the syllable; the vowel is its sound peak.

Five verified examples, with the source's floating arrows replaced by clear text:
- `ké` → `k + é` → `C + V` → `CV` (to cry/shout).
- `fò` → `f + ò` → `C + V` → `CV` (to jump).
- `fẹ́` → `f + ẹ́` → `C + V` → `CV` (to want).
- `gbà` → `gb + à` → `C + V` → `CV` (to accept).
- `sùn` → `s + un` → `C + nasalised V` → `CV` (to sleep).

Remember that `gb` is one Yorùbá consonant letter/sound, not two separate syllables. Therefore `gbà` is one CV syllable. In `sùn`, the `n` indicates vowel nasalisation; it is not a separate syllable or a consonant coda.
### C. Syllabic nasal (N)

In some Yorùbá words, a nasal consonant can stand alone as a syllable. In the JCC-approved orthography described in the National Language Centre manual, the syllabic nasal is written:
- `m` before `b`;
- `n` before other consonants;
- `ng` before vowels in one spelling convention, with `n` accepted as an alternative in the approved orthography.

Examples from published Yorùbá pronunciation references:
- `Bímbọ́lá` → `Bí | m | bọ́ | là` → `CV | N | CV | CV`
- `aláńgbá` → `a | lá | ǹ | gbá` → `V | CV | N | CV`
- `ó ḿbọ̀` → `ó | ḿ | bò` → `V | N | CV` (the `m` is written before `b`).
- `ó ńké` → `ó | ń | ké` → `V | N | CV` (the `n` is written before another consonant).
- `ng ò lọ` → `ng | ò | lọ` → `N | V | CV` (`ng` is the syllabic nasal spelling used before a vowel in this example; the approved orthography also permits `n`).

The `m`, `n` and `ng` forms above are supported by the National Language Centre orthography manual. The tone on a syllabic nasal should be shown where needed to distinguish it from an `n` that marks vowel nasalisation.

## 3. Tone marks and Yorùbá spelling

The JCC-approved orthography adopted for education and WAEC use distinguishes three tones:
- high tone: acute accent, for example `á`;
- low tone: grave accent, for example `à`;
- mid tone: no accent mark when the text marks all tones, as a teaching manual should.

If a writer marks only selected words, a macron may be used on an otherwise all-mid-tone word to avoid ambiguity. The syllabic nasal should also receive a tone mark where it could be confused with the `n` used to show vowel nasalisation.

The underdots are part of the spelling, not decoration:
- `ẹ` is not interchangeable with `e`;
- `ọ` is not interchangeable with `o`;
- `ṣ` is not interchangeable with `s`.

The letter sequence `gb` represents one Yorùbá consonant sound. Do not divide it into two consonants when identifying the syllable structure.

## 4. A nasal vowel is not the same as a syllabic nasal

This difference is important.

- A **nasal vowel** is a vowel pronounced with nasal resonance. In the spelling, it is represented with `n` after the vowel, for example the `in` sequence in `ìrìn`.
- A **syllabic nasal** is the consonant `m` or `n` standing alone as the syllable nucleus, as in the `m` of `Bí | m | bọ́ | là`.

The `n` written after a vowel to show nasalisation must not automatically be counted as a separate syllable. Count a syllabic nasal only when the nasal consonant itself forms the syllable.

## 5. Syllable parts: what can safely be taught

A syllable has a **nucleus** (its central sound peak). In the three Yorùbá patterns taught here:
- the nucleus is a vowel in V and CV syllables;
- the nucleus is a syllabic nasal in an N syllable.

A CV syllable also has an **onset**: the consonant before its vowel. A V syllable has no consonant onset.

Standard Yorùbá syllables are open: the ordinary patterns end in their vowel nucleus, and a syllabic nasal is itself a complete syllable. The lesson must not teach an ordinary final consonant as a syllable coda.

## 6. Worked word segmentation

| Word | Syllable division | Pattern of each syllable |
|---|---|---|
| `adé` | `a | dé` | `V | CV` |
| `èédú` | `è | é | dú` | `V | V | CV` |
| `ké` | `k + é` | `CV` |
| `fò` | `f + ò` | `CV` |
| `fẹ́` | `f + ẹ́` | `CV` |
| `gbà` | `gb + à` | `CV` |
| `sùn` | `s + un` | `CV` (`n` marks vowel nasalisation) |
| `olùgbàlà` | `o | lù | gbà | là` | `V | CV | CV | CV` |
| `Bímbọ́lá` | `Bí | m | bọ́ | là` | `CV | N | CV | CV` |
| `aláńgbá` | `a | lá | ǹ | gbá` | `V | CV | N | CV` |

When segmenting, say the word slowly, listen for each syllable peak, then write the syllables in the same order. Do not split the single consonant `gb` into two separate syllables.

## 7. Class exercise

**Question 1 — 2 marks**

What is a syllable?

**Answer:** A syllable is a unit of pronunciation organised around one sound peak or nucleus. In Yorùbá, it is a tone-bearing unit.

**Marking:** correct unit of pronunciation (1); nucleus/tone-bearing explanation (1).

**Question 2 — 3 marks**

Name the three basic syllable structures in Standard Yorùbá.

**Answer:** V, CV and N.

**Marking:** V (1); CV (1); N (1).

**Question 3 — 4 marks**

Divide `adé` and `èédú` into syllables and give the pattern of each syllable.

**Answer:**
- `adé` → `a | dé` → `V | CV`.
- `èédú` → `è | é | dú` → `V | V | CV`.

**Marking:** correct division of `adé` (1); correct patterns for `adé` (1); correct division of `èédú` (1); correct patterns for `èédú` (1).

**Question 4 — 3 marks**

Classify the following examples by syllable structure: `fò`, the `m` in `Bímbọ́lá`, and `gbà`.

**Answer:** `fò` is CV; `m` is N; `gbà` is CV.

**Marking:** one mark for each correct classification.

**Question 5 — 2 marks**

Explain the difference between a nasal vowel and a syllabic nasal.

**Answer:** A nasal vowel is a vowel pronounced with nasal resonance and written with an `n` after the vowel; a syllabic nasal is an `m` or `n` consonant that forms a syllable by itself.

**Marking:** nasal vowel explained (1); syllabic nasal explained (1).

**Question 6 — 2 marks**

In `Bí | m | bọ́ | là`, which syllable has the N structure, and why?

**Answer:** `m` has the N structure because the nasal consonant stands alone as a syllable.

**Marking:** identifies `m` (1); gives the reason (1).

**Total: 16 marks.**

2. Write one example each of V, CV and N from the lesson.
3. Explain why the `n` in a nasal-vowel spelling should not automatically be counted as a separate syllable.

**Suggested answers:**
1. `o | lù | gbà | là` → `V | CV | CV | CV`.
2. V: `a`; CV: `fò`; N: `m` in `Bímbọ́lá`.
