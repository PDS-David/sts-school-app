## NNDP — children 451–461

The source lists objectives concerning Lagos municipal self-government, coordinating nominations/elections for Lagos seats, education, nationwide branches and cooperation with the National Congress of British West Africa. It then identifies Herbert Macaulay as founder/leader and records the party's success in all three Lagos Legislative Council seats in 1923, 1928 and 1933.

Historical accounts support the 1923 founding and Macaulay's leadership, and confirm the three Lagos-seat victories. The NNDP's actual political base remained Lagos, despite its national aspirations. The claimed nationwide branch network and exact formal relationship with the National Congress of British West Africa need evidence. The first-officer names/titles given for Egerton Shyngle, T.H. Jackson and J.C. Zizer are not independently confirmed in the sources consulted; do not replace them with guessed names.

## NYM — children 463–477

The source says the Lagos Youth Movement became the Nigerian Youth Movement in 1963. This is incorrect: the Lagos Youth Movement emerged in 1933/1934 and changed its name to the Nigerian Youth Movement in **1936**.

The source lists unity, public enlightenment, education, adult suffrage and ending indirect rule among its objectives. The phrase “complete independence within the British Empire” is contradictory; verify the original charter wording and distinguish self-government within the Commonwealth from complete independence.

The source's account of the 1938 electoral success is supported: the NYM won Lagos Town Council representation and the three Lagos elective seats, displacing the NNDP. The Daily Service was associated with the movement as its newspaper. However:
- The NYM did not “give birth to nationalism”; nationalist activity predated it, including the NNDP and earlier political associations/press.
- It made a significant pan-Nigerian contribution, but the 1941 Ikoli–Akinsanya leadership crisis weakened its cross-ethnic character and contributed to decline.
- Avoid presenting the movement as uniformly national or permanently united.

## Action Group — children 479–492

The source correctly identifies the Action Group's 1951 founding, Obafemi Awolowo's leadership and Bode Thomas as secretary. Its objectives include mass literacy/education, electoral participation in the Western Region and cooperation toward self-rule.

The source says Egbe Omo Oduduwa was formed in 1945 in London by Yoruba students. Historical accounts differ on the association's London origins and its Nigerian organization; the Obafemi Awolowo Foundation dates Awolowo's formation of the cultural group to 1949, while other accounts describe earlier London activity. Preserve the distinction and do not collapse these into one unsupported date.

The source correctly links the Action Group to:
- Anthony Enahoro's motion for self-government by 1956, moved on 31 March 1953;
- the Western Region's free primary education programme introduced in 1955;
- the party's role in the independence struggle and its later position as federal opposition after the 1959 election.

Claims that it “greatly developed” the region or was “the best financial and best administered” party are evaluative and require evidence. The claim that it was the opposition in the Eastern Region needs election-specific support; the party's strongest electoral base was the West.

## NPC — children 494–503

The source gives the cultural precursor's name as “Jamiyaa Jama'ar Arewa” and dates its transformation to 1952. Historical sources identify **Jam'iyyar Mutanen Arewa** (also rendered Jama'iyyar Mutanen Arewa) as a Northern cultural association dating to 1948/1949; it became the Northern People's Congress as a political party in **1951**. The source's 1952 date and association name need correction.

The source names Ahmadu Bello as party leader and Premier of Northern Nigeria from 1954; this is broadly correct. However, the source has no NPC objectives section even though the scheme requires objectives and achievements. A corrected supplement may provide a clearly labelled, sourced objective summary, but must not pretend the objectives were present in the original.

The source says the NPC controlled the federal government and produced Nigeria's first Prime Minister. This needs the coalition context: after the 1959 federal election, the NPC was the largest party and formed a coalition government with the NCNC; Abubakar Tafawa Balewa, an NPC leader, became the first Prime Minister. The NPC dominated Northern Region government until January 1966. Northern Nigeria was represented at constitutional conferences by regional delegations; it is too broad to say the party alone represented the entire region at every conference.

4. **Yoruba Encyclopedia, “Nigerian Youth Movement.”** Confirms the 1936 renaming and 1938 electoral success, and describes the 1941 Ikoli–Akinsanya crisis: [entry](https://encyclopedia.ac/history/history-political/nigerian-youth-movement/).

Odugbemi, “Interrogating Nigeria's First Generation Political Parties.”** Discusses the NPC's cultural precursor, 1948/1949 origins and 1951 political-party formation: [article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338557800_INTERROGATING_NIGERIA%27S_FIRST_GENERATION_POLITICAL_PARTIES_TOWARDS_PROVIDING_A_POLITICAL_PARADIGM_FOR_A_NATION_AT_THE_BRINK_OF_COLLAPSE).
