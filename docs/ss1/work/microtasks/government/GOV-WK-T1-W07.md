## Fascism — lines 560–573

The source gives a broad description of fascism as aggressive nationalism, leader-centred dictatorship and opposition to liberal democracy and communism. The historical dates require separation:
- The Italian Fascist movement was founded by Mussolini in **1919**.
- Mussolini came to power as prime minister in **1922**.
- He was removed from office in **1943**.
- After the 1943 armistice, a German-backed Fascist successor government operated in northern Italy until 1945.

Thus, 1922–1943 describes Mussolini's rule over Italy before his removal, not the founding and full end of Italian Fascism. The source's description of fascism as uniformly rejecting religion is too broad: Mussolini's regime signed the Lateran Treaties with the Catholic Church in 1929. Its relationship with religion and the economy must be explained historically, not as a universal rule for every fascist movement.

## Nazism — lines 578–593

The **1933–1945** dates are accurate for Nazi rule in Germany. The source's racial-superiority statement at line 579 is the Nazi regime's false racist ideology, not a fact about any human group. The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum explains that Nazi racial claims were based on prejudice and false theories; race is not a biological hierarchy. The corrected lesson must explicitly attribute and reject the claim, not repeat it as an objective description or reproduce Nazi propaganda.

The source's list of one-party rule, leader-centred government, control of mass media, suppression of opposition and central economic direction broadly describes documented features of Nazi rule. Present them as historical facts about that regime, not as a model to emulate. No graphic detail is required.

## Totalitarianism — lines 594–606

The source says totalitarianism is also called authoritarianism. This is inaccurate. Political-science references distinguish the concepts:
- **Authoritarianism** is a broader category of non-democratic rule, often characterized by restricted political pluralism and limits on contestation.
- **Totalitarianism** is generally used for a more ideologically driven party-state seeking pervasive control across political and social life, with little room for independent institutions or genuine opposition.

The distinction is influential but debated; the supplement should describe the common scholarly usage and avoid claiming that every authoritarian regime is totalitarian. The source's phrase “absolute control” should be presented as an analytical ideal or ambition, not literal omnipotence: no government can be assumed to control every person, idea and property completely.

## External historical and political-science references consulted

1. **United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, “Fascism”** (Holocaust Encyclopedia). Describes the origins and development of fascism and confirms that Mussolini founded the Italian Fascist movement in 1919 and came to power in 1922: [entry](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/fascism-1).
2. **United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, “Benito Mussolini”** (Holocaust Encyclopedia; last edited 17 September 2018). Confirms Mussolini's rule from 1922–1943, his removal in 1943 and the later German-backed Italian Social Republic: [entry](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/benito-mussolini-1).
3. **Treccani, “Fascismo,” Dizionario di Storia** (2010). Records the movement's founding in March 1919, accession to power in October 1922 and Mussolini's removal on 25 July 1943: [entry](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/fascismo_%28Dizionario-di-Storia%29/).
4. **John F. Pollard, *The Vatican and Italian Fascism, 1929–32*** (Cambridge University Press; chapter published online 3 February 2010). Documents the Lateran Treaties signed on 11 February 1929 between the Vatican and Mussolini's government: [chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/vatican-and-italian-fascism-192932/introduction/99EEC85A107BA509BCE520C400D12C0D).
5. **United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, “Nazi Racism”** (Holocaust Encyclopedia; last edited 23 September 2025). Explains that Nazi racial superiority claims were false racist beliefs, not scientific facts: [entry](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/nazi-racism).
6. **United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, “Introduction to the Holocaust”** (Holocaust Encyclopedia). Confirms the Nazi regime's 1933–1945 period: [entry](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/introduction-to-the-holocaust?parent=en%2F28).
7. **Chamsy El-Ojeili, “Totalitarianism,” Wiley Encyclopedia of Social Theory** (first published 22 September 2017). Describes totalitarianism as an all-embracing party-state and distinguishes it from broader authoritarianism: [entry](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781405165518.wbeost032.pub2).
8. **“Authoritarian Regimes,” Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Politics** (published 2018). Summarizes Juan Linz's widely used distinction between authoritarian regimes and totalitarian regimes: [chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/41327/chapter/352327108).
