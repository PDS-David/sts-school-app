## Source content and factual checks

### Revenue allocation — child 434

The source identifies revenue allocation as contentious but provides no formula or principles. A complete supplement must distinguish:
- vertical allocation among the Federation, states and local governments;
- horizontal allocation among states/local governments;
- the Federation Account under section 162;
- RMAFC's technical role and the 13% derivation principle.

No percentages are invented here; any figures in the lesson must cite the exact formula and effective date.

### Minority questions — child 435

The source raises fear of domination by three major ethnic groups and unequal allocation of economic resources, naming Urhobo, Ijaw, Itsekiri and Tiv communities. Treat these as examples, not an exhaustive list or a single homogeneous category. Minority concerns include political representation, security, state creation, local autonomy, environmental/resource questions and constitutional safeguards. Do not attribute uniform views to any ethnic community.

### Creation of states — child 436

The source correctly identifies 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory. The FCT is not a state. Section 8 of the 1999 Constitution sets a specific process for creating a new state; public demand alone does not create one.

### Inter-communal rivalry — child 437

The source says “Aguleri/Ugueli wars that lasted 100 years” and groups “Fulani/Tivs and Ife/Modakeke riots in the 80s.” Corrections:
- The well-documented communities are **Aguleri and Umuleri**. Major episodes occurred in 1995 and 1999; a single continuous 100-year war is not established.
- The source's “Fulani/Tiv” label may conflate the **Tiv–Jukun** conflicts documented from the 1980s. Verify the intended event before substituting a name.
- Ife–Modakeke disputes had episodes in the 1980s and 1990s, including major tensions in 1997–2000. The history includes land, chieftaincy and local-government boundary issues; do not reduce it to one “riot.”
- These conflicts are recorded only at a high historical level here; no graphic detail is needed.

### Boundary disputes and Bakassi — child 438

The Osun/Oyo example gives no named communities, boundary segment or authoritative ruling; it remains unverified until a specific location and record are identified.

The Bakassi account needs precise chronology:
- **10 October 2002:** the International Court of Justice delivered its judgment in the Cameroon–Nigeria Land and Maritime Boundary case and determined sovereignty over the peninsula.
- **12 June 2006:** Nigeria and Cameroon signed the Greentree Agreement, setting the modalities for withdrawal and transfer of authority.
- **14 August 2008:** the transfer-of-authority ceremony was completed under the agreement; Nigeria's President was **Umaru Musa Yar'Adua**.
- The 2008 event was a transfer of authority pursuant to an ICJ judgment and international agreement, not a unilateral “secession.”

### Power sharing and resource control — children 439–440

Both are headings only. The source does not explain:
- whether “power sharing” means federal/state division, executive inclusion/rotation, legislative representation or fiscal distribution;
- how “resource control” differs from ownership, environmental regulation, derivation and Federation Account allocation.

These topics must be defined and separated in the supplement; no intended meaning is inferred from the headings.
