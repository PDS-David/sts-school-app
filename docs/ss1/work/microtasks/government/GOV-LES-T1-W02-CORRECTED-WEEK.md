# Week 2 corrected supplement: Features, functions and value of studying Government

**Class:** SS 1  
**Subject:** Government  
**Term/week:** First Term, Week 2  
**Use:** This original supplement replaces the Week Two teaching row for delivery. It does not reproduce the assigned textbook pages.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the lesson, a student should be able to:
1. identify and explain important features of government, distinguishing law generally from the Constitution;
2. explain the main functions of government and distinguish constitutional powers of the organs from the wider policy objectives of the state; and
3. explain why Government is studied and distinguish the purpose of studying Government from the purpose of government itself.

## 1. Features or characteristics of government

Government is the set of institutions and public officials through which a state exercises public authority and manages common affairs. The following are six important features or characteristics.

1. **Power and authority:** Government needs the lawful capacity to make decisions and carry them out. Power is the ability to influence or direct conduct; authority is power recognised as legitimate under the law. Public power should be exercised within constitutional limits.
2. **Law:** Government operates through rules that regulate conduct and provide standards for public decisions. Law is not another name for the Constitution. The Constitution is the supreme legal framework of the state; statutes made by the legislature and other valid rules also form part of the legal system. Section 1 of Nigeria's Constitution establishes its supremacy, while section 4 vests legislative powers in the National Assembly and State Houses of Assembly.
3. **Revenue:** Government needs public funds to perform its responsibilities. Revenue may come from taxes, duties, fees and other lawful sources. Public money should be raised, authorised, managed and accounted for under the law.
4. **Personnel:** Government works through people and public institutions. These include elected office-holders, civil and public servants, judges, police officers, teachers, health workers and other officials. Their responsibilities differ according to their offices and the law.
5. **Public support and legitimacy:** Public confidence, consent, cooperation and participation help institutions govern effectively. In a constitutional democracy, public support should be built through lawful conduct, accountability and respect for rights; popularity does not permit a government to act outside the law.
6. **Welfare services:** Government sets policy, provides or coordinates public services and creates conditions for people's well-being. Examples include education, health, roads, water and electricity. The way services are delivered may involve public agencies and other lawful providers.

### Law is not the same as the Constitution

**Law** is the wider system of valid rules. The **Constitution** is the supreme framework that establishes institutions, allocates public powers and protects rights. It is itself law, but it is not the name for every rule that regulates people's conduct. A statute passed under the Constitution is also law.

## 2. Functions of government

Government performs several connected functions. The following ten categories organise the topics in the source lesson. They are a teaching classification, not one exhaustive list contained in a single section of the Constitution.

1. **Making laws:** The legislature makes laws for the level of government within its constitutional authority. Section 4 of the Constitution allocates legislative powers.
2. **Maintaining public order and security:** Government establishes lawful institutions and processes to protect the public, prevent disorder and enforce valid laws. Section 11 addresses public order and security. Law enforcement must respect constitutional rights and due process.
3. **Defence:** The state protects its territory and sovereignty. Section 217 provides for the Armed Forces of the Federation. This is a public responsibility; it does not mean that every public institution performs military functions.
4. **Protecting lives, property and rights:** Public institutions protect people and property and provide lawful ways to respond to threats and disputes. Protection is not limited to citizens alone; everyone within the jurisdiction is entitled to the protections the law provides.
5. **Administering justice:** Courts hear cases, interpret and apply the law, and resolve disputes under section 6. The Judiciary determines legal responsibility and imposes lawful sentences. The correctional system has a separate role after a court order or sentence.
6. **Providing and coordinating social services:** Government develops and supports services such as health care, education, roads, water and other public infrastructure. Sections 17 and 18 set out social and educational objectives.
7. **Promoting opportunities for suitable employment:** Section 17(3)(a) speaks of citizens having opportunities to secure adequate livelihoods and suitable employment. This is a constitutional policy objective; it is not a promise that government will directly employ every citizen.
8. **Managing the economy:** Government makes and implements economic policy, manages public resources, supports economic activity and pursues the objectives set out in section 16.
9. **Supporting constitutional political processes:** Government must operate within democratic and constitutional arrangements, including lawful elections and peaceful changes of office. Independent electoral bodies administer elections under the applicable law; election administration is not simply the private task of the incumbent executive.
10. **Conducting external relations:** Government develops relations with other states and international organisations. Section 19 sets out the foreign-policy objectives of Nigeria.

### Constitutional powers and policy objectives are different

The Constitution separates two ideas that should not be confused:

- **Powers of the organs:** sections 4, 5 and 6 allocate legislative, executive and judicial powers. These provisions explain which institutions make laws, implement them and adjudicate disputes.
- **Objectives and responsibilities of the state:** sections 14–19 set out political, economic, social, educational and foreign-policy objectives. Section 14(2)(b) states that the security and welfare of the people are the primary purpose of government. Section 11 addresses public order and security; section 217 provides for the Armed Forces.

The source describes maintaining law and order as the “primary and most important” function. That is a textbook ranking, not a constitutional ranking of every function. The Constitution's statement of the primary purpose is broader: the security and welfare of the people.

### Employment and correctional services: two necessary qualifications

- **Employment:** Government can promote conditions and opportunities for suitable employment, but section 17(3)(a) does not promise a government job to every citizen.
- **Custody and rehabilitation:** The Nigerian Correctional Service Act 2019 establishes a service with custodial and non-custodial functions. Its objectives include safe custody, reformation, rehabilitation and reintegration. The Judiciary decides cases and imposes lawful sentences; correctional services administer the custodial or non-custodial measures authorised by law. It is therefore incomplete to describe correctional institutions as existing only to punish offenders.

## 3. Why do we study Government?

Studying Government gives learners political education and helps them understand how public institutions work. The following are eight reasons, expressed as educational benefits rather than promises that study alone will solve political problems.

1. **Political education:** It introduces the institutions and processes through which public decisions are made and helps learners understand how they may participate lawfully.
2. **Rights and responsibilities:** It helps learners understand their rights, duties and obligations and the lawful ways to protect them.
3. **Understanding political systems:** It helps learners understand the system adopted in their country and consider how that system affects public decision-making.
4. **Patriotism and civic responsibility:** It can develop informed concern for the country and willingness to contribute constructively. Patriotism should not mean blind loyalty or silence about wrongdoing.
5. **International organisations:** It helps learners understand why states join international organisations and how states cooperate with one another.
6. **Political awareness and accountability:** It can help people recognise abuses of power and understand why constitutional limits and accountability matter. Studying Government alone cannot guarantee that tyranny will be prevented.
7. **Evaluating leaders and parties:** It gives learners concepts for assessing leaders, parties and policies using evidence rather than rumours or slogans.
8. **Democracy and the rule of law:** It helps learners understand representative institutions, lawful limits on public power and peaceful political participation.

## 4. The purpose of studying Government is not the purpose of government

The source's statement that the ultimate aim of government is to make people political scientists confuses two different questions.

- **Purpose of studying Government:** to develop political understanding, civic knowledge and the ability to assess institutions, leaders and public policies. Studying the subject may introduce a learner to Political Science, but it does not mean that every learner must become a professional political scientist.
- **Purpose of government:** the purpose of public authority and institutions. In Nigeria, section 14(2)(b) of the Constitution states that the security and welfare of the people are the primary purpose of government. Sections 15–19 add wider political, economic, social, educational and foreign-policy objectives.

Political philosophers have offered different views about the proper purpose and limits of government. **Hobbes** emphasised common authority and security; **Locke** emphasised consent and protection of natural rights; **Rousseau** connected legitimate authority with the general will and common good; **Aristotle** understood political community in relation to human social nature. These are different philosophical arguments, not one universally accepted answer. They should be compared fairly rather than treated as identical or as a substitute for Nigeria's constitutional text.

## 5. Quick recap

- The six features in this lesson are power/authority, law, revenue, personnel, public support/legitimacy and welfare services.
- Law is broader than the Constitution; the Constitution is the supreme legal framework.
- Government performs legislative, security, defence, justice, social, economic, political and external-relations functions.
- Sections 4–6 allocate powers to the Legislature, Executive and Judiciary; sections 14–19 set wider objectives of the state.
- Section 14(2)(b) identifies the security and welfare of the people as government's primary purpose.
- The Nigerian Correctional Service provides custodial and non-custodial services, including rehabilitation and reintegration; it is not solely a punishment institution.
- Studying Government develops political understanding; government itself exists to serve public purposes, not to produce political scientists.
