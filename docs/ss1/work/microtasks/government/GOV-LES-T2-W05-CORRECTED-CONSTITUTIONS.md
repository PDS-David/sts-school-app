## Teaching completed
The replacement explains the meaning of a constitution and teaches all three instruments:
- **Clifford 1922:** 46-member Legislative Council; Governor as President, 26 official members, 15 nominated unofficial members and four elected seats (three Lagos, one Calabar); male £100 income/residence franchise; Legislative Council jurisdiction over Lagos/Southern Provinces, with separate northern arrangements. It distinguishes the pre-existing Executive Council from the new Legislative Council.
- **Richards 1946/1947:** operational 1 January 1947; stated aims of unity, accommodation of regional diversity and greater African participation; central Legislative Council for Nigeria; regional consultative/advisory bodies; £50 income threshold and direct elections still limited to Lagos and Calabar.
- **Macpherson 1951:** consultation including the 1950 Ibadan conference; 12-member central Council of Ministers, four from each region; Northern/Western bicameral and Eastern unicameral arrangements; differentiated regional franchise; increased participation but not full regional sovereignty.
- **1953–1954:** Anthony Enahoro's self-government motion was moved on 31 March 1953; the ensuing crisis led to further constitutional negotiations and the Lyttleton Constitution of 1954.
