## Meaning and factors — children 180–187

The source attributes a definition to K.B.C. Onwubiko that links nationalism to shared language and historical experience and a desire for self-government. The wording is corrupted (“has not failed to define”) and the exact edition/page is not provided. The definition is also too narrow: nationalist movements can unite people across different languages and historical experiences. A corrected lesson should define nationalism around collective political identity, self-determination and opposition to foreign rule, while treating shared language/history as possible, not required, factors.

The source's factors include education, economic exploitation, rising living costs, the press and political parties. These are plausible contributors, but the explanation needs chronology and context:
- The First World War (1914–1918), postwar economic instability and the Great Depression (beginning in 1929) are distinct periods. The Great Depression was not simply “caused by the First World War”; postwar instability was one contributing condition among several.
- The source's list of nationalist figures includes Jimi Solanke. Reliable biographies identify him as an actor, poet, storyteller and cultural artist; his placement as a nationalist political leader is unsupported. Do not guess which name the source intended.
- W.E.B. Du Bois should be identified as a Pan-Africanist intellectual and organizer, not a Nigerian nationalist leader.
- The press was important to nationalist debate, but newspaper names and dates should be verified and linked to specific editors/organizations.
- The party list should distinguish territories and dates: NNDP (Nigeria, 1923), NCNC (Nigeria/Cameroons, 1944), CPP (Gold Coast/Ghana, 1949) and Action Group (Nigeria, 1951).

## Effects — children 189–194

The source identifies political-party formation, political education, labour mobilization and independence as effects of nationalist activity. These are supportable themes. The statement that nationalism caused “rapid economic growth” is an unsupported causal generalization, and the phrase “sold West Africa to the outside world by being popular” is unclear. Do not invent an interpretation for that phrase; seek clarification or preserve it as source wording.

The source mentions Pa Michael Imoudu and a nationwide strike. Imoudu was a prominent labour leader and mobilizer, but the 1945 general strike was organized collectively through trade unions and a joint labour leadership. A corrected account should describe his role without crediting him as the sole organizer.

## Earlier resistance figures — child 181

The source names Jaja of Opobo, Nana of Itsekiri, the Awujale of Ijebuland and Ovonramwen Nogbaisi. The names and events need precision:
- “Nana of Itsekiri” refers to Nana Olomu, who was captured and exiled to the Gold Coast in 1894.
- “Awujale” is the royal title of the ruler of Ijebuland, not a personal name. The source does not identify the officeholder or specific event.
- Jaja of Opobo and Ovonramwen's exile chronology was already checked in GOV-WK-T2-W02; cross-reference those open items rather than duplicating the work.
- Ovonramwen's name should be written accurately; preserve the source's spelling in the source locator and correct only in a separate supplement.

## Nigerian nationalist leaders — children 196–203

- **Herbert Macaulay (1864–1946):** The NNDP was founded in 1923. Macaulay and Dr John Caulcrick bought the Lagos Daily News in 1927; the paper had been founded in 1925. The source's claim that Macaulay “co-founded” the paper and its description of a law placing all Southern Nigerian land under British control need correction/identification. Historical accounts describe Macaulay's protests over land issues and a specific Lagos land case.
- **Nnamdi Azikiwe (1904–1996):** Born 16 November 1904 in Zungeru. He founded the West African Pilot in 1937; the source's “1937–1940” period is unsupported. The NCNC was founded in 1944; Azikiwe became its Secretary-General in 1946. He was Premier of the Eastern Region (1954–1959), Governor-General (1960–1963) and President (1963–1966).
- **Ahmadu Bello (1910–1966):** The source's birth year 1909 is incorrect; Oxford's Dictionary of National Biography gives 1910. Distinguish the NPC's earlier cultural-association history from its emergence as a political party in 1951. Any specific schools attributed to Bello need evidence.
- **Obafemi Awolowo (1909–1987):** Born 6 March 1909. He was Premier of the Western Region (1954–1959), and his government introduced free primary education in 1955. The source's claim that he co-founded the TUC in 1943 is not established; sources show his involvement in labour organizations and the Nigerian Worker, but the formal founding leadership should be identified accurately.

11. **DAWN Commission, “Sir Jimi Solanke”; Channels Television, “Veteran Actor Jimi Solanke Is Dead.”** Identify Solanke as an actor, performer, poet and storyteller, not as a nationalist party leader: [DAWN](https://dawncommission.org/sir-jimi-solanke/); [Channels](https://www.channelstv.com/2024/02/05/veteran-cultural-actor-jimi-solanke-is-dead/).
