## Federalism features — children 420–427

The source lists written/rigid constitutions, bicameral legislatures, Supreme Court interpretation, no secession, equal representation in the Senate and a quota/federal-character principle. Corrections and qualifications:
- **Constitutional timeline:** the source stops at the 1989 Constitution and omits the 1999 Constitution currently in force (as amended). The 1989 Constitution was promulgated for the Third Republic but not fully implemented before the transition collapsed in 1993. The timeline should also distinguish the 1960, 1963 and 1979 constitutions.
- **Bicameralism:** the federal National Assembly is bicameral. State Houses of Assembly are unicameral under the 1999 Constitution; historical regional legislatures also differed.
- **Judicial interpretation:** the Supreme Court is the final appellate authority and its interpretations bind lower courts, but other courts also interpret and apply the Constitution.
- **Secession:** section 2(1) of the 1999 Constitution describes Nigeria as one indivisible and indissoluble sovereign state. The Constitution provides no unilateral secession procedure; do not imply that the section itself creates a specific criminal offence.
- **Senate representation:** under the 1999 Constitution, each of the 36 states has three senators and the FCT has one. The FCT is not a state; do not generalize this exact composition to all earlier constitutional periods.
- **Federal character and quota:** section 14(3)–(4) establishes the federal character principle for public institutions. Specific quota policies are related but distinct; do not use the terms as synonyms.
