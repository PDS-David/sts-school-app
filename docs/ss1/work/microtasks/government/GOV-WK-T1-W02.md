### Features of government — lines 112–120

The source lists six features and places an evaluation prompt at line 121. The law subsection describes the body of rules regulating conduct as “the constitution”. That is not a sound general definition: the Constitution is the supreme legal framework, while valid statutes and other laws are also part of the legal system. The Federal Ministry of Justice's Constitution publication states constitutional supremacy in section 1 and separately vests legislative powers in the National Assembly and State Houses of Assembly in section 4. The distinction is recorded for delivery correction; the original source is not altered.

### Functions of government — lines 124–135

The source's ten categories broadly align with several constitutional domains:
- Sections 4–6 allocate legislative, executive and judicial powers.
- Section 11 addresses public order and public security.
- Section 14(2)(b) states the security and welfare of the people as the primary purpose of government.
- Sections 15–19 address political, economic, social, educational and foreign-policy objectives.
- Section 217 provides for the Armed Forces.

The list must not be presented as one exhaustive constitutional list or as functions performed by a single undifferentiated government. Section 17(3)(a) describes policy to provide citizens opportunities to secure a livelihood and suitable employment; it does not guarantee a government job to each citizen. The source's “primary and most important” ranking is a textbook judgement, not a universal constitutional rule. The source's prison/punishment account needs a separate correctional-law check before delivery.

### Reasons for studying Government — lines 136–145

The source lists political education, understanding rights and duties, understanding systems of government, patriotism/nationalism, international organizations, preventing tyranny, evaluating political leadership and supporting democracy/rule of law. Line 144 states, as printed, “The ultimate aim of government is to make us political scientists”. This is retained as source evidence, not accepted as fact.

The claim confuses the purpose of the school subject with the purpose of government. Section 14(2)(b) of the Nigerian Constitution states that the security and welfare of the people are the primary purpose of government. OpenStax's *Introduction to Political Science* describes political scientists as systematically studying political phenomena (§1.3), distinguishes empirical from normative political science (§§1.4–1.5), and notes that there is no unanimity about the proper purpose of government (§2.1). The supplement should state that studying Government can develop political understanding and the ability to assess institutions, leaders and policy; it must not say that government exists to produce political scientists.
