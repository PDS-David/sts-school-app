# Week 7 corrected supplement: Fascism, Nazism and totalitarianism

**Class:** SS 1  
**Subject:** Government  
**Term/week:** First Term, Week 7  
**Use:** This original supplement replaces the Week Seven teaching row for delivery. It does not reproduce the assigned textbooks or Nazi propaganda.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the lesson, a student should be able to:
1. explain fascism and distinguish the founding of the Italian Fascist movement from the dates of Mussolini's rule;
2. describe Nazism as a racist and antisemitic ideology and historical regime, explicitly identifying its racial claims as false;
3. distinguish totalitarianism from the broader concept of authoritarianism; and
4. compare democracy and fascism using political institutions, participation, rights and accountability.

## 1. Fascism

**Fascism** is a family of far-right, authoritarian and ultranationalist political movements associated with leader-centred rule, hostility to political pluralism and liberal democracy, and the subordination of individuals and independent organisations to the claimed interests of the nation or state. Historical fascist movements differed; the term should be applied with attention to time and place.

### Italian Fascism: movement and regime dates

The dates must be separated:

- **1919:** Benito Mussolini founded the Italian Fascist movement.
- **1922:** Mussolini became Prime Minister of Italy.
- **1943:** Mussolini was removed from office in July 1943.
- **1943–1945:** after the Italian armistice, a German-backed Fascist successor government operated in northern Italy until 1945.

Thus, 1922–1943 describes Mussolini's rule as head of government before his removal. It is not the full life span of the movement or every later Fascist institution.

### Features associated with fascist regimes

Commonly identified features include:

1. **Aggressive ultranationalism:** the nation is presented as supreme and political disagreement may be portrayed as disloyalty.
2. **Leader-centred rule:** political authority is concentrated around a leader and a ruling movement.
3. **Anti-pluralism:** independent opposition parties, unions, media and civic organisations are restricted or suppressed.
4. **Hostility to liberal democracy and communism:** fascist movements opposed political pluralism and many forms of socialist and communist organisation.
5. **Propaganda and political control:** the regime seeks to shape public information and political participation.
6. **State direction of society and the economy:** the state may organise relations among employers, workers and professional bodies while private property and private businesses continue to exist.

These are broad historical characteristics, not a claim that every fascist movement had identical institutions or policies.

### Religion and the economy: avoid overgeneralisation

Italian Fascism did not uniformly reject religion. Mussolini's government signed the **Lateran Treaties in 1929** with the Catholic Church. This agreement is evidence of negotiation and accommodation, even though relations between the Fascist state and religious institutions could also involve conflict and political pressure.

Fascism's relationship with capitalism also needs careful description. The Italian regime retained private property and private firms while expanding state direction and corporatist institutions. It is inaccurate to say that every fascist movement simply abolished capitalism or that all fascist governments followed one identical economic policy.

## 2. Nazism in Germany, 1933–1945

**Nazism** was the ideology and ruling movement of the National Socialist German Workers' Party under Adolf Hitler. The Nazi regime governed Germany from **1933 to 1945**.

Nazi ideology combined extreme ultranationalism, dictatorship, antisemitism, expansionism and a false racist hierarchy. The regime's claims that humanity could be divided into superior and inferior races were **false racist ideology, not scientific fact**. There is no biological hierarchy that ranks human groups. The regime used these false claims to justify discrimination, persecution and mass atrocities against Jews and other targeted groups. This lesson identifies those crimes and beliefs in historical terms without reproducing propaganda or graphic details.

### Features of Nazi rule

Historical features included:

- one-party dictatorship and leader-centred authority;
- the suppression of political opposition and independent organisations;
- control and manipulation of mass media and public information;
- state promotion of racist and antisemitic ideology;
- aggressive ultranationalism and expansionist policy; and
- the use of state institutions to persecute targeted groups.

These are descriptions of a historical dictatorship, not a model to imitate. Nazi racial claims are false and harmful and must never be presented as facts about any people.

## 3. Totalitarianism

**Totalitarianism** is a political-science concept commonly used for a regime that seeks pervasive control over political and social life through an official ideology, a dominant party or leader, control of public information, suppression of independent organisation and extensive state power.

The term describes an ambition and an analytical pattern. “Absolute control” should not be interpreted as literal omnipotence: no government can be assumed to control every thought, relationship or action of every person.

### Totalitarianism and authoritarianism are not identical

- **Authoritarianism** is a broader category of non-democratic rule. It commonly involves restricted political pluralism and limits on meaningful competition for power.
- **Totalitarianism** is generally used for a more ideologically driven system that seeks much more pervasive control across political and social life and attempts to mobilise society around an official doctrine.

The distinction is influential but debated among scholars. Not every authoritarian regime is totalitarian, and the labels should be applied carefully to particular historical cases.

## 4. Democracy and fascism: comparison

The following table is a teaching comparison of general political characteristics. It is not a claim that every historical regime was identical in every detail.

| Feature | Constitutional democracy | Fascist dictatorship |
|---|---|---|
| Source of political authority | Authority derives from the people under constitutional rules | Leader and ruling movement claim to embody the nation |
| Political competition | Genuine competition among parties and candidates | Opposition and pluralist competition are restricted or suppressed |
| Public participation | Citizens participate through elections and lawful civic activity | Participation is directed by the ruling movement and controlled by the state |
| Rights and dissent | Rights, opposition and independent civic organisations receive legal protection | Dissent and independent organisations are commonly restricted |
| Accountability | Leaders are subject to law, scrutiny and the possibility of losing office | Power is concentrated and independent accountability is weakened |
| Public information | Independent media and open debate are protected in principle | Information and media are controlled or manipulated by the regime |

Democracy is not simply majority rule: constitutional rights and minority protections matter. Fascism rejects political pluralism and concentrates power; that is a fundamental contrast with constitutional democracy.

## 5. Quick recap

- The Italian Fascist movement was founded in 1919; Mussolini became Prime Minister in 1922 and was removed in 1943; a German-backed successor regime continued in northern Italy until 1945.
- Italian Fascism's relationship with religion and private business was historically complex; the Lateran Treaties were signed in 1929.
- Nazi rule in Germany lasted from 1933 to 1945. Nazi racial-superiority claims were false racist ideology, not science.
- Totalitarianism is generally used for an ideological regime seeking pervasive control; authoritarianism is a broader category.
- “Absolute control” is an analytical description of an ambition, not literal omnipotence.
- Constitutional democracy protects political competition, rights, accountability and minority protections; fascist dictatorship suppresses pluralism and concentrates power.
