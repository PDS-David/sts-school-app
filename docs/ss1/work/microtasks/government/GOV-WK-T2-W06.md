## Lyttleton Constitution — children 290–311

The source identifies federal division of powers, regionalization of the judiciary and public service, regional governors and premiers. The 1954 Constitution Order in Council came into force on 1 October 1954 and formally established the Federation of Nigeria. Several details require precision:
- The federal House of Representatives Speaker was appointed by the Governor-General from outside its membership under section 7. Regional House procedures differed; some Speakers were appointed and others elected. The source's general statement about all Houses is too broad.
- The Constitution permitted different electoral methods in different parts of Nigeria. Direct elections were not uniform throughout the country; Northern representation continued to involve indirect electoral arrangements through Native Authorities/electoral colleges.
- The Governor-General chaired/presided over the Federal Executive Council; he was not the Council itself. The constitution did not yet create a Nigerian Prime Minister at the centre.
- Derivation was important in the fiscal arrangements, particularly for mineral rents/royalties and personal income tax, but the broader revenue-sharing history also involved need, population, national interest and other criteria. The source's “derivation instead of need and national interest” statement is too absolute.
- Regional structures strengthened regional political identities, but “regionalism, ethnicity and national disunity” is an interpretation requiring context, not a single automatic consequence.

## Resumed 1958 Constitutional Conference — children 313–316

The source correctly gives the London venue and dates: 29 September to 27 October 1958. The official report confirms that the resumed conference met at Lancaster House, London, and considered outstanding matters from the 1957 conference, including fundamental rights, minority concerns, fiscal arrangements, regional self-government and independence.

The source says creation of additional regions was considered to allay minority fears. Distinguish the proposal from the outcome: the Willink Commission recommended safeguards for minorities but did not recommend creating new regions before independence. The resumed conference agreed on the arrangements leading to Nigerian independence on 1 October 1960.

The scheme's “Lagos conference of 1958” is inconsistent with the official report and source body. A separate constitutional conference took place in Lagos in 1954; the resumed conference of 1958 took place in London. Do not silently rewrite the scheme.

## External references consulted

1. **The Nigeria (Constitution) Order in Council 1954, SI 1954/1146**, primary legal instrument. Establishes the Federation, House of Representatives, Speaker appointment and electoral regulations; the Order permits different direct/indirect methods in different parts of Nigeria: [official legislation PDF](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1954/1146/pdfs/uksi_19541146_en.pdf).
2. **Report by the Resumed Nigeria Constitutional Conference, London, September–October 1958**, primary conference report. Confirms Lancaster House, London, 29 September–27 October 1958 and the conference's agenda: [official report](https://ir.nilds.gov.ng/bitstream/handle/123456789/807/979.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1).
3. **UK Parliament, Hansard, “Constitutional Conference,” 4 November 1958.** Records the outcome, including Northern regional self-government and the agreement on independence: [Hansard](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1958-11-04/debates/607dc1b7-6210-456d-936a-c5d852ff72c7/ConstitutionalConference).
4. **Central Bank of Nigeria, Economic and Financial Review, “Evolution of Tax Assignment and Revenue-Sharing Formula in Nigeria.”** Explains the Chick Commission, derivation, needs/population and later revenue-sharing changes: [CBN report](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/OUT/PUBLICATIONS/EFR/RD/2007/CBN%20ECONOMIC%20AND%20FINANCIAL%20REVIEW%20OF%20MARCH%202006.PDF).
5. **IMF, “Political Economy of Oil-Revenue Sharing in a Developing Country: Illustrations from Nigeria.”** Summarizes the 1954 Chick Commission and the evolving use of derivation and other criteria: [IMF paper](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2003/016/article-A001-en.xml).
6. **British Documents on the End of Empire, Nigeria Part I.** Records the Willink Commission's minority safeguards, its recommendation against creating new regions before independence, and the 1 October 1960 independence date: [document collection](https://sas-space.sas.ac.uk/5992/1/vB7_Nigeria_Part_I.pdf).
7. **Society of Clerks-at-the-Table, “The Nigerian Federal Constitution, 1954.”** Summarizes the federal and regional legislative structures and differing Speaker arrangements: [historical parliamentary reference](https://www.societyofclerks.org/Documents/Tables/The%20Table%201954%20%28vol%2023%29.pdf).
