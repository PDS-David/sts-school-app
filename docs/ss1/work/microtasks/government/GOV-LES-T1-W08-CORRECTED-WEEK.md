## Corrections and factual treatment

- Statehood: distinguishes the Montevideo Convention's four Article 1 qualifications from additional textbook characteristics. Article 3's recognition clause is explained without claiming that recognition is irrelevant to practical diplomacy.
- Permanence: distinguishes a state's continuity through government changes from dissolution, merger/unification and separation; conquest is not presented as the only way a state can cease to exist.
- Nation: distinguishes nation, state and nation-state; common descent is not made compulsory.
- Political culture: teaches cognitive, affective and evaluative orientations and treats ethnicity, history, socio-economic conditions and instability as contextual influences rather than fixed group traits.
- Nigerian federalism: explains the 1953 London and 1954 Lagos conferences and the Lyttleton Constitution of 1954, including the roles of colonial administration and Nigerian political actors. It does not say Nigeria simply copied Britain's own federal system.
