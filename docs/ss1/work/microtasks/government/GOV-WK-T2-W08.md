## 1989 Constitution — children 376–391

The source identifies the presidential system, two-party structure, recall of legislators, FCT status, independent judiciary and fundamental rights. The two-party framework was a defining feature of the 1989 Constitution and the Babangida transition programme. However, the constitution was promulgated for the Third Republic, while the transition was repeatedly delayed and collapsed following the annulment of the 12 June 1993 presidential election. It was not fully implemented as a civilian national constitution.

The merits/demerits section needs qualification:
- Candidate age and education requirements were intended as eligibility thresholds; they did not prove that candidates were mature, competent or knowledgeable. The source's phrase about eliminating “immature and ignorant” people is an opinion, not an established result.
- Constitutional recognition and prominence of local government do not guarantee rapid rural development; outcomes depend on resources, accountability and implementation.
- The two-party system could simplify party choice but also restricted political choice; present both as arguments, not objective outcomes.

## 1999 Constitution — children 393–412

The source says the Constitution retained both the 1979 and 1989 constitutions. This is inaccurate. Decree No. 24 of 1999 states that the 1979 Constitution, after necessary amendments, was promulgated as the new Constitution. The 1989 Constitution was a separate Third Republic instrument and was not fully implemented. Decree No. 24 was made on 5 May 1999 and the Constitution came into force on 29 May 1999.

Further corrections:
- The Constitution provides for a bicameral National Assembly and unicameral State Houses of Assembly.
- The educational qualification is “school certificate or its equivalent”; different offices have different age thresholds, and some thresholds have since been amended.
- The constitutional schedule specifies 768 Local Government Areas and six FCT Area Councils, commonly counted together as 774 local council areas. The FCT Area Councils are not state LGAs.
- The Constitution provides for federal and state courts, including the Supreme Court, Court of Appeal, Federal High Court and State High Courts. Calling the judiciary simply “centralized” is too broad without identifying the particular centralizing feature.
- The claim that the Constitution was “made to suit Abacha's purpose” is not established as a fact. The 1995 draft arose during Abacha's regime, but Abacha died in 1998; the 1999 Constitution was promulgated by General Abdulsalami Abubakar in May 1999. The promulgation decree says the 1979 Constitution, with amendments, was used as the basis.
- The criticism that the Constitution gives excessive power to the Federal Government is an argument. It should be supported with named provisions, such as the Exclusive Legislative List and fiscal arrangements, while acknowledging the constitutional division of powers.
- The amendment process is demanding but not impossible; the Constitution specifies thresholds in section 9. “Too rigid” should be presented as a critique, not a factual description.
- The Fourth Republic began on 29 May 1999; the Constitution restored the presidential system previously used under the 1979 Constitution rather than introducing presidential government for the first time.
