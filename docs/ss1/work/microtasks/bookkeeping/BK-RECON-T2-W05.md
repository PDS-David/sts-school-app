# SS1 Bookkeeping — Reconstructed Second Term, Week 5

**Project-authored reconstruction — Bookkeeping; not recovered source material**

## Topic
Ledger classification and account relationships

## Learning outcomes
Learners should be able to explain the purpose of a ledger; classify personal, real and nominal accounts; distinguish debtors from creditors; and explain the relationship between books of original entry and ledger accounts.

## Prerequisite
Learners should understand the double-entry foundation introduced in the first term and the subsidiary books studied in Weeks 1–4.

## 1. Meaning and purpose of the ledger
A ledger is the principal book in which transactions are grouped under separate accounts. Books of original entry record transactions first, generally in date order and by transaction type. The ledger shows the effect on each individual account and helps the business determine account balances.

Examples of ledger accounts include Cash, Bank, Sales, Purchases, Furniture, Capital, Amina Stores and Bello Traders.

## 2. Main classes of accounts
For this introductory classification, accounts are grouped as follows:

| Class | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Personal accounts | Accounts of persons, firms or organisations with whom the business deals | Customers, suppliers, banks, loan providers |
| Real accounts | Accounts of assets and property | Cash, bank, land, buildings, furniture, equipment |
| Nominal accounts | Accounts of expenses, losses, incomes and gains | Rent, wages, electricity, sales, commission received |

A bank account is treated as a personal account under the traditional classification because it represents the relationship with a banking institution, although it also represents the business's bank balance in practice. Follow the classification convention stated in a question or syllabus.

## 3. Debtors and creditors
A **debtor** is a customer or other party who owes money to the business, commonly because the business sold goods or services on credit.

A **creditor** is a supplier or other party to whom the business owes money, commonly because the business bought goods or services on credit.

Example:
- The business sells goods on credit to Tola Stores for 12,000. Tola Stores is a debtor.
- The business buys goods on credit from Uche Traders for 8,500. Uche Traders is a creditor.

A debtor is not the same as a creditor. Identify the direction of the debt from the business's point of view.

## 4. How subsidiary books connect to ledger accounts
When a business sells goods on credit:
- the customer's personal account is debited with the amount owed;
- the Sales Account is credited with the sales day book total.

When a business buys goods on credit:
- the supplier's personal account is credited with the amount owed;
- the Purchases Account is debited with the purchases day book total.

For returns:
- returns inwards reduce the customer's debt;
- returns outwards reduce the amount owed to the supplier.

This lesson explains the relationship; detailed posting is practised in Week 6.

## 7. Independent assessment
**Q1 (4 marks).** Define a ledger and state one purpose.
**Answer:** A ledger is the principal book in which transactions are grouped under separate accounts. It helps show the effect of transactions on each account and determine balances.
**Marking:** Definition, 3 marks; purpose, 1 mark.

**Q2 (6 marks).** Classify: (a) cash, (b) rent expense, (c) a credit customer, (d) sales, (e) office furniture, (f) a credit supplier.
**Answer:** (a) Real; (b) Nominal; (c) Personal; (d) Nominal; (e) Real; (f) Personal.
**Marking:** 1 mark each.

**Q3 (4 marks).** Explain the difference between a debtor and a creditor.
**Answer:** A debtor owes money to the business; a creditor is owed money by the business.
**Marking:** 2 marks for each correct relationship.

**Q4 (4 marks).** State the ledger accounts affected by a credit sale of goods for 15,000 and identify which party is the debtor.
**Answer:** Debit the customer's personal account 15,000; credit Sales Account 15,000. The customer is the debtor.
**Marking:** Customer debit, 1; Sales credit, 1; correct amount, 1; identifies customer as debtor, 1.

**Total: 18 marks.**

## Completion check
Learners can classify common accounts, identify debtors and creditors from the business's point of view and explain why transactions move from books of original entry to the ledger.
