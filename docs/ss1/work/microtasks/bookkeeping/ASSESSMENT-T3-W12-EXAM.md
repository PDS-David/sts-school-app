# SS1 Bookkeeping — Third Term Examination

**Class:** SS1 | **Term:** Third Term | **Scheme slot:** Week 12 examination  
**Duration:** 1 hour 30 minutes | **Total:** 60 marks  
**Instructions:** Answer all questions. Show calculations and workings. Use ₦ for monetary amounts.  
**Provenance:** Project-authored examination paper and marking guide; not recovered from the original source DOCX.

## Section A — Objective test (10 marks)

1. Under the imprest system, reimbursement normally equals:
   A. petty cash expenditure  B. the float plus expenditure  C. sales  D. closing inventory
2. A trial balance is prepared from:
   A. ledger balances  B. invoices only  C. bank statements only  D. cash vouchers only
3. An error of principle occurs when:
   A. a transaction is posted to the wrong class of account  B. figures are added wrongly  C. a transaction is omitted entirely  D. a balance is carried down
4. A suspense account is:
   A. temporary  B. a permanent expense  C. an asset used for stock  D. a sales account
5. Gross profit equals:
   A. net sales less cost of goods sold  B. assets less liabilities  C. capital less drawings  D. cash less bank
6. Returns outwards reduce:
   A. purchases  B. sales  C. capital  D. closing inventory
7. Carriage inwards is normally included in:
   A. cost of goods sold  B. office salaries  C. drawings  D. bank loan
8. Net profit is transferred to:
   A. capital  B. purchases  C. creditors  D. petty cash
9. A balance sheet is prepared:
   A. as at a particular date  B. for every transaction  C. only when cash is received  D. before every sale
10. A bank lender is interested in a balance sheet partly because it shows:
    A. assets and liabilities  B. staff attendance  C. advertising costs only  D. product design

## Section B — Short answers (10 marks)

11. State two advantages of the imprest system. (2)
12. State two errors that may not be disclosed by a trial balance. (2)
13. Explain the purpose of a suspense account. (2)
14. Define cost of goods sold and gross profit. (2)
15. State two groups of people who use a balance sheet and one reason for each. (2)

## Section C — Calculations and application (40 marks)

16. **Petty cash (8 marks).** A petty cashier is given ₦15,000. Vouchers show: bus fares ₦2,400; postage ₦1,100; cleaning ₦1,800; stationery ₦2,200; refreshments ₦1,000.
    a. Calculate total expenditure. (3)
    b. Calculate the balance remaining. (2)
    c. Calculate reimbursement under the imprest system. (3)

17. **Trial balance and errors (8 marks).** The debit column totals ₦245,600 and the credit column totals ₦239,100.
    a. Calculate the difference. (2)
    b. State the temporary suspense entry needed to make the columns agree. (3)
    c. Give two reasons why agreement after this entry does not prove that the books are error-free. (3)

18. **Trading account (10 marks).** A trader has opening inventory ₦40,000; purchases ₦150,000; returns outwards ₦10,000; carriage inwards ₦5,000; closing inventory ₦35,000; sales ₦240,000; returns inwards ₦8,000.
    Calculate:
    a. net sales (2);
    b. net purchases (2);
    c. cost of goods sold (3);
    d. gross profit (3).

19. **Profit and loss account (6 marks).** Gross profit is ₦72,000. Expenses: salaries ₦20,000; rent ₦9,000; electricity ₦4,000; carriage outwards ₦3,000. Other income: commission received ₦2,000. Calculate net profit.

20. **Balance sheet equation (8 marks).** A business has equipment ₦80,000; inventory ₦25,000; debtors ₦15,000; cash ₦5,000; bank ₦20,000; creditors ₦18,000; bank loan ₦32,000.
    a. Calculate total assets. (3)
    b. Calculate total liabilities. (2)
    c. Calculate owner's capital. (3)

## Marking guide

**Section A:** 1 A; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A; 5 A; 6 A; 7 A; 8 A; 9 A; 10 A.

**Section B:** Award 1 mark per valid point.
11. Controls petty cash; provides a fixed spending limit; makes replenishment auditable.
12. Complete omission; error of principle; compensating errors; reversal of entries (any two).
13. Temporarily records a trial-balance difference while errors are located and corrected.
14. Cost of goods sold is the cost of inventory sold; gross profit is net sales less cost of goods sold.
15. Owners: assess position/performance; lenders: assess repayment capacity; suppliers: assess credit risk; investors: assess financial position (any two groups with reasons).

**Question 16 (8 marks):** Expenditure = 2,400 + 1,100 + 1,800 + 2,200 + 1,000 = **₦8,500** (3). Balance = 15,000 − 8,500 = **₦6,500** (2). Reimbursement = **₦8,500** (3).

**Question 17 (8 marks):** Difference = ₦6,500 (2). Debit total exceeds credit total, so enter ₦6,500 on the credit side of suspense (3). Any two valid undetected errors with explanation (3), e.g. complete omission or error of principle.

**Question 18 (10 marks):** Net sales = 240,000 − 8,000 = **₦232,000** (2). Net purchases = 150,000 − 10,000 = **₦140,000** (2). COGS = 40,000 + 140,000 + 5,000 − 35,000 = **₦150,000** (3). Gross profit = 232,000 − 150,000 = **₦82,000** (3).

**Question 19 (6 marks):** Expenses = 20,000 + 9,000 + 4,000 + 3,000 = ₦36,000 (2). Net profit = 72,000 − 36,000 + 2,000 = **₦38,000** (4).

**Question 20 (8 marks):** Assets = 80,000 + 25,000 + 15,000 + 5,000 + 20,000 = **₦145,000** (3). Liabilities = 18,000 + 32,000 = **₦50,000** (2). Capital = 145,000 − 50,000 = **₦95,000** (3).
