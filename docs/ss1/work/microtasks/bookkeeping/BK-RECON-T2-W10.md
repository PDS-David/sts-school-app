## Topic
Bank reconciliation statement

## Learning outcomes
Learners should be able to explain why the cash-book bank balance may differ from the bank statement; identify common timing differences and bank-originated entries; update the cash book for items not yet recorded; and prepare a simple bank reconciliation statement.

## Prerequisite
Learners should understand the bank column of the cash book, debit and credit balances, and the meaning of a bank overdraft.

## 1. Meaning
A bank reconciliation statement is a statement prepared to explain the difference between the bank balance shown by the business's cash book and the balance shown by the bank statement at a particular date.

The bank statement is the record supplied by the bank. The business's cash book is its own record. Their balances may differ temporarily because transactions are recorded at different times or because the bank has recorded an item that the business has not yet entered.

## 2. Common causes of differences
**Timing differences**
- Unpresented cheques: cheques issued and entered in the cash book but not yet presented to the bank for payment.
- Deposits not yet credited: money paid into the bank and entered in the cash book but not yet shown on the bank statement.

**Items first appearing on the bank statement**
- bank charges;
- direct debits or standing orders;
- credit transfers received directly into the account;
- dishonoured cheques;
- interest charged or credited by the bank.

The business should first update its cash book for bank-originated items that it has not yet recorded. Timing differences are then used to reconcile the updated cash-book balance to the bank statement balance.

## 3. Two-stage method
**Stage 1 — Update the cash book.** Enter bank charges, direct debits, credit transfers, dishonoured cheques and other bank statement items missing from the cash book. Recalculate the bank balance.

**Stage 2 — Prepare the reconciliation statement.** Starting with the updated cash-book balance, adjust for unpresented cheques and deposits not yet credited to arrive at the bank statement balance. Always read the question carefully: the direction of each adjustment depends on which balance is the starting point.

## 4. Worked example
At 30 June, the cash book shows a favourable bank balance of 25,000. The following items appear on the bank statement but have not yet been entered in the cash book:
- bank charges, 500;
- a direct credit received from a customer, 1,200.

The following timing differences are also identified:
- cheques issued totalling 4,000 have not yet been presented;
- a deposit of 6,500 has not yet been credited by the bank.

### Stage 1: Update the cash book
Start with cash-book balance: 25,000.
Add direct credit received: +1,200.
Deduct bank charges: −500.
Updated cash-book balance = **25,700 favourable**.

### Stage 2: Prepare the reconciliation statement
Starting with the updated cash-book balance:
- add unpresented cheques: +4,000;
- deduct deposit not yet credited: −6,500.

Reconciled bank statement balance = 25,700 + 4,000 − 6,500 = **23,200 favourable**.

| Bank reconciliation statement at 30 June | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Updated cash-book balance | 25,700 |
| Add: unpresented cheques | 4,000 |
| Less: deposit not yet credited | (6,500) |
| **Balance as per bank statement** | **23,200** |

The reconciliation agrees. The bank charges and direct credit were first entered in the updated cash book; they are not repeated as timing differences in the statement.

## 6. Guided practice
The cash book shows 14,000 favourable. Bank charges of 300 and a direct credit of 2,000 are missing from the cash book. Calculate the updated cash-book balance.

**Answer:** 14,000 − 300 + 2,000 = **15,700 favourable**.

## 7. Independent assessment
**Q1 (4 marks).** Define a bank reconciliation statement and state its purpose.
**Answer:** It is a statement explaining the difference between the cash-book bank balance and bank statement balance at a given date. Its purpose is to identify and explain reconciling items and help confirm the correct bank position.
**Marking:** Definition 2; purpose 2.

**Q2 (4 marks).** Explain unpresented cheques and deposits not yet credited.
**Answer:** Unpresented cheques have been issued and entered in the cash book but have not yet been presented to the bank. Deposits not yet credited have been paid in and entered in the cash book but have not yet appeared on the bank statement.
**Marking:** 2 marks each.

**Q3 (5 marks).** Cash book balance is 32,000 favourable. Bank charges of 400 and a direct credit of 1,500 are missing from the cash book. Calculate the updated balance.
**Answer:** 32,000 − 400 + 1,500 = **33,100 favourable**.
**Marking:** Correct treatment 3; final balance 2.

**Q4 (5 marks).** Updated cash-book balance is 18,000 favourable. Unpresented cheques are 2,500 and deposits not yet credited are 3,200. Calculate the bank statement balance.
**Answer:** 18,000 + 2,500 − 3,200 = **17,300 favourable**.
**Marking:** Correct direction of adjustments 3; final balance 2.

**Total: 18 marks.**

## Completion check
Learners can separate cash-book updates from timing differences and reconcile a bank statement balance without reversing adjustment directions.
