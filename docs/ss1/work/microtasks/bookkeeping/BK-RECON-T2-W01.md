## Topic
Books of original entry: purpose and overview

## Learning outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:
1. Explain what a book of original entry is.
2. State why transactions are first recorded in books of original entry.
3. Distinguish books of original entry from ledger accounts.
4. Identify the appropriate book for common business transactions.
5. Match a transaction to a suitable source document.

## Prerequisite
Learners should already understand business transactions, debit and credit, and the introductory cash book and double-entry ideas taught in the first term.

## 1. Meaning of books of original entry
A book of original entry is an accounting record in which a business first records transactions from source documents before posting the relevant information to ledger accounts. These books are also called books of prime entry or subsidiary books.

A source document provides evidence that a transaction took place. Examples include an invoice, receipt, credit note, debit note, cheque counterfoil and bank advice. The document is checked before the transaction is entered in the appropriate book.

## 2. Why businesses use books of original entry
They help a business to:
- record transactions promptly and in date order;
- group transactions of a similar type together;
- reduce repeated writing in the ledger;
- provide a clear audit trail from a ledger entry back to its source document;
- make it easier to check, total and post transactions accurately.

A book of original entry does not replace the ledger. It organises the first record; the ledger groups the resulting entries under individual accounts.

## 3. Common books of original entry
| Book | Main use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sales day book | Credit sales of goods for resale | Goods sold on credit to a retailer |
| Purchases day book | Credit purchases of goods for resale | Goods bought on credit from a wholesaler |
| Returns inwards book | Goods returned by credit customers | A customer returns damaged goods previously bought on credit |
| Returns outwards book | Goods returned to credit suppliers | The business returns defective goods previously bought on credit |
| Cash book | Cash and bank receipts and payments | Cash sales, cheque payments and bank receipts |
| General journal (journal proper) | Appropriate entries not first recorded in the other specialised books | Opening entries, correction entries and certain non-routine adjustments |

Important boundaries:
- A cash sale is recorded in the cash book, not the sales day book.
- A credit sale of goods bought for resale is recorded in the sales day book.
- Buying a motor vehicle on credit is not a purchase of goods for resale; it is not entered in the purchases day book as ordinary goods purchases.
- A return must be identified from the business's point of view: goods returned by customers are returns inwards; goods returned to suppliers are returns outwards.
- A trade discount is normally deducted before the transaction is recorded; it is not itself entered as a separate cash-book payment.

## 4. Source documents and first recording
The document depends on the transaction. A credit sale may be supported by a sales invoice; goods returned by a customer may be supported by a credit note; goods returned to a supplier may be supported by a debit note. A cash receipt or payment may be supported by a receipt, payment voucher or bank record.

The learner should ask:
1. What happened?
2. Is it a cash/bank transaction or a credit transaction?
3. Are the goods for resale, or is the item an asset/expense?
4. Which source document supports the transaction?
5. Which book of original entry is appropriate?

## 5. Worked classification example
A small business has the following transactions:

1. Sold goods on credit to Amina Stores, 18,000.
2. Bought goods on credit from Bello Traders, 12,500.
3. Amina Stores returned goods, 2,000.
4. Returned goods to Bello Traders, 1,500.
5. Sold goods for cash, 4,000.
6. Bought a delivery bicycle for cash, 25,000.

Classification:
1. Sales day book — credit sale of goods for resale.
2. Purchases day book — credit purchase of goods for resale.
3. Returns inwards book — customer returned goods.
4. Returns outwards book — business returned goods to supplier.
5. Cash book — cash sale.
6. Cash book — cash payment for an asset. The bicycle is not goods bought for resale.

The amounts are fictional teaching figures. They are not taken from the duplicate second-term document.

## 6. Guided practice
For each transaction, name the appropriate book of original entry and give one reason.

A. Sold goods on credit to Kola Stores, 9,600.
B. Paid office electricity by bank transfer, 3,200.
C. Returned goods to a supplier, 1,750.
D. A customer returned goods previously bought on credit, 900.

### Guided answers
A. Sales day book — credit sale of goods for resale.
B. Cash book — payment through the bank.
C. Returns outwards book — goods returned to a supplier.
D. Returns inwards book — goods returned by a customer.

## 7. Independent assessment
**Question 1 (5 marks).** Define a book of original entry and state two reasons businesses use it.

**Answer:** A book of original entry is the book in which a transaction is first recorded from its source document before the relevant information is posted to the ledger. Reasons include recording transactions in date order, grouping similar transactions, reducing repeated ledger entries and providing an audit trail.

**Marking:** Definition, 2 marks; any two valid reasons, 1.5 marks each.

**Question 2 (6 marks).** Select the correct book for each item:
(a) cash received from a customer for goods sold immediately;
(b) goods bought on credit for resale;
(c) goods returned by a credit customer;
(d) goods returned to a credit supplier;
(e) goods sold on credit;
(f) a correction entry that does not belong in a specialised day book.

**Answer:** (a) Cash book; (b) Purchases day book; (c) Returns inwards book; (d) Returns outwards book; (e) Sales day book; (f) General journal.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each correct book.

**Question 3 (4 marks).** State two differences between a book of original entry and a ledger.

**Answer:** A book of original entry records transactions first, usually in date order and grouped by type; a ledger groups entries under individual accounts. The first provides the initial record from source documents; the ledger shows the account-by-account effect and balances.

**Marking:** Two clear contrasts, 2 marks each.

**Total: 15 marks.**
