# SS1 Bookkeeping — Reconstructed Second Term, Week 3

<badge>Project-authored reconstruction — Bookkeeping</badge>

## Topic
Sales day book and purchases day book

## Learning outcomes
By the end of the lesson, learners should be able to:
1. Explain the purpose of the sales day book and purchases day book.
2. Distinguish credit sales from cash sales.
3. Identify which transactions belong in the sales and purchases day books.
4. Enter credit transactions in the correct day book using source-document details.
5. Calculate the net amount after a trade discount and total each day book accurately.

## Prerequisite
Learners should understand books of original entry, source documents and the distinction between cash and credit transactions (Weeks 1–2).

## 1. Meaning of the sales day book
The sales day book is a book of original entry used to record **credit sales of goods bought for resale**. It is prepared from copies of sales invoices issued to credit customers.

It does not record:
- cash sales;
- sale of old business assets, such as a used office table or delivery van;
- goods returned by customers (these belong in the returns inwards book).

## 2. Meaning of the purchases day book
The purchases day book is a book of original entry used to record **credit purchases of goods for resale**. It is prepared from suppliers' invoices received by the business.

It does not record:
- cash purchases;
- purchase of fixed assets, such as furniture, computers or vehicles;
- goods returned to suppliers (these belong in the returns outwards book).

The phrase *goods for resale* is important. A shop buying exercise books to sell to customers records the credit purchase in the purchases day book. The same shop buying a computer for its office does not.

## 3. Trade discount
A supplier may allow a trade discount from the catalogue or list price. The day book records the **net invoice amount after deducting the trade discount**.

Trade discount is not entered as a separate amount in the day book in the basic examples in this lesson.

Formula:

Net amount = List price − Trade discount

For a percentage discount:

Trade discount = List price × Discount rate ÷ 100

## 4. Formats

### Sales day book

| Date | Customer | Invoice No. | Folio | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| | | | | |

### Purchases day book

| Date | Supplier | Invoice No. | Folio | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| | | | | |

- **Date:** date on the invoice.
- **Customer/Supplier:** name of the person or business.
- **Invoice number:** reference on the source document.
- **Folio:** ledger page reference, completed when posting.
- **Amount:** net credit invoice amount after trade discount, where applicable.

## 5. Worked example A — sales day book
A business sells goods on credit as follows:

- 3 March: Ada Stores, invoice S101, goods listed at 24,000 less 10% trade discount.
- 6 March: Bayo Mart, invoice S102, goods listed at 18,000 with no discount.

Calculation:
- Ada Stores: 24,000 × 10% = 2,400 discount; net amount = 24,000 − 2,400 = 21,600.
- Bayo Mart: net amount = 18,000.
- Total sales day book = 21,600 + 18,000 = 39,600.

| Date | Customer | Invoice No. | Folio | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| Mar. 3 | Ada Stores | S101 | — | 21,600 |
| Mar. 6 | Bayo Mart | S102 | — | 18,000 |
| | **Total** | | | **39,600** |

## 6. Worked example B — purchases day book
A business buys goods for resale on credit as follows:

- 4 April: from Chika Wholesalers, invoice P201, list price 35,000 less 5% trade discount.
- 9 April: from Dada Supplies, invoice P202, list price 28,000 with no discount.

Calculation:
- Chika Wholesalers: 35,000 × 5% = 1,750 discount; net amount = 35,000 − 1,750 = 33,250.
- Dada Supplies: net amount = 28,000.
- Total purchases day book = 33,250 + 28,000 = 61,250.

| Date | Supplier | Invoice No. | Folio | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| Apr. 4 | Chika Wholesalers | P201 | — | 33,250 |
| Apr. 9 | Dada Supplies | P202 | — | 28,000 |
| | **Total** | | | **61,250** |

## 7. How the totals relate to the ledger
At the end of the period, the day-book total is posted to the appropriate goods account, while each customer's or supplier's individual amount is posted to that person's account.

For the sales day book:
- debit each credit customer's personal account;
- credit the Sales Account with the total of the sales day book.

For the purchases day book:
- credit each credit supplier's personal account;
- debit the Purchases Account with the total of the purchases day book.

The full posting process is practised in the later ledger-posting lesson. The folio column is used to cross-reference the ledger entries.

## 8. Guided practice
Classify each transaction. State whether it belongs in the sales day book, purchases day book or neither.

A. Sold goods on credit to Efe Stores.
B. Bought goods for resale on credit from Femi Traders.
C. Sold goods for cash to a walk-in customer.
D. Bought a printer for the office on credit.
E. Sold an old office desk on credit.

### Guided answers
A. Sales day book — credit sale of goods for resale.
B. Purchases day book — credit purchase of goods for resale.
C. Neither — cash sales are recorded in the cash book.
D. Neither — the printer is a fixed asset, not goods for resale.
E. Neither — sale of a business asset is not a sale of trading goods.

## 9. Independent assessment

**Question 1 (4 marks).** State the purpose of:
(a) the sales day book; and
(b) the purchases day book.

**Answer:**
(a) Records credit sales of goods bought for resale.
(b) Records credit purchases of goods for resale.

**Marking:** 2 marks each, including the credit and goods-for-resale conditions.

**Question 2 (4 marks).** State whether each transaction belongs in the sales day book, purchases day book or neither:
(a) goods sold on credit to a customer;
(b) goods bought for cash for resale;
(c) goods bought on credit for resale;
(d) a delivery van bought on credit.

**Answer:** (a) Sales day book; (b) Neither — cash book; (c) Purchases day book; (d) Neither — fixed asset purchase.

**Marking:** 1 mark each.

**Question 3 (5 marks).** Prepare the sales day book total from these credit sales:
- 2 May: Gani Stores, invoice S301, list price 20,000 less 10% trade discount.
- 5 May: Hawa Mart, invoice S302, 15,500 with no discount.
- 8 May: Ikenna Shop, invoice S303, list price 12,000 less 5% trade discount.

**Answer:**
- Gani Stores: 20,000 − 2,000 = 18,000.
- Hawa Mart: 15,500.
- Ikenna Shop: 12,000 − 600 = 11,400.
- Total = 18,000 + 15,500 + 11,400 = **44,900**.

**Marking:** Net amount for each invoice, 1 mark each; correct total, 2 marks.

**Question 4 (5 marks).** Prepare the purchases day book total from these credit purchases of goods for resale:
- 3 June: Jola Wholesalers, invoice P401, list price 40,000 less 5% trade discount.
- 7 June: Kemi Distributors, invoice P402, 22,500 with no discount.

**Answer:**
- Jola Wholesalers: 40,000 − 2,000 = 38,000.
- Kemi Distributors: 22,500.
- Total = 38,000 + 22,500 = **60,500**.

**Marking:** Correct discount and net amount for Jola, 2 marks; Kemi amount, 1 mark; correct total, 2 marks.

**Total: 18 marks.**

## Completion check
A learner is ready to proceed when they can distinguish credit trading transactions from cash transactions and asset purchases, prepare both day books from invoice details and calculate net amounts after trade discount.
