# SS1 Bookkeeping — Reconstructed Second Term, Week 9

**Project-authored reconstruction — Bookkeeping; not recovered source material**

## Topic
Three-column cash book: balancing and integrated practice

## Learning outcomes
Learners should be able to enter mixed transactions in a three-column cash book; total and balance cash and bank columns; identify a bank overdraft; and post discount totals to the relevant ledger accounts.

## Prerequisite
Week 8: three-column cash-book layout, discount columns and contra entries.

## 1. What is balancing?
Balancing compares the debit and credit totals of an account and determines the difference. In a three-column cash book:
- the cash column normally has a debit balance or zero; it cannot normally have a credit balance because cash in hand cannot be negative;
- the bank column may have a debit balance (money at bank) or a credit balance (bank overdraft);
- discount columns are totalled, not balanced.

## 2. Balancing procedure
1. Add the debit-side cash entries and credit-side cash entries.
2. Find the difference and enter it as balance c/d on the smaller side so both sides agree.
3. Bring the balance down on the opposite side as balance b/d at the start of the next period.
4. Repeat separately for the bank column.
5. Add each discount column and post its total to the appropriate discount account.

The balance c/d is the figure used to make the two sides equal at period-end. The balance b/d is the opening balance for the next period.

## 3. Worked integrated example
A business has the following opening balances on 1 November: cash 12,000; bank 30,000.

Transactions:
- Nov. 2: cash sales 4,500.
- Nov. 4: paid rent in cash 2,000.
- Nov. 6: received 4,900 by bank transfer from a customer who owed 5,000; discount allowed 100.
- Nov. 8: paid a supplier 7,800 by cheque in full settlement of 8,000; discount received 200.
- Nov. 10: deposited 3,000 cash into bank.

### Three-column cash book (summary)

| Debit side | Discount | Cash | Bank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Balance b/d | — | 12,000 | 30,000 |
| Cash sales | — | 4,500 | — |
| Customer | 100 | — | 4,900 |
| Cash (contra) | — | — | 3,000 |
| **Total before balancing** | **100** | **16,500** | **37,900** |

| Credit side | Discount | Cash | Bank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Rent | — | 2,000 | — |
| Supplier | 200 | — | 7,800 |
| Bank (contra) | — | 3,000 | — |
| Balance c/d | — | 11,500 | 30,100 |
| **Total** | **200** | **16,500** | **37,900** |

The bank balance is 30,100 debit; cash balance is 11,500 debit. The discount allowed total is 100 and discount received total is 200. The two discount totals are separate and are not added to the cash/bank totals.

On 11 November, bring down cash 11,500 and bank 30,100 on the debit side.

## 4. Posting discount totals
At the end of the period:
- debit Discount Allowed Account with the total of the discount allowed column (100 in the example);
- credit Discount Received Account with the total of the discount received column (200 in the example).

The corresponding customer and supplier accounts are posted with the full amounts settled, as shown in the Week 8 explanation. Cash-book cash/bank columns record only the actual money received or paid.

## 5. Bank overdraft
If the credit side of the bank column exceeds the debit side, the difference is a bank overdraft. It is shown as a credit balance and brought down on the debit side in the next period. Do not describe a credit bank balance as money available in the bank; it represents an amount owed to the bank.

## 6. Guided practice
Opening balances: cash 8,000; bank 15,000. Cash receipts 2,000; cash payments 3,500; bank receipts 4,000; bank payments 6,000. Calculate closing balances.

**Answer:** Cash = 8,000 + 2,000 − 3,500 = 6,500 debit. Bank = 15,000 + 4,000 − 6,000 = 13,000 debit.

## 7. Independent assessment
**Q1 (4 marks).** Explain balance c/d and balance b/d.
**Answer:** Balance c/d is the amount entered to make the two sides equal at the end of the period; balance b/d is that balance brought forward as the opening figure for the next period.
**Marking:** 2 marks each.

**Q2 (4 marks).** Opening cash is 9,000. Cash received is 3,500 and cash paid is 4,200. Calculate closing cash.
**Answer:** 9,000 + 3,500 − 4,200 = **8,300 debit**.
**Marking:** Correct method 2; answer 2.

**Q3 (5 marks).** Opening bank balance is 18,000 debit. Bank receipts are 6,500 and bank payments are 27,000. Calculate and explain the closing position.
**Answer:** 18,000 + 6,500 − 27,000 = **2,500 credit**, a bank overdraft.
**Marking:** Method 3; amount 1; identifies overdraft 1.

**Q4 (5 marks).** Discount allowed total is 450 and discount received total is 320. State the postings to the discount accounts.
**Answer:** Debit Discount Allowed Account 450; credit Discount Received Account 320.
**Marking:** 2.5 marks each.

**Total: 18 marks.**

## Completion check
Learners can balance cash and bank columns independently, recognise an overdraft and keep discount totals separate from cash/bank balances.
