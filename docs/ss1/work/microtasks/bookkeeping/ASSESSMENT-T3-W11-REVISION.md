## Coverage

Petty cash and imprest; trial balance; errors affecting and not affecting agreement; suspense account; trading account; cost of goods sold and gross profit; profit and loss account; balance sheet and its users.

## Section A — Multiple choice (10 marks)

1. The imprest system aims to restore petty cash to:
   A. a fixed float  B. zero  C. the bank balance  D. the sales total
2. A trial balance is prepared mainly to test:
   A. arithmetical agreement of ledger balances  B. profitability  C. cash availability  D. stock quality
3. An error of complete omission means:
   A. a transaction is not recorded at all  B. wrong account on one side  C. wrong addition only  D. a balance is reversed
4. An error of commission involves:
   A. correct amount in the wrong account of the same class  B. no entry  C. unequal trial balance totals  D. wrong period only
5. A suspense account may be opened when:
   A. trial balance totals disagree  B. profit is high  C. cash is stolen  D. stock is counted
6. Gross profit is:
   A. net sales less cost of goods sold  B. sales less expenses  C. assets less liabilities  D. capital less drawings
7. Cost of goods sold is generally:
   A. opening inventory + net purchases + direct expenses − closing inventory
   B. sales + closing inventory
   C. capital + liabilities
   D. expenses − income
8. Net profit is calculated after:
   A. deducting indirect expenses from gross profit and adding other income  B. adding capital to sales  C. deducting liabilities from assets  D. balancing petty cash
9. A balance sheet reports:
   A. assets, liabilities and capital at a date  B. daily sales only  C. only expenses  D. only cash receipts
10. A supplier is most likely to use a balance sheet to assess:
    A. ability to pay debts  B. employee punctuality  C. product colour  D. advertising reach

## Section B — Short answers (10 marks)

11. Define the imprest system. (2)
12. State two purposes and two limitations of a trial balance. (4)
13. What is a suspense account, and why should it not be treated as a permanent solution? (2)
14. Distinguish gross profit from net profit. (2)

## Section C — Applied questions (30 marks)

15. **Petty cash (6 marks).** A petty cashier receives a float of ₦20,000. During the week, vouchers are: transport ₦4,000; postage ₦1,500; cleaning ₦2,500; stationery ₦3,000. Calculate total expenditure and the reimbursement required to restore the float.

16. **Trial balance and suspense (8 marks).** A trial balance has debit total ₦186,000 and credit total ₦178,500. Calculate the difference. State the temporary entry needed to make the trial balance agree and explain what must happen afterwards.

17. **Trading account (8 marks).** Opening inventory ₦30,000; purchases ₦120,000; returns outwards ₦5,000; carriage inwards ₦4,000; closing inventory ₦25,000; net sales ₦180,000. Calculate net purchases, cost of goods sold and gross profit.

18. **Profit and loss (4 marks).** Gross profit is ₦55,000; salaries ₦18,000; rent ₦8,000; electricity ₦3,000; commission received ₦2,000. Calculate net profit.

19. **Balance sheet (4 marks).** Assets are cash ₦12,000, bank ₦28,000, inventory ₦20,000, equipment ₦60,000 and debtors ₦10,000. Liabilities are creditors ₦25,000 and a bank loan ₦30,000. Calculate capital using the accounting equation.

## Answer key and marking guide

**Section A:** 1 A; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A; 5 A; 6 A; 7 A; 8 A; 9 A; 10 A.

**Section B:**
11. A fixed amount is advanced to the petty cashier; after accounting for expenditure, reimbursement restores the float (2).
12. Purposes: check arithmetic, assist location of some errors, summarise ledger balances (any two, 1 each). Limitations: does not reveal complete omission, error of principle, compensating errors, or reversal of entries (any two, 1 each).
13. Temporary account used to hold a difference while errors are investigated (1); errors must be found and corrected, not left hidden (1).
14. Gross profit = net sales less cost of goods sold; net profit is after indirect expenses and other income (1 each).

**Question 15 (6 marks):** Expenditure = 4,000 + 1,500 + 2,500 + 3,000 = **₦11,000** (3). Cash remaining = ₦9,000 (1). Reimbursement = **₦11,000** to restore the ₦20,000 float (2).

**Question 16 (8 marks):** Difference = ₦7,500 (2). If debit total exceeds credit total, a temporary **credit** entry of ₦7,500 in suspense account makes totals agree (3). Investigate the errors and pass correcting entries; clear suspense when resolved (3).

**Question 17 (8 marks):**
- Net purchases = 120,000 − 5,000 = ₦115,000 (2).
- Cost of goods sold = 30,000 + 115,000 + 4,000 − 25,000 = **₦124,000** (3).
- Gross profit = 180,000 − 124,000 = **₦56,000** (3).

**Question 18 (4 marks):** Indirect expenses = 18,000 + 8,000 + 3,000 = ₦29,000 (1). Net profit = 55,000 − 29,000 + 2,000 = **₦28,000** (3).

**Question 19 (4 marks):** Total assets = ₦130,000 (1). Total liabilities = ₦55,000 (1). Capital = assets − liabilities = **₦75,000** (2).

**Teacher note:** A trial balance agreement does not prove that every transaction is correct or recorded.
