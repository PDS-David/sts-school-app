# SS1 Bookkeeping — Reconstructed Second Term, Week 4

**Project-authored reconstruction — Bookkeeping; not recovered source material**

## Topic
Returns inwards and returns outwards books

## Learning outcomes
Learners should be able to define returns inwards and returns outwards; identify the source documents; distinguish the two books; record credit returns accurately; and explain how their totals relate to ledger accounts.

## Prerequisite
Weeks 1–3: books of original entry, source documents, sales and purchases day books, and trade discount.

## 1. Meaning
**Returns inwards** are goods previously sold on credit by the business that customers return. They are recorded in the **returns inwards book**, also called the sales returns book. A credit note issued by the business is the usual source document.

**Returns outwards** are goods previously bought on credit by the business that it sends back to suppliers. They are recorded in the **returns outwards book**, also called the purchases returns book. A debit note prepared by the business is a common source document; the supplier may acknowledge it with a credit note.

The names are from the business's point of view:
- goods coming back in from a customer = returns inwards;
- goods going out back to a supplier = returns outwards.

Cash refunds and cash purchases/returns are recorded through the cash book as appropriate, not automatically in the credit returns books.

## 2. Formats

### Returns inwards book
| Date | Customer | Credit note no. | Folio | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| | | | | |

### Returns outwards book
| Date | Supplier | Debit note no. | Folio | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| | | | | |

Record the net value of the goods returned, using the original invoice basis. Do not add a second trade discount if the original invoice value already reflects it.

## 3. Worked example — returns inwards
A business receives these goods back from credit customers:
- 4 July: Lami Stores returns goods valued at 3,600; credit note CN11.
- 9 July: Muna Mart returns goods valued at 2,450; credit note CN12.

| Date | Customer | Credit note | Folio | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| Jul. 4 | Lami Stores | CN11 | — | 3,600 |
| Jul. 9 | Muna Mart | CN12 | — | 2,450 |
| | **Total returns inwards** | | | **6,050** |

Calculation: 3,600 + 2,450 = 6,050.

## 4. Worked example — returns outwards
A business returns goods to credit suppliers:
- 5 August: Nuru Wholesalers, 1,800; debit note DN21.
- 12 August: Ojo Distributors, 2,700; debit note DN22.

| Date | Supplier | Debit note | Folio | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| Aug. 5 | Nuru Wholesalers | DN21 | — | 1,800 |
| Aug. 12 | Ojo Distributors | DN22 | — | 2,700 |
| | **Total returns outwards** | | | **4,500** |

Calculation: 1,800 + 2,700 = 4,500.

## 5. Ledger relationship
At the basic level:
- the total returns inwards book is posted to the debit of Returns Inwards Account; each customer's personal account is credited;
- the total returns outwards book is posted to the credit of Returns Outwards Account; each supplier's personal account is debited.

These entries reverse the relevant part of the original credit sale or purchase. The folio column cross-references the ledger posting.

## 6. General journal: when it is appropriate
The general journal is not a substitute for a returns book. Use the returns book when goods are returned in an ordinary credit sale or purchase. The general journal is reserved for appropriate non-routine entries that do not belong in a specialised book, such as an agreed correction or a transfer between accounts. Detailed correction-of-errors methods are treated in the later term.

## 7. Guided practice
Classify each item and name the source document:
A. A credit customer returns damaged goods.
B. The business sends defective goods back to a credit supplier.
C. A walk-in cash customer returns goods and receives cash.
D. A business corrects an account classification that was posted to the wrong expense account.

**Answers:** A. Returns inwards book; credit note. B. Returns outwards book; debit note. C. Cash book records the cash refund (with supporting refund evidence). D. General journal, if the correction is appropriately made by journal entry.

## 8. Independent assessment
**Q1 (4 marks).** Define returns inwards and returns outwards.
**Answer:** Returns inwards are goods returned by credit customers to the business; returns outwards are goods returned by the business to credit suppliers.
**Marking:** 2 marks each.

**Q2 (4 marks).** Name the book and usual source document for (a) goods returned by a credit customer and (b) goods returned to a credit supplier.
**Answer:** (a) Returns inwards book and credit note; (b) returns outwards book and debit note.
**Marking:** 1 mark for each book and document.

**Q3 (6 marks).** Prepare the returns inwards book total: 2 September, Pako Stores, CN31, 2,850; 7 September, Reni Mart, CN32, 1,950; 11 September, Sola Shop, CN33, 3,200.
**Answer:** Total = 2,850 + 1,950 + 3,200 = **8,000**.
**Marking:** Entries, 1 mark each; correct total, 3 marks.

**Q4 (4 marks).** State two differences between a returns book and the general journal.
**Answer:** A returns book records a specific class of routine goods returns; the general journal records appropriate non-routine entries not assigned to another specialised book. Returns books use credit/debit note details; the journal uses debit/credit accounts and a narration.
**Marking:** Two clear differences, 2 marks each.

**Total: 18 marks.**
