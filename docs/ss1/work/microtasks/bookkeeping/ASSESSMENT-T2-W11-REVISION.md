# SS1 Bookkeeping — Second Term Week 11 Revision

**Resource type:** Project-authored revision worksheet  
**Class:** SS1 | **Term slot:** Second Term, Week 11 (revision)  
**Suggested duration:** 75 minutes | **Total:** 50 marks  
**Provenance:** Newly authored against the provisional Bookkeeping reconstruction plan. The registered second-term source is an erroneous duplicate of first term and is not used as evidence for these questions.

## Coverage

Books of original entry; general journal; sales and purchases day books; returns books; ledger classification; posting; trade and cash discounts; three-column cash book; bank reconciliation.

## Section A — Multiple choice (10 marks)

1. A book of original entry is used to:
   A. record transactions before posting to ledger  B. prepare a balance sheet  C. store cash  D. calculate depreciation only
2. The general journal is suitable for:
   A. selected entries not recorded in a specialised day book  B. all cash sales only  C. bank statements only  D. stocktaking only
3. A sales day book records:
   A. credit sales of goods for resale  B. cash sales  C. sale of fixed assets  D. wages
4. Returns inwards are goods:
   A. returned by customers  B. returned to suppliers  C. bought for cash  D. taken by owner
5. A debtor is a person or business that:
   A. owes the business money  B. is owed money by the business  C. supplies electricity  D. audits the business
6. Trade discount is normally deducted:
   A. from list price before recording the net invoice amount  B. after balancing the ledger  C. from wages  D. from capital
7. Discount allowed is:
   A. an expense  B. income  C. a liability  D. an asset
8. In a three-column cash book, discount allowed appears on the:
   A. debit side discount column  B. credit side discount column  C. bank column only  D. balance sheet
9. An unpresented cheque is one:
   A. issued but not yet presented to the bank  B. received and banked  C. cancelled before issue  D. deposited as cash
10. A bank reconciliation statement explains differences between:
    A. cash-book bank balance and bank-statement balance  B. sales and purchases  C. assets and capital  D. debtors and creditors

## Section C — Applied questions (30 marks)

16. **Day-book classification (8 marks).** Identify the correct book of original entry for each:
    a. Credit sale of goods for resale to Bisi ₦18,000.
    b. Cash purchase of goods ₦9,000.
    c. Goods returned by a credit customer ₦2,000.
    d. Goods returned to a credit supplier ₦1,500.
    e. Purchase of office furniture on credit ₦30,000.
    f. Correction of an error through a journal entry.
    g. Credit purchase of goods from Tunde ₦24,000.
    h. Cash sale of goods ₦6,000.

17. **Discount calculations (6 marks).** An invoice lists goods at ₦80,000 less trade discount of 10%. The customer later pays the net amount within the cash-discount period and receives 5% cash discount. Calculate:
    a. trade discount; b. net invoice amount; c. cash discount; d. cash received.

18. **Three-column cash book (8 marks).** A trader receives a cheque of ₦20,000 from a customer and allows cash discount of ₦1,000. The trader pays a supplier ₦15,000 by cheque and receives cash discount of ₦750. State the cash-book entries for the two transactions and identify which discount column is used for each.

19. **Bank reconciliation (8 marks).** The cash book shows a favourable bank balance of ₦35,000. Cheques issued but not yet presented total ₦8,000. Deposits not yet credited by the bank total ₦5,000. Starting with the cash-book balance, calculate the bank-statement balance using these timing differences.

## Answer key and marking guide

**Section A:** 1 A; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A; 5 A; 6 A; 7 A; 8 A; 9 A; 10 A.

**Section B:** 1 mark per valid point. Books of original entry provide chronological first records and support later ledger posting; sales day book records credit sales while sales returns book records customer returns; trade discount reduces list price before recording while cash discount rewards prompt settlement; discount allowed is an expense and discount received is income; timing differences include unpresented cheques and deposits not yet credited.

**Question 16 (8 marks):** a Sales day book; b Cash book; c Returns inwards book; d Returns outwards book; e General journal; f General journal; g Purchases day book; h Cash book. Award 1 each.

**Question 17 (6 marks):** Trade discount = ₦8,000 (1); net invoice = ₦72,000 (1); cash discount = 5% × ₦72,000 = ₦3,600 (2); cash received = ₦68,400 (2).

**Question 18 (8 marks):**
- Customer receipt: Dr Bank ₦20,000; Cr Discount Allowed ₦1,000 (discount allowed column on debit side); Cr customer's account ₦21,000 in ledger (4). In the cash book, record bank receipt ₦20,000 and discount allowed ₦1,000 on debit side.
- Supplier payment: Cr Bank ₦15,000; Dr Discount Received ₦750 (discount received column on credit side); Dr supplier's account ₦15,750 in ledger (4). In the cash book, record bank payment ₦15,000 and discount received ₦750 on credit side.
**Important:** Cash-book discount columns record the discount component; the personal ledger account is settled at the gross amount.

**Question 19 (8 marks):** Start with cash-book favourable balance ₦35,000; add unpresented cheques ₦8,000; subtract deposits not yet credited ₦5,000; bank-statement balance = **₦38,000** (8). Accept a correctly explained reconciliation format.
