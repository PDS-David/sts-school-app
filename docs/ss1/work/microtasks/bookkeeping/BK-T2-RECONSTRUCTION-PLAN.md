## Reconstructed second-term scheme

The following 10 teaching weeks are a coherent proposed sequence. Weeks 11 and 12 are revision and examination markers. The week numbering is project-authored, not copied from the duplicate file.

| Week | Topic | Intended learning outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Books of original entry: purpose and overview | Explain why transactions are first recorded in books of original entry; distinguish books of original entry from the ledger; identify the source document supporting a transaction. |
| 2 | The general journal | Identify when the journal proper is used; explain its date, particulars, folio and debit/credit columns; prepare simple journal entries with narrations. |
| 3 | Subsidiary books: sales and purchases day books | Classify credit sales and credit purchases of goods for resale; enter transactions in the correct day book; distinguish cash transactions from credit transactions. |
| 4 | Returns books and general journal applications | Record returns inwards and returns outwards; select the appropriate subsidiary book; use the general journal for appropriate non-routine entries without confusing it with cash-book entries. |
| 5 | Ledger classification and account relationships | Classify personal, real and nominal accounts; distinguish debtors and creditors; explain how subsidiary-book totals and individual accounts relate to ledger accounts. |
| 6 | Posting from subsidiary books to ledger accounts | Post individual credit transactions to personal accounts and period totals to relevant nominal accounts; use dates, particulars and folio references accurately. |
| 7 | Discounts: trade discount and cash discount | Distinguish trade discount from cash discount; calculate simple discounts; explain where each is recorded and avoid treating trade discount as a separate ledger entry in the basic exercises. |
| 8 | Three-column cash book: layout and entries | Identify cash, bank and discount columns; distinguish discount allowed from discount received; record receipts, payments and eligible discount transactions. |
| 9 | Three-column cash book: balancing and integrated practice | Prepare and balance a three-column cash book; handle contra entries and bank overdraft appropriately; complete a fully worked integrated exercise. |
| 10 | Bank reconciliation statement | Explain why the cash-book bank balance may differ from the bank statement; identify timing differences and bank-originated items; prepare a simple reconciliation from clearly stated data. |
| 11 | Revision | Retrieve and connect second-term learning; complete mixed practice. |
| 12 | Examination | Assess the term's stated learning outcomes. |

## Why this sequence

- Weeks 1–6 extend the first-term double-entry foundation into journals, subsidiary books and ledger posting.
- Weeks 7–9 extend the first-term cash-book work to discounts and the three-column cash book.
- Week 10 introduces bank reconciliation, a distinct practical application not covered in the supplied third-term material.
- The third-term topics of petty cash, trial balance, correction of errors, suspense account and final accounts are deliberately reserved for their existing term.
- No topic is asserted to be the exact historical second-term scheme. The centre may later replace or adjust this proposed sequence if an authentic source is recovered.
