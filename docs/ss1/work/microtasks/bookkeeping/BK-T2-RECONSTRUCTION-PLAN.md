## Reconstructed second-term scheme

The following 10 teaching weeks are a **project-authored Bookkeeping reconstruction**, informed by the Bookkeeping-specific references above and adapted to avoid unnecessary duplication with the centre's surviving first- and third-term content. Weeks 11 and 12 are revision and examination markers. Week numbers are project-authored, not recovered from the duplicate file.

| Week | Topic | Intended learning outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Books of original entry: purpose, types and source-document links | Explain why transactions are first recorded in books of original entry; identify the appropriate book for common transactions; distinguish books of original entry from the ledger. |
| 2 | General journal: purpose, format and narration | Identify transactions suited to the general journal; explain its date, particulars, folio, debit and credit columns; prepare simple entries with narrations. |
| 3 | Sales and purchases day books | Identify credit sales and credit purchases of goods for resale; classify source documents; enter transactions in the correct day book and total the books accurately. |
| 4 | Returns inwards and returns outwards books | Distinguish returns inwards from returns outwards; select the correct returns book; record and total returns from credit notes and related source documents. |
| 5 | Ledger classification and account relationships | Classify personal, real and nominal accounts; distinguish debtors and creditors; explain the relationship between books of original entry and ledger accounts. |
| 6 | Posting from subsidiary books to ledger accounts | Post individual transactions to personal accounts and appropriate totals to ledger accounts; use dates, particulars and folio references correctly. |
| 7 | Discounts: trade discount and cash discount | Distinguish trade discount from cash discount; calculate simple discounts; distinguish discount allowed from discount received and identify the appropriate bookkeeping treatment. |
| 8 | Three-column cash book: layout and discount columns | Identify cash, bank and discount columns; distinguish discount allowed and received; extend prior cash-book learning without reteaching the first-term single-/double-column lessons. |
| 9 | Three-column cash book: recording, balancing and integrated practice | Record receipts, payments and eligible discounts; balance the cash and bank columns; treat contra entries correctly; complete an integrated exercise with checked answers. |
| 10 | Bank reconciliation statement | Explain why the cash-book bank balance may differ from the bank statement; identify timing differences and bank-originated entries; prepare a simple reconciliation from fully stated data. |
| 11 | Revision | Retrieve and connect the term's Bookkeeping learning; complete mixed practice. |
| 12 | Examination | Assess the term's stated learning outcomes. |

## Sequencing rationale and boundaries

- Weeks 1–4 develop the **books of original entry** strand explicitly present in the Bookkeeping-specific SS1 second-term schemes.
- Weeks 5–6 extend the first-term double-entry and cash-book posting foundation to subsidiary books and ledger relationships.
- Week 7 introduces discount concepts needed for the three-column cash book.
- Weeks 8–9 extend, rather than repeat, the first-term cash-book work by introducing the discount columns and integrated three-column cash-book practice.
- Week 10 adds bank reconciliation, a Bookkeeping topic present in the Bookkeeping-specific Lagos State scheme transcription and not taught in the supplied third-term document.
- Petty cash is not inserted into this reconstruction because the centre's supplied third-term Bookkeeping source already teaches petty cash and the imprest system. Trial balance, correction of errors, suspense account and final accounts likewise remain in the supplied third term.
- The alternative Bookkeeping schemes' differing week orders and overlaps are recorded as reference variation, not silently treated as the centre's historical scheme.
- No topic is asserted to be the exact historical second-term scheme. If an authentic centre source is recovered later, it can supersede this reconstruction.
