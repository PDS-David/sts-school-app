# SS1 Bookkeeping — Reconstructed Second Term, Week 8

**Project-authored reconstruction — Bookkeeping; not recovered source material**

## Topic
Three-column cash book: layout and entries

## Learning outcomes
Learners should be able to identify the cash, bank and discount columns; distinguish discount allowed from discount received; record receipts and payments; and identify contra entries.

## Prerequisite
First-term single- and double-column cash books, contra entries, and Week 7 discount calculations.

## 1. Meaning
A three-column cash book records cash and bank transactions and cash discounts. It has three amount columns on each side: Discount, Cash and Bank. It serves as both a book of original entry and a ledger account for cash and bank.

## 2. Layout

| Debit (receipts) side | Discount allowed | Cash | Bank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Date / particulars / folio | | | |

| Credit (payments) side | Discount received | Cash | Bank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Date / particulars / folio | | | |

- **Receipts/debit side:** money received in cash or paid into the bank; discount allowed to a customer is entered in the discount column on this side.
- **Payments/credit side:** money paid out in cash or from the bank; discount received from a supplier is entered in the discount column on this side.
- The cash column records physical cash; the bank column records money in the business bank account.
- A bank overdraft is a credit balance in the bank column.

## 3. Discount columns
When a customer owing 10,000 pays 9,700 in full settlement, the business allows 300 discount:
- debit side: Discount 300; Cash/Bank 9,700; particulars: customer.
- customer account is credited with 10,000.
- Discount Allowed Account is debited with 300.

When the business pays a supplier's 8,000 account by paying 7,800 and receiving 200 discount:
- credit side: Discount 200; Cash/Bank 7,800; particulars: supplier.
- supplier account is debited with 8,000.
- Discount Received Account is credited with 200.

The discount column entries are memorandum totals for posting to the discount accounts; they do not represent cash received or paid.

## 4. Contra entries
A contra entry transfers money between cash and bank:
- cash deposited into the bank: credit Cash column, debit Bank column;
- cash withdrawn from the bank for office use: debit Cash column, credit Bank column.

Mark the entry **C** in the folio column to indicate that the corresponding entry appears on the opposite side of the same cash book. Contra entries do not change the total of cash plus bank; they transfer value between them.

## 5. Worked example
A business records:
1. Owner starts business with cash 20,000 and bank 50,000.
2. Receives 4,900 cash from a customer in full settlement of 5,000; discount allowed 100.
3. Pays a supplier 7,800 by bank in full settlement of 8,000; discount received 200.
4. Deposits 6,000 cash into bank.

Entries (discount column shown separately):

| Debit side particulars | Discount | Cash | Bank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Capital | — | 20,000 | 50,000 |
| Customer | 100 | 4,900 | — |
| Cash (contra) | — | — | 6,000 |

| Credit side particulars | Discount | Cash | Bank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Supplier | 200 | — | 7,800 |
| Bank (contra) | — | 6,000 | — |

The supplier payment and customer receipt are shown in the cash book at the actual cash/bank amount; the discount is recorded in its separate discount column. The cash deposit is a contra entry.

## 6. Guided practice
A customer owing 12,000 pays 11,400 by bank transfer in full settlement. What goes in the debit side discount and bank columns?

**Answer:** Discount allowed 600; Bank 11,400; particulars: customer.

The business pays a supplier 5,000 by cheque and receives 100 discount in full settlement. What goes in the credit side?

**Answer:** Discount received 100; Bank 4,900; particulars: supplier.

## 7. Independent assessment
**Q1 (4 marks).** Name the three amount columns on each side of a three-column cash book.
**Answer:** Discount, cash and bank.
**Marking:** 1 mark per column name and 1 for recognising they appear on both sides.

**Q2 (4 marks).** Explain the difference between discount allowed and discount received.
**Answer:** Discount allowed is granted by the business to a customer; discount received is granted to the business by a supplier.
**Marking:** 2 marks each.

**Q3 (5 marks).** A customer owing 18,000 pays 17,100 by bank in full settlement. Calculate the discount and state the cash-book entries.
**Answer:** Discount = 900. Debit side: Discount 900; Bank 17,100; particulars: customer.
**Marking:** Calculation 2; correct discount column 1; correct bank column 1; particulars 1.

**Q4 (5 marks).** The business deposits 3,500 cash into its bank. State both sides of the contra entry and explain why it is marked C.
**Answer:** Debit Bank 3,500 and credit Cash 3,500; mark C because both entries are in the same cash book and cross-refer to each other.
**Marking:** Debit bank 2; credit cash 2; C explanation 1.

**Total: 18 marks.**

## Completion check
Learners can place receipts, payments, discounts and transfers in the correct columns and explain the direction of a contra entry.
