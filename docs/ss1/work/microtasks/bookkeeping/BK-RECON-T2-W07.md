# SS1 Bookkeeping — Reconstructed Second Term, Week 7

**Project-authored reconstruction — Bookkeeping; not recovered source material**

## Topic
Trade discount and cash discount

## Learning outcomes
Learners should be able to define and distinguish trade discount and cash discount; calculate each from given figures; identify discount allowed and discount received; and explain the basic recording treatment.

## Prerequisite
Learners should understand credit transactions, day books, ledger accounts and percentage calculations.

## 1. Meaning of discount
A discount is a reduction from an amount otherwise payable. In basic bookkeeping, two common forms are trade discount and cash discount. They have different purposes and recording treatments.

### Trade discount
A trade discount is a reduction from the list price, often given to encourage bulk purchases or to reflect a trade arrangement. It is deducted before the transaction is recorded in the day book. In the basic method taught here, the net amount is recorded; a separate trade discount account is not opened.

### Cash discount
A cash discount is a reduction offered to encourage prompt payment or settlement within an agreed period. It arises when an amount is settled. From the seller's point of view, it is **discount allowed**; from the buyer's point of view, it is **discount received**.

## 2. Calculating discounts
Discount amount = Price × Rate ÷ 100.

Net amount after discount = Price − Discount amount.

Example: list price 50,000 less 8% trade discount:
- discount = 50,000 × 8 ÷ 100 = 4,000;
- net invoice amount = 50,000 − 4,000 = 46,000.

Example: a customer owing 20,000 is allowed 2% cash discount for prompt payment:
- discount allowed = 20,000 × 2 ÷ 100 = 400;
- cash received = 20,000 − 400 = 19,600.

## 3. Recording treatment
Trade discount is deducted before recording the net transaction in the relevant day book.

Cash discount is recorded when settlement occurs. In a three-column cash book:
- discount allowed is entered in the discount column on the receipts side;
- discount received is entered in the discount column on the payments side.

The discount columns are totalled, not balanced like cash and bank columns. The discount allowed total is posted to the debit of Discount Allowed Account; the discount received total is posted to the credit of Discount Received Account. Individual customer/supplier entries complete the double entry.

## 4. Worked example
A customer owes 30,000 and pays within the discount period, receiving 5% cash discount:
- discount allowed = 30,000 × 5% = 1,500;
- cash received = 30,000 − 1,500 = 28,500.

A business owes a supplier 16,000 and settles promptly, receiving 2.5% cash discount:
- discount received = 16,000 × 2.5% = 400;
- cash paid = 16,000 − 400 = 15,600.

## 5. Compare the two discounts

| Feature | Trade discount | Cash discount |
|---|---|---|
| Reason | Trade/quantity/list-price arrangement | Prompt settlement |
| When calculated | At invoice stage | At payment/receipt stage |
| Amount recorded | Net invoice amount | Discount and actual cash/bank settlement |
| Separate discount account in basic treatment | No | Yes |

## 6. Guided practice
A. Calculate the net price of goods listed at 36,000 less 10% trade discount.
B. A customer owes 12,000 and receives 5% cash discount. Calculate discount allowed and cash received.
C. A business owes 24,000 and receives 2% cash discount. Calculate discount received and cash paid.

**Answers:**
A. Discount 3,600; net price 32,400.
B. Discount allowed 600; cash received 11,400.
C. Discount received 480; cash paid 23,520.

## 7. Independent assessment
**Q1 (4 marks).** Define trade discount and cash discount.
**Answer:** Trade discount is a reduction from list price at the invoice stage; cash discount is a reduction granted for prompt payment/settlement.
**Marking:** 2 marks each.

**Q2 (4 marks).** State two differences between the discounts.
**Answer:** Trade discount is deducted before recording the invoice, while cash discount arises on settlement; trade discount is not separately recorded in the basic day-book method, while cash discount is recorded in discount accounts/columns.
**Marking:** 2 marks per clear difference.

**Q3 (5 marks).** Goods have a list price of 72,000 less 7.5% trade discount. Calculate the discount and net invoice amount.
**Answer:** Discount = 72,000 × 7.5% = 5,400; net amount = 72,000 − 5,400 = **66,600**.
**Marking:** Correct method, 2; discount, 1; net amount, 2.

**Q4 (5 marks).** A customer owing 40,000 settles within the discount period and receives 3% cash discount. Calculate discount allowed and cash received.
**Answer:** Discount = 40,000 × 3% = 1,200; cash received = 40,000 − 1,200 = **38,800**.
**Marking:** Method, 2; discount, 1; cash received, 2.

**Total: 18 marks.**
