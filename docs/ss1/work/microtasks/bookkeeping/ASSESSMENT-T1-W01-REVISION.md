# SS1 Bookkeeping — First Term Week 1 Revision

**Resource type:** Project-authored revision worksheet  
**Class:** SS1  
**Term slot:** First Term, Week 1 (revision)  
**Suggested duration:** 60 minutes  
**Total:** 40 marks  
**Provenance:** Newly authored, project-authored revision material. It is not recovered from, or a correction to, the original DOCX.

## Learning coverage

Revise the meaning and purpose of bookkeeping; cash and bank accounts; single- and double-column cash books; debit and credit entries; contra entries; double entry; ledger posting and balancing.

## Section A — Objective questions (10 × 1 = 10 marks)

Choose the best answer.

1. Bookkeeping is mainly concerned with:
   A. advertising goods  B. systematic recording of financial transactions  C. recruiting workers  D. fixing selling prices
2. The cash book records:
   A. only credit sales  B. cash and bank receipts and payments  C. only assets  D. only expenses
3. An amount received in cash is entered on the:
   A. debit side of the cash book  B. credit side  C. balance sheet only  D. purchases day book
4. A payment made by cheque is normally entered in the:
   A. cash column only  B. bank column on the credit side  C. bank column on the debit side  D. sales day book
5. A contra entry affects:
   A. two expense accounts  B. cash and bank columns of the same cash book  C. only the sales account  D. only the capital account
6. The letter commonly used in the folio column for a contra entry is:
   A. S  B. C  C. P  D. J
7. The double-entry principle requires every transaction to have:
   A. one entry  B. two equal aspects  C. three accounts  D. no source document
8. When an asset increases, the asset account is generally:
   A. debited  B. credited  C. closed  D. omitted
9. Posting means transferring entries from a book of original entry to:
   A. a receipt  B. ledger accounts  C. an invoice only  D. a bank statement
10. A balance carried down is commonly abbreviated:
    A. b/d  B. c/d  C. Dr  D. Cr

## Section B — Short answers (5 × 2 = 10 marks)

11. State two reasons why a business keeps proper bookkeeping records.
12. Distinguish between the cash column and bank column of a double-column cash book.
13. What is a contra entry? Give one example.
14. State the debit and credit rule for assets.
15. Explain the difference between balancing a cash book and posting to a ledger.

## Section C — Applied questions (20 marks)

16. **Cash book (8 marks).** On 1 March, a trader had cash ₦18,000 and bank ₦42,000. During the month:
    - Mar 3: cash sales ₦12,000
    - Mar 5: paid rent in cash ₦5,000
    - Mar 8: deposited ₦10,000 cash into the bank
    - Mar 12: received a cheque from a customer ₦15,000 and banked it immediately
    - Mar 16: paid a supplier by cheque ₦9,000

    Prepare a two-column cash book extract and calculate the closing cash and bank balances.

17. **Double entry (6 marks).** State the two accounts affected and the debit/credit entries for:
    a. Owner introduced ₦50,000 cash as capital.
    b. Bought office furniture for cash ₦12,000.
    c. Paid electricity by bank transfer ₦4,000.

18. **Ledger posting (6 marks).** Explain the posting for transaction (b) in Question 17. Name the two ledger accounts and state which side receives each entry.

## Answer key and marking guide

**Section A:** 1 B; 2 B; 3 A; 4 B; 5 B; 6 B; 7 B; 8 A; 9 B; 10 B. Award 1 mark each.

**Section B (2 marks each):**
11. Any two: know amounts received/paid; determine profit or loss; monitor cash/bank; support decisions; provide evidence/accountability (1 each).
12. Cash column records physical cash; bank column records money held in or paid through the bank (1 each).
13. A transfer between cash and bank recorded on both sides of the same cash book; e.g. cash deposited into bank (1 each).
14. Increase in an asset is debited; decrease is credited (1 each).
15. Balancing totals the two sides and finds the difference; posting transfers entries to the relevant ledger accounts (1 each).

**Question 16 (8 marks):**
- Correct debit entries: opening cash 18,000; opening bank 42,000; cash sales 12,000; cheque received/banked 15,000; contra cash-to-bank 10,000 in bank debit column (3 marks).
- Correct credit entries: rent cash 5,000; contra cash-to-bank 10,000 in cash credit column; supplier cheque 9,000 (3 marks).
- Closing cash = 18,000 + 12,000 − 5,000 − 10,000 = **₦15,000** (1 mark).
- Closing bank = 42,000 + 10,000 + 15,000 − 9,000 = **₦58,000** (1 mark).

**Question 17 (6 marks):** 2 marks each.
a. Dr Cash ₦50,000; Cr Capital ₦50,000.
b. Dr Furniture ₦12,000; Cr Cash ₦12,000.
c. Dr Electricity Expense ₦4,000; Cr Bank ₦4,000.

**Question 18 (6 marks):** Furniture account is debited ₦12,000 (3); Cash account is credited ₦12,000 (3).
