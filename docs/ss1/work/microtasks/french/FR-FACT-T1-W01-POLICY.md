## Executive finding

The dated official records confirm that French has been included in Nigeria's primary, junior-secondary and senior-secondary subject framework, and that the Federal Ministry of Education has supported French-language programmes and the Nigeria French Language Village at Ajara-Badagry. They do **not** support the source's superlative claim that French is the most important foreign language after English, do not establish that French is actually taught in every school or institution, and do not prove the source's claim that the language was not imposed by any power.

The 2014 National Policy on Education lists French as a subject in Primary Classes 4–6 and the Junior Secondary curriculum, and as one of the Senior Secondary Humanities offerings. It does not list French among the compulsory cross-cutting Senior Secondary subjects. A targeted search of the complete 2014 policy found no wording that French is the "second official language". The 2019 FME Integrated Service Charter nevertheless describes a National Education Policy directive concerning that status and says the Village was working on its implementation through teacher training. The NFLV's 2016 brochure says French was declared the second official language in 1997. Those documents establish what the Ministry and the Village said at those dates; they do not establish present legal status or full implementation.

## Dated primary-source register

### 1. Federal Ministry of Education / Nigerian Educational Research and Development Council — National Policy on Education, 6th edition (first print 2013; reprint 2014)

URL: https://education.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/NATIONAL-POLICY-ON-EDUCATION.pdf

- Title/edition: PDF lines 0–16; the document identifies the 6th edition, first print 2013, reprint 2014, published by NERDC.
- Primary curriculum: Section 2, paragraph 20(e), printed pp. 10–11; PDF lines 556–581. French is listed in Primary Classes 4–6 and is to be taught from the fourth year.
- Junior Secondary curriculum: paragraph 23, printed pp. 12–13; PDF lines 596–617. French Language appears in the JSS curriculum list.
- Senior Secondary curriculum: paragraph 38.2.3, printed p. 20; PDF lines 739–748. French appears among Humanities subject offerings. Paragraph 38.1 separately lists the compulsory cross-cutting subjects; French is not among those four.
- Tertiary education: paragraph 80, printed p. 39; PDF lines 1227–1235. The definition of tertiary education names the Nigeria French Language Village as an inter-university centre and lists universities and Colleges of Education.
- Targeted search of the full text for "second official" returned no match. This is a finding about this edition's wording, not proof that an earlier declaration never occurred.
- Retrieved 2026-11-07.

## Contradictions, limitations and excluded claims

1. **"Most important foreign language after English":** no dated primary source located that ranks French this way. Excluded from teaching.
2. **"French is compulsory throughout secondary education":** the 2014 NPE lists French in the JSS curriculum and as an SS Humanities offering, while its separate compulsory cross-cutting SS list does not include French. Do not generalise this to a universal compulsory requirement at every level.
3. **"French is taught in universities and Colleges of Education":** the FME Charter and NFLV brochure document compulsory immersion programmes for undergraduates of French and College of Education students at NFLV. They do not establish that every university or College of Education offers French.
4. **"French is currently Nigeria's second official language":** the FME 2019 Charter and NFLV 2016 brochure refer to the historical policy directive/declaration. The FME/NERDC 2014 NPE contains no "second official" wording. No current gazetted instrument or current FME statement establishing present legal status was located. Do not teach this as an unqualified current legal fact.
5. **"Not imposed by any power" and the full economic, commercial, political and cultural motives:** no primary source located that establishes these assertions. The original Abacha announcement/speech and a gazetted instrument were not located. Leave unresolved.
6. A secondary transcription of the 2004 NPE section 10(b) exists, but the original 2004 scan could not be retrieved and inspected directly during this task; it is not treated here as primary-verified evidence.
