# SS1 French — original visual/object inspection record

Inspection method: the hash-verified original DOCX members were extracted from the registered source archive. Their OOXML drawing/picture/OMML objects were enumerated directly, and the relevant pages were rendered for visual inspection. A PDF conversion was used as a view of the original page, not as the sole source of object identity.

## First-Term DOCX

- **Country/capital separators and groupings:** 36 drawing/picture objects at direct-body children 23, 30–46, 52–66 and 71–73. The rendered pages show arrow lines connecting each country to its capital and, in the final group, each country to its demonym. The arrows add layout/grouping only; they contain no independent text or data. The country/capital and nationality replacements present the same relationships as labelled text/table pairs. No image asset is required.
- **Être/avoir auxiliary separators:** drawing/picture objects at children 149–159, 187 and 189–196. The rendered tables use arrows/separators to align French verb forms with English glosses. The replacement teaches the conjugation tables directly. No asset is required.
- **Accent/diacritic drawings:** objects at children 233, 243, 249–256 and 263. Rendered examples show the accent marks and example words; the replacement uses the correct Unicode characters (é, è, â/ê/î/ô/û, ç, ë/ï) rather than blank spaces or detached shapes. No image asset is required.
- **Punctuation object:** child 277, rendered on the punctuation page. It labels punctuation signs (full stop, comma, question/exclamation marks and related signs); the replacement lists the marks as text and gives a sentence example. No image asset is required.
- **Routine-section object:** child 347, rendered as a brace grouping the reflexive routine verbs. The replacement lists and conjugates the pronominal verbs directly. No image asset is required.

The isolated final text fragment “a silent h.” is not an image. It is accounted for in the accents lesson with the distinction between h muet and h aspiré, including l’homme/l’hôtel versus le héros/le haricot.

## Second-Term DOCX

Three Office Math (OMML) objects were inspected in the original DOCX:

- Direct-body child 65 contains the glyph `ɲ` in the example “Agnès, agneau”. The replacement retains /ɲ/ and adds standard examples.
- Direct-body child 66 contains the glyph `Ↄ` in “peigne, camping”. This glyph is not a standard IPA transcription for the two examples as a group. The replacement separates the sounds: Larousse gives peigne as /pɛɲ/ and camping as /kɑ̃piŋ/. This avoids teaching the two distinct final sounds as one.
- Direct-body child 76 contains `ḝ` in the simple-vowel list between a, i, o and u. The surrounding list clearly calls for the five basic vowel letters; the replacement teaches a, e, i, o and u and does not repeat the corrupted glyph as a French vowel.

These are text/phonetic-symbol corrections, not image assets. The source’s phonetic examples and corrected scope are included in `SUP-SS1-FRENCH-SOUNDS-T2`.

No embedded DrawingML pictures, VML pictures or OMML objects were found in the Third-Term source. The scheme and lesson content are text. No deployable visual asset is required from this source.
