# First Term Week 2 — Anglophone-country scope research

**Prepared:** 11 November 2026  
**Source row:** FRENCH-BODY-002, children 51–66  
**Question:** What does the source mean by “Anglophone countries”, and can its 15-country list be taught as a list of countries where English is an official language?

## Finding

The source does not define Anglophone (for example, legal official language, official working language, language of instruction, widespread second-language use, or a historical/educational grouping). Those categories are not interchangeable.

The source's 15-country list cannot currently be presented as a verified list of countries where English is an official language. Government/constitutional evidence identifies other official languages for several entries: Egypt's Constitution, Article 2, identifies Arabic; Angola's Constitution, Article 19, identifies Portuguese; Libya's 2011 Constitutional Declaration, Article 1, identifies Arabic. Ethiopia's Constitution, Article 5, gives all Ethiopian languages equal state recognition and identifies Amharic as the federal government's working language.

The existing SUP-SS1-FRENCH-ANGLOPHONE-T1 lesson deliberately teaches only three bounded examples (Namibia, Kenya and South Africa). It does not verify the other twelve rows or complete the source's exhaustive “write the Anglophone countries” exercise. Accordingly, FRENCH-T1-W02-OBJ-2 and correction SS1-02-french-COR-002 remain PARTIAL.

## Required completion path

1. The teacher/owner must define what Anglophone means in this lesson: legal official language, official working language, or broader English use.
2. If the intended meaning is legal official language, complete primary-source verification for all 15 entries, including the current constitutional status of Sudan and Somalia, and correct the source's list where necessary.
3. If the intended meaning is broader English use, define a teachable criterion (for example, English used in government/education) and source every classification using that criterion.
4. Replace the current three-example activity with a complete, keyed exercise matching the approved criterion, or approve a narrower selected-examples objective.
5. Keep the objective PARTIAL until the above is done and the teacher approves the revised scope.

## Editorial safeguard

Do not describe a country as Anglophone solely because English is taught, used by some officials, used in higher education, or appears in a historical document. Record the actual legal or social criterion. The 15-country source list is preserved as source evidence, not endorsed as a current legal-language classification.
