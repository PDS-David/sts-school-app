## Construction inventory

1. **Triangle equal in area to a polygon, reflex/interior angle below 180°** — Example 1, children 1217–1231; figure F01 at 1217. Construct polygon ABCDE; extend AB in both directions; join DA and DB; through E draw a line parallel to DA to meet BA produced at F; through C draw a line parallel to DB to meet the other extension at G; join DF and DG; FGD is the required triangle. Preserve the source's less-than-180° condition (the superscript-zero marker is a degree symbol: 180°).
2. **Triangle equal in area to a polygon with an interior angle above 180°** — Example 2, children 1233–1244; figure F02 at 1233. Construct ABCDE; extend AB; join DA and DB; through E draw a line parallel to DA to meet AB at F; through C draw a line parallel to DB to meet AB produced at G; join DF and DG; FGD is the required triangle. The source text has an apparent directional ambiguity in its use of “AB” versus “AB produced”; preserve the original construction diagram and do not silently rewrite it.
3. **Triangle equal in area to a regular polygon** — Example 3, children 1246–1257; figure F03 at 1246. Construct regular polygon ABCDEF; draw diagonals to meet at O; extend base AB in both directions to P and Q so PQ equals the polygon perimeter (sum of all side lengths); join OP and OQ; OPQ is the required triangle.
4. **Triangle equal in area to a parallelogram** — Example 4, children 1259–1271; figure F04 at 1259. Construct parallelogram ABCD; join C to A; produce BA; through D draw a line parallel to CA to meet BA produced at E; join CE; CEB is the required triangle.
5. **Parallelogram equal in area to a triangle** — Example 5, children 1274–1287; figure F05 at 1276. Construct triangle ABC; drop perpendicular CD from apex C to base AB; bisect CD at E; extend the bisector to G; through B draw a line parallel to AG to meet the bisector at F; ABFG is the required parallelogram.
6. **Equal-area triangle on a different base** — Example 6, children 1293–1305; figure F06 at 1295. Construct ABC; extend AB by the required new base length AD; join C to D; through B draw a line parallel to DC to meet AC at E; join ED; ADE is the required triangle.
7. **Triangle from a stated area** — Example 7, children 1307–1317; figure F07 at 1309. Stated area is 4½ cm². Construct rectangle ABCD of area 3 cm × 1½ cm = 4½ cm²; produce BC and set CE equal to BC; join E to A; ABE is the required triangle. The superscript/subscript 1/2 is a fraction, not degree notation.
8. **Equal-area rectangle with a different side** — Example 8, children 1323–1336; figure F08 at 1325. Construct rectangle ABCD; mark BE on AB as the required new side; join EC; produce BC; through A draw a line parallel to EC meeting BC produced at F; FB is the other side; complete rectangle EBFG.
9. **Square equal in area to a rectangle** — Example 9, children 1338–1352; figure F09 at 1339. Construct rectangle ABCD; produce AB; with B as centre and radius BC cut the produced line at E; draw a semicircle on AE meeting BC produced at F; BF is the square side; with B as centre and radius BF locate H on BA; with H and F as centres and the same radius locate G; HBFG is the required square.
10. **Rectangle equal in area to a triangle** — Example 10, children 1356–1366; figure F10 at 1358. Construct ABC; drop perpendicular CD from C to AB; bisect CD at E; through E draw a line parallel to AB; erect perpendiculars at A and B to meet that parallel at F and G; ABGF is the required rectangle.
11. **Square equal in area to a parallelogram** — Example 11, children 1369–1383; figure F11 at 1371. Construct parallelogram ABCD; produce DA; erect perpendicular AE at A to meet CB at F; with A as centre and radius AF cut DA produced at G; construct semicircle on DG meeting AE at H; AH is the required square side; with A as centre and radius AH locate K; with H and K as centres and radius AH locate J; AKJH is the required square.
12. **Divide a triangle into four equal-area strips** — Example 12, children 1389–1406; figure F12 at 1391. Construct triangle ABC; construct a semicircle on AC; divide AC into four equal parts; erect perpendiculars to AC from the division points to meet the semicircle at F, E and D; with C as centre and radii CD, CE and CF transfer arcs to AC at D1, E1 and F1; through these points draw lines parallel to AB. The source says three or two divisions give three or two equal areas respectively.
13. **Divide a triangle into two equal areas by a perpendicular-based construction** — Example 13, children 1408–1419; figure F13 at 1409. Construct ABC; drop perpendicular CD from C to AB; construct semicircle on DB; bisect AB and let the bisector meet the semicircle at E; with B as centre and radius BE cut AB at F; through F draw a line parallel to DC to meet CB at G; FG divides the triangle into two equal areas.
14. **Divide a triangle into two equal areas from a given point on one side** — Example 14, children 1423–1435; figure F14 at 1425. Construct ABC with given point P; join P to C; bisect AB at D; through D draw a line parallel to PC meeting CB at E; join EP; EP is the dividing line.
15. **Circle equal in area to the sum of two circles** — Example 15, children 1437–1448; figure F15 at 1439. Draw perpendicular lengths AB and AC equal to the diameters of the two given circles; join BC; bisect BC at P; with P as centre and radius PA (or PB or PC) draw the required circle. This is the right-triangle/diameter construction; preserve the original figure's exact point labels.
16. **Square equal in area to the sum of two squares** — Example 16, children 1454–1464; figure F16 at 1455. Draw AB equal to the side of one square; at A draw AC perpendicular to AB and equal to the side of the other square; join BC; construct a square on BC.
17. **Square equal in area to the difference of two squares** — Example 17, children 1466–1476; figure F17 at 1468. Draw AB equal to the side of the smaller square; erect perpendicular at B; with A as centre and radius equal to the side of the larger square cut that perpendicular at C; construct a square on BC.
18. **Square twice the area of a given square** — Example 18, children 1482–1492; figure F18 at 1483. Draw square ABCD; draw diagonal BD; use BD as the side of square BDEF. The new square has twice the area of ABCD.
