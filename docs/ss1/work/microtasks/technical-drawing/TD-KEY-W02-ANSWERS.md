## Verified construction and numerical keys

1. **Escribed circle on triangle 55, 60, 50 mm.** Construct the SSS triangle by intersecting arcs. Semiperimeter s=82.5 mm; Heron area Δ≈1,288.03 mm². Exradii are Δ/(s−55)=46.84 mm, Δ/(s−60)=57.25 mm and Δ/(s−50)=39.63 mm. For the selected excentre, bisect the two exterior angles, drop a perpendicular to the corresponding side line and draw the circle.
2. **Escribed circles on a regular 50 mm-side hexagon.** Construct the regular hexagon, extend the relevant side lines, bisect adjacent exterior angles to locate each excentre, and use the perpendicular distance to a side line as radius. A regular hexagon has six congruent exscribed-circle configurations by symmetry; the exact circle requested should be identified from the source figure.
3. **Circle through A and tangent to line at R.** The centre lies on the perpendicular to the tangent at R and on the perpendicular bisector of chord AR. Their intersection is O; draw radius OR. This verifies OA=OR and OR ⟂ tangent.
4. **Circle through two points and tangent to a line.** The centre lies on the perpendicular bisector of the chord joining the two given points and on the perpendicular to the tangent at its contact point. The source's semicircle construction is a geometric method to locate the intersection; the final circle is centred at O with radius to either given point/contact.
5. **Circle diameter 35 mm.** Circumference = πd = 35π ≈ **109.96 mm**.

## Additional general-revision constructions

For the source's general-revision Q1 (equilateral triangle side 60 mm with three equal circles, two circles touching each side), draw the equilateral triangle and its three 60° angle bisectors. Each circle centre lies on a vertex angle bisector; its perpendicular distance to either adjacent side is its radius. For equal circles to touch pairwise along the three sides, adjacent centres are separated by 2r. With triangle side a=60 mm, the centre spacing along the base is a−2√3r, so a−2√3r=2r and r=a/(2+2√3)=15(√3−1)≈**10.98 mm**. Construct one circle at each vertex with that radius; check all three pairwise tangencies.

For the source's general-revision Q2, draw a tangent from external point S to the given circle with centre O: join SO; bisect SO at M; draw the auxiliary circle centred at M with radius MS (the circle on diameter SO); its intersection T with the given circle gives ST as a tangent because angle STO is 90°. The two intersection points give the two possible tangents.

- Divide the 90° central angle into four equal 22.5° parts by angle bisection.
- Each chord is 2×40×sin(11.25°)≈15.61 mm.

## Safe scoring and replacement wording for ambiguous source MCQs

### Weekend Q2 — two valid radii

The original figure shows O as the centre and B and Q as two contact points. OB and OQ are both radii, so both options B and C are mathematically correct. For any delivery that retains the source options, **accept either B or C for full credit**; do not force one answer.

### Weekend Q4 — two invalid method steps

The original single-answer wording asks which is not a procedure for constructing an escribed circle. Both option B (“bisect two sides”) and option C (“draw a line from the determined centre parallel to one side”) are not required/valid steps; the perpendicular from the excentre to a side line is required to obtain the radius. **Accept either B or C for full credit** if the original options are retained.

Original replacement wording (single-answer): “Which construction gives the radius of an escribed circle?” Correct answer: “Draw a perpendicular from the excentre to the side line (or its extension) that the circle touches.”
