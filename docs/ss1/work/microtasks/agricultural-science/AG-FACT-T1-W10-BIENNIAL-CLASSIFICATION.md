## Correct life-cycle definition
- **Annual:** completes its life cycle (germination, vegetative growth, flowering, seed production and death) within one growing season or year.
- **Biennial:** requires two growing seasons to complete its biological life cycle. It commonly grows vegetatively and stores reserves in the first season, then flowers, sets seed and dies in the second.
- **Perennial:** lives for more than two years and may survive to produce over multiple seasons.
- Important distinction: a perennial species may be **cultivated as an annual crop** and harvested within one production cycle. Harvest duration does not automatically change the plant's biological life span.
- Source: [Oregon State University Extension — Know the life cycles of plants: annuals, biennials and perennials](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9899-know-life-cycles-plants-annuals-biennials-perennials), sections “Annuals”, “Biennials” and “Perennials”.

## Assessment of Weekend Assignment Q2

The question's answer depends on what “annual crop” means:

### If it means crop-production practice
- Cotton (*Gossypium hirsutum*) may be biologically annual or perennial but is usually cultivated as an annual.
- Cowpea (*Vigna unguiculata*) is annual.
- Yam species (*Dioscorea* spp.) are perennial vines, but CGIAR Genebanks says they are mainly treated as annuals in cultivation.
- Pepper (*Capsicum annuum*) is annual in FAO EcoCrop and is commonly cultivated as an annual, although some cultivars can persist as short-lived perennials in warm climates.
- On this production-cycle interpretation, all four options can be grown/managed as annual crops. There is no single “except”.

### If it means strict biological life span
- Cotton is variable by species/cultivar and can be perennial.
- Cowpea is annual.
- Yam species are perennial vines, though cultivated as annuals.
- Capsicum pepper is annual or short-lived perennial depending on species and environment; black pepper is perennial.
- The options therefore still do not give one stable exception. “Pepper” is not species-specific, and yam/cotton also have perennial biological forms.

### Sources for the MCQ options
- [FAO EcoCrop — Cotton (*Gossypium hirsutum*)](https://ecocrop.apps.fao.org/ecocrop/srv/en/dataSheet?id=1160): annual or perennial; usually cultivated as annual.
- [FAO EcoCrop — Cowpea (*Vigna unguiculata*)](https://ecocrop.apps.fao.org/ecocrop/srv/en/cropView?id=10835): annual.
- [CGIAR Genebanks — Yam](https://genebanks.cgiar.org/resources/crops/yam/): yams are annual or perennial vines; for cultivation they are mainly treated as annuals.
- [FAO EcoCrop — *Capsicum annuum*](https://ecocrop.apps.fao.org/ecocrop/srv/en/dataSheet?id=618): annual.
- [University of Florida IFAS — *Capsicum annuum*](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/fp105): tender perennial in warmer climates, grown as a warm-season annual in frost-prone zones.

## Finding
1. The definition “within as year” is wrong. Biennials require **two growing seasons** to complete their biological life cycle.
2. Of the five examples in the source's biennial list, **onion and carrot** are true biennials. Cassava and ginger are perennial species commonly cultivated as annual crops; Capsicum pepper is generally annual or short-lived perennial, not biennial.
3. The Weekend Assignment's intended key may be **D. pepper**, because the source labels pepper as biennial. That classification is not supported for the common Capsicum pepper.
4. The MCQ is not reliable as written. If annual means grown/harvested within one production cycle, cotton, cowpea, yam and Capsicum pepper may all be annual crops. If it means biological lifespan, the options contain species and cultivar variation and do not produce one unique exception.
