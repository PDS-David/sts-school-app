Week 5 Assessment Supplement: Technology in Agriculture

This supplement answers the Week 5 evaluation question asking for a definition of technology. It is additional teaching support, not a replacement for the original lesson.

## 1. Meaning of Technology

Technology is the practical application of knowledge to solve problems or carry out useful tasks. In agriculture, technology is the combination of tools, equipment, materials, methods and skills that farmers use to produce crops and livestock, protect resources, store produce and improve farm work.

Agricultural technology is not limited to machines. It includes both physical items and the knowledge needed to use them well.

## 2. Science and Technology

- **Science** is the systematic study of the natural world. It helps people discover and explain how things work.
- **Technology** applies knowledge from science and other experience to practical tasks. It uses tools, equipment, materials and methods to solve problems.

For example, science helps explain what nutrients plants need. Agricultural technology includes soil-testing tools, improved planting materials and farm practices that help farmers use that knowledge. Science helps explain how plants inherit traits; technology includes improved crop varieties developed through breeding.

## 3. Two Broad Types of Agricultural Technology

1. **Material technology:** physical products or materials used in farming. Examples include tools, tractors, irrigation equipment, improved seeds or plant varieties, improved animal breeds, vaccines and storage equipment.
2. **Knowledge-based technology:** information, skills and practices farmers use to manage production. Examples include crop and livestock management, crop rotation, integrated pest management, and soil and water conservation practices.

The two types work together. A farmer may have an irrigation pump (material technology), but also needs knowledge about when and how to manage water (knowledge-based technology).

## 4. Examples of Agricultural Technology

- Improved seeds and animal breeds: provide useful crop or livestock characteristics.
- Farm machinery and tools: help carry out field operations and transport materials.
- Irrigation systems: help supply water to crops when rainfall is insufficient.
- Storage structures such as silos: help protect harvested produce before use or sale.
- Crop rotation and soil-conservation practices: apply knowledge to maintain productive soil and manage crops.
- Weather, market and advisory information delivered by radio or mobile phone: helps farmers make informed decisions.

**Question:** Distinguish science from technology and give one agricultural example of each.

**Answer:** Science is the systematic study of the natural world and helps explain how things work. Technology applies knowledge to practical tasks. Science explains plant inheritance; technology includes improved crop varieties developed through breeding.
