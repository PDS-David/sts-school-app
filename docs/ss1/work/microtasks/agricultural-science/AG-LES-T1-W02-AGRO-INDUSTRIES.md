Week 2 Cross-Week Revision Supplement: Agro-Based Industries, Agro-Allied Industries and Raw Materials

This is a cross-week revision supplement for the Week 2 questions about agro-based industries and the raw materials they use. It draws on the Week 1 teaching about agro-allied industries and the Week 6 examples of industries and raw materials. It is not a new Week 2 scheme objective or a replacement for the original source.

## 1. Agro-Based Industries

An agro-based industry processes raw materials obtained from agriculture, forestry or fisheries into finished or partly finished products. The raw material may come from crops, animals, trees or fish.

Examples and raw materials:
1. Textile or cotton-ginnery industry: cotton.
2. Beverage industry: cocoa, tea, coffee or kola nuts.
3. Soap industry: oil seeds and vegetable oils.
4. Paper industry: pulpwood or other suitable wood.
5. Sugar industry: sugarcane.
6. Flour mill: cereal grains such as wheat, maize, millet or sorghum.
7. Brewery: cereal grains such as barley, maize or sorghum.
8. Fruit-canning or juice industry: fruits such as mango, orange or pineapple.
9. Tyre and rubber-products industry: natural rubber latex.
10. Oil mill: oil seeds such as groundnut, soybean or palm kernels.

## 2. Agro-Allied Industries

In the Week 1 source, agro-allied industries are described as industries that supply goods or services needed for farming. They support agricultural production rather than processing harvested farm produce as their main activity.

Examples include:
- Fertilizer manufacturers and blenders, which supply plant nutrients.
- Pesticide and herbicide manufacturers, which supply crop-protection products.
- Agricultural machinery and equipment manufacturers or suppliers, which provide tractors, planters, pumps and other equipment.
- Animal-feed manufacturers, which supply feed for livestock and poultry.
- Seed companies, which supply planting materials.
- Irrigation-equipment and pump suppliers, which provide equipment for moving water to farms.
- Veterinary-drug and animal-health service providers, which support livestock health.

## 3. The Difference

- Agro-based industries process agricultural raw materials into products. Examples are a cotton textile mill, a paper mill or a sugar factory.
- Agro-allied industries supply inputs, equipment or services that help farmers produce crops and livestock. Examples are a fertilizer factory, a farm-machinery manufacturer or an animal-feed factory.

The terms are not used consistently in every source. Week 1 block 105 uses agro-allied for farm-input suppliers, while Week 6 block 482 writes “agro-allied/agro-based” together for industries using agricultural raw materials. FAO describes agro-industries broadly as including both upstream input/equipment supply and downstream processing. For clarity in this supplement, “agro-based” means the processing industries, while “agro-allied” means the input-supply/support industries, following the Week 1 distinction. The Week 6 wording is recorded as a source inconsistency rather than repeated as if the terms were identical.

## 4. How Agriculture and Industry Support Each Other

Agriculture supplies raw materials to agro-based processors. Agro-allied suppliers provide farmers with inputs and equipment. Industries also provide a market for farm produce, while farms provide food and raw materials for industrial workers and factories.

## Terminology decision

This supplement distinguishes:
- **Agro-based:** downstream processing of agricultural raw materials (e.g. cotton to textiles, pulpwood to paper, sugarcane to sugar).
- **Agro-allied:** upstream input suppliers and support services for agricultural production (e.g. fertilizer, pesticides, machinery, feeds, seed and irrigation suppliers).

This is a clear working distinction for the Week 2 assessment. It follows Week 1 block 105 and is consistent with FAO's distinction between input-supply businesses and food-processing agribusinesses. The source's Week 6 block 482 uses the terms together, and broader references sometimes use agro-industry as an umbrella for both upstream and downstream activity. The supplement records that inconsistency rather than pretending the source is consistent.
