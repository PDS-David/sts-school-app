## Five Nigeria-Relevant Organisations

1. **Sasakawa Africa Association (SAA-Nigeria):** Works with Nigerian agricultural extension services, extension workers and smallholder farmers. Its activities include training, improved crop practices, demonstration plots, climate-resilient farming, post-harvest support and stronger links to markets.
2. **Heifer International Nigeria:** Supports smallholder farmers, women and young people with skills, technical guidance, access to resources, finance, insurance and market connections. Its Nigerian work includes dairy, fruits and vegetables, poultry, rice and small-ruminant value chains.
3. **TechnoServe Nigeria:** Supports agricultural value chains including maize, rice, cashew, tomato, cassava, soy and poultry. Its work aims to improve smallholder productivity and income, access to farm inputs and finance, and connections to markets.
4. **Catholic Relief Services (CRS) Nigeria:** Works in agriculture, livelihoods and resilience. It supports households, communities and farmer groups to increase agricultural production and diversify incomes; its Nigeria work has also supported cassava seed entrepreneurs and planting-material markets.
5. **Women Farmers Advancement Network (WOFAN):** A Nigerian non-profit organisation that supports smallholder farmers, particularly women and young people. Its work includes agricultural community projects, training of extension workers, farmer empowerment and climate-related information and resilience.

## How NGOs Contribute to Agriculture

The Week 4 source identifies six contribution areas:
- Research: finding solutions to agricultural problems and supporting improved crops or livestock.
- Finance: supporting agricultural projects.
- Inputs: helping farmers obtain items such as fertilizers, pesticides and other farm inputs.
- Extension services: improving farmers' knowledge through information, training and advice.
- Rural development: supporting basic facilities and services in rural communities.
- Irrigation and drainage: helping make water available or manage excess water for agricultural production.

Different organisations specialise in different areas. The five examples above are included because their own records show agricultural work in Nigeria; they should not be treated as having identical programmes.
