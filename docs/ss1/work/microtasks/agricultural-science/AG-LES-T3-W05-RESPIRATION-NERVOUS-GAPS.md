## Learning objectives

1. Explain how inhalation and exhalation occur in a mammal.
2. Trace air movement and gas exchange in a named farm animal.
3. Distinguish breathing, gas exchange and cellular respiration.

## 1. Breathing and respiration are related but different

- **Breathing (pulmonary ventilation)** is the movement of air into and out of the lungs.
- **Gas exchange** is the movement of oxygen and carbon dioxide between the air in the lungs and the blood, and between blood and body tissues.
- **Cellular respiration** is the process by which cells release usable energy from nutrients. In aerobic respiration, cells use oxygen and produce carbon dioxide and water.

Breathing brings oxygen into the lungs and removes carbon dioxide. It supports cellular respiration, but breathing itself is not the same process as cellular respiration.

## 2. Mechanics of inhalation and exhalation in a mammal

A cow, goat, sheep and pig are mammals. Their lungs are ventilated by changes in the volume of the chest cavity and the pressure inside the lungs.

### A. Inhalation (inspiration)

1. The diaphragm contracts and moves downwards, becoming flatter.
2. The external intercostal muscles between the ribs contract and move the ribs upwards and outwards.
3. The volume of the thoracic (chest) cavity increases.
4. The lungs expand with the chest wall, so pressure inside the alveoli falls below atmospheric pressure.
5. Air moves from the atmosphere through the air passages into the lungs because it moves down a pressure gradient.

**Remember:** during inhalation, chest-cavity volume increases and pressure inside the lungs decreases, so air enters.

### B. Exhalation (expiration) during quiet breathing

1. The diaphragm relaxes and returns towards its dome-shaped position.
2. The external intercostal muscles relax and the ribs return towards their resting position.
3. Elastic recoil of the lungs and chest wall reduces the volume of the thoracic cavity.
4. Pressure inside the lungs rises above atmospheric pressure.
5. Air flows out of the lungs.

Quiet exhalation is mainly passive. During forced exhalation, such as vigorous exercise or coughing, additional muscles including the internal intercostal and abdominal muscles can help push air out.

### Comparison

| Inhalation | Exhalation during quiet breathing |
|---|---|
| Diaphragm contracts and flattens | Diaphragm relaxes and becomes dome-shaped |
| External intercostal muscles contract | External intercostal muscles relax |
| Ribs move up and out | Ribs return down and in |
| Thoracic volume increases | Thoracic volume decreases |
| Lung pressure falls below atmospheric pressure | Lung pressure rises above atmospheric pressure |
| Air enters the lungs | Air leaves the lungs |

## 3. Respiration in a named farm animal: the cow

The cow is a useful example of a farm mammal with lungs. The respiratory process includes air movement, gas exchange, transport of gases in the blood and cellular respiration.

### A. Route taken by inhaled air

Air follows this general route:

**Nostrils → nasal passages → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli**

- The nostrils and nasal passages admit, warm, moisten and filter inhaled air.
- The pharynx and larynx form the passage towards the trachea.
- The trachea carries air towards the chest and divides into two main bronchi.
- The bronchi branch into smaller bronchioles within the lungs.
- The bronchioles lead to microscopic air spaces called alveoli.

### B. Gas exchange in the lungs

Alveoli have very thin walls and are surrounded by tiny blood capillaries.

1. Oxygen concentration is higher in the air inside the alveoli than in the blood arriving at the lung capillaries.
2. Oxygen diffuses across the thin alveolar-capillary surface into the blood.
3. Much of the oxygen binds to haemoglobin inside red blood cells.
4. Carbon dioxide carried by the blood diffuses from the capillaries into the alveoli.
5. Carbon dioxide leaves the lungs when the cow exhales.

### C. Transport of gases and cellular respiration

1. Blood carries oxygen from the lungs to body tissues.
2. Oxygen moves from the blood into body cells.
3. Cells use oxygen during aerobic cellular respiration to release energy from glucose.
4. Carbon dioxide and water are produced. The carbon dioxide enters the blood and is transported back to the lungs.
5. The cow exhales the carbon dioxide.

The simplified word equation for aerobic cellular respiration is:

**Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy (ATP)**

This is a new, correctly written word equation for this supplement. It is not a replacement or silent correction of the unclear equations printed in the original source.

### D. Summary flow

**Air enters the cow's lungs → oxygen diffuses into blood → blood carries oxygen to cells → cells release energy using oxygen → carbon dioxide returns in blood → carbon dioxide diffuses into alveoli → cow exhales.**

## 4. The neuron: structural and functional unit of nervous tissue

The nervous system receives information, processes it and coordinates responses. Nervous tissue contains two broad cell groups:

- **Neurons:** specialised cells that receive, process and transmit information using electrical signals and chemical communication at synapses.
- **Glial cells (neuroglia):** supporting cells that protect, nourish, insulate and help maintain the environment around neurons.

A **neuron** is the basic structural and functional unit of nervous tissue because it is the specialised cell that carries nerve impulses and communicates with other cells.

### Main parts of a typical neuron

| Part | Description and function |
|---|---|
| Cell body (soma) | Contains the nucleus and most of the cell's organelles; maintains the neuron and integrates incoming signals. |
| Dendrites | Usually branched extensions that receive signals from receptors or other neurons and carry them towards the cell body. |
| Axon | A usually single long extension that carries an electrical impulse away from the cell body towards target cells. |
| Myelin sheath | A fatty insulating covering formed by glial cells around many axons; it helps nerve impulses travel faster. |
| Nodes of Ranvier | Gaps between myelin segments that help rapid conduction along a myelinated axon. |
| Axon terminals | Branching ends of the axon that communicate with another neuron, muscle or gland at a synapse. |
| Synapse | The junction where a neuron communicates with another cell, usually using chemical messengers called neurotransmitters. |

### Direction of information flow

**Dendrites → cell body → axon → axon terminals → synapse → next cell**

This is the usual direction in a typical neuron. Not every neuron has exactly the same shape, but the diagram described here is a useful basic model.

### Three functional types of neuron

1. **Sensory (afferent) neuron:** carries information from a receptor towards the central nervous system.
2. **Interneuron (relay neuron):** connects neurons within the brain or spinal cord and helps process information.
3. **Motor (efferent) neuron:** carries commands from the central nervous system to an effector such as a muscle or gland.

## 5. Reflex action and the reflex arc

A **reflex action** is a rapid, automatic and involuntary response to a stimulus. It helps an animal respond to a change or possible danger without waiting for a deliberate decision.

A **reflex arc** is the pathway followed by nerve impulses during a reflex action.

### Five main parts of a reflex arc

1. **Receptor:** detects the stimulus, for example a sensory ending in the skin.
2. **Sensory neuron:** carries the impulse from the receptor towards the central nervous system.
3. **Integration centre:** the spinal cord or brainstem processes the information; one or more interneurons may connect sensory and motor pathways.
4. **Motor neuron:** carries an instruction away from the central nervous system.
5. **Effector:** a muscle or gland that produces the response.

### Example: withdrawal reflex

If a calf's foot receives a harmful stimulus, receptors detect it. A sensory neuron carries an impulse to the spinal cord. The spinal cord circuits activate motor neurons supplying flexor muscles. The muscles contract and the calf withdraws the limb.

The withdrawal response is protective. A spinal reflex does not mean that the animal has no conscious perception of pain; reflex movement and conscious pain behaviour are different observations.

### Four examples of reflex actions in animals

1. **Withdrawal reflex:** a calf withdraws a limb after a harmful stimulus to the hoof or foot.
2. **Suckling reflex:** a young calf responds to contact in the mouth by closing the mouth and moving the tongue to suckle.
3. **Blink or palpebral reflex:** an animal blinks when the eyelid region is lightly touched or the cornea is stimulated.
4. **Patellar (knee-jerk) reflex:** stimulation of the patellar tendon causes a brief extension movement at the stifle (knee) joint.

Other examples include the swallowing reflex, cough reflex, pupillary light reflex and anal/perineal reflex.

These are examples for learning, not instructions for testing animals. Do not pinch a calf, touch its eye or strike a tendon to demonstrate a reflex. Observe natural behaviour or use a teacher-approved video or veterinary demonstration.

## 6. Quick revision

- Inhalation: diaphragm contracts and flattens; ribs move up and out; chest volume increases; lung pressure falls; air enters.
- Quiet exhalation: diaphragm relaxes; chest volume decreases; lung pressure rises; air leaves.
- In a cow, air passes through the nose, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi and bronchioles to the alveoli.
- Oxygen diffuses from alveoli into blood; carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into alveoli.
- Blood carries oxygen to cells for aerobic cellular respiration.
- The neuron is the basic structural and functional unit of nervous tissue.
- Dendrites receive information; the cell body integrates it; the axon carries impulses away; axon terminals communicate at synapses.
- A reflex arc contains a receptor, sensory neuron, integration centre, motor neuron and effector.

## 7. Check your understanding

### Question 1 (8 marks)
Explain the mechanics of inhalation and quiet exhalation in a cow.

**Answer:** During inhalation, the diaphragm contracts and flattens while the external intercostal muscles contract and move the ribs up and out. The thoracic cavity increases in volume, pressure in the lungs falls below atmospheric pressure and air enters. During quiet exhalation, the diaphragm and external intercostal muscles relax. The chest and lungs recoil, thoracic volume decreases, lung pressure rises above atmospheric pressure and air flows out.

- 2 marks — External intercostal/rib movement during inhalation and exhalation.
- 2 marks — Correct volume and pressure changes.
- 2 marks — Correct direction of airflow in both phases.

### Question 2 (8 marks)
Trace the route of air in a cow from the nostrils to the gas-exchange surface. Explain how oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged.

**Answer:** Air passes through the nostrils, nasal passages, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi and bronchioles to the alveoli. Oxygen diffuses from alveolar air through the thin alveolar-capillary surface into blood and binds mainly to haemoglobin. Carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into the alveoli and is removed during exhalation.

**Marking:**
- 4 marks — Correctly traces the air route, with one mark for each group of structures in the correct sequence.
- 2 marks — Explains oxygen diffusion into blood and carriage by red blood cells.
- 2 marks — Explains carbon dioxide diffusion into alveoli and removal during exhalation.

### Question 3 (6 marks)
Describe how oxygen taken into a cow's lungs is used by its body cells.

**Answer:** Oxygen enters the alveoli and diffuses into the blood. Red blood cells carry it to tissues. Oxygen moves into cells, where it is used in aerobic cellular respiration to release energy from glucose. Carbon dioxide and water are produced; carbon dioxide enters the blood and is carried back to the lungs for exhalation.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Oxygen enters blood and is transported to tissues.
- 2 marks — Explains the use of oxygen in cellular respiration to release energy.
- 2 marks — Explains carbon dioxide production and transport back to the lungs.

### Question 4 (6 marks)
Define a neuron and describe the functions of the cell body, dendrites and axon.

**Answer:** A neuron is a specialised nerve cell and the basic structural and functional unit of nervous tissue. The cell body contains the nucleus and maintains the cell. Dendrites receive signals and carry them towards the cell body. The axon carries nerve impulses away from the cell body towards other neurons, muscles or glands.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Defines a neuron and identifies its role as the basic unit.
- 1 mark — Cell body.
- 1 mark — Dendrites.
- 2 marks — Axon and direction of impulse transmission.

### Question 5 (5 marks)
List the five main parts of a reflex arc in the correct order.

**Answer:** Receptor → sensory neuron → integration centre (spinal cord or brainstem) → motor neuron → effector.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each correct part in the correct sequence.

### Question 6 (4 marks)
Give four examples of reflex actions in farm animals.

**Answer:** Any four accurate examples, such as withdrawal of a calf's limb, suckling reflex in a young calf, blink/palpebral reflex and patellar (knee-jerk) reflex. Swallowing, coughing, pupillary light and anal/perineal reflexes are also acceptable.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each correct example.

### Question 7 (3 marks)
Explain why a reflex action can occur before an animal makes a deliberate decision.

**Answer:** A receptor detects a stimulus and a nerve impulse travels along a sensory neuron to the spinal cord or brainstem. The central nervous system rapidly activates a motor neuron, which stimulates an effector. The response is automatic and does not require a deliberate decision first.
