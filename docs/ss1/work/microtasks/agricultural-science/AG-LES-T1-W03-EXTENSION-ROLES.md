## Lesson body

Week 3 Supplement: Roles of Agricultural Extension in Agricultural Development

Agricultural extension is a service that connects farmers and rural communities with useful agricultural knowledge, skills, advice, research and support services. Extension agents work with farmers: they listen to farmers' needs, share information and help farmers make informed decisions.

The Week 3 source states that extension agents teach farmers improved methods of farming (block 273). This supplement explains additional roles supported by Nigerian agricultural research and extension institutions and international agricultural-development organisations.

## Main Roles of Agricultural Extension

1. **Teaching and training farmers:** Extension agents explain improved crop and livestock practices and help farmers build practical skills.
2. **Demonstrating improved methods:** They organise or support demonstration plots and practical sessions so farmers can see how a method or technology works.
3. **Sharing research and innovations:** They communicate research findings, improved varieties, new technologies and useful information in language farmers can understand.
4. **Providing advice and helping solve problems:** They help farmers identify production challenges and seek suitable advice from specialists, research institutes or relevant services.
5. **Connecting farmers and researchers:** They carry useful research information to farmers and communicate farmers' questions, experiences and problems back to researchers. This makes extension a two-way link.
6. **Linking farmers to markets and services:** They help farmers find information and connections relating to markets, farm inputs, credit, veterinary services and other support.
7. **Encouraging sustainable and climate-resilient farming:** They share information about soil and water conservation, biodiversity, climate adaptation and responsible production.
8. **Providing timely information through communication channels:** They use meetings, radio, mobile phones, helplines and digital platforms to make useful agricultural information easier to obtain.

## Example

A maize farmer notices an unfamiliar problem in the field and is unsure how to reach buyers. An extension agent can listen to the farmer, help record and describe the problem, seek advice from a research or specialist service, explain reliable information to the farmer, and help connect the farmer with relevant input or market services. The agent can also report the farmer's experience to the research system. The extension agent does not replace a researcher, veterinarian or buyer; the agent helps farmers reach the right knowledge and services.

## Key Point

Agricultural extension is more than giving instructions. It involves teaching, demonstration, advice, information-sharing and links among farmers, researchers, markets and support services. Information also flows back from farmers so that research and services can respond to real farming needs.

## Practice and complete marking guide

### P1 — Definition (2 marks)

**Question:** What is agricultural extension?

**Answer:** Agricultural extension is a service that connects farmers and rural communities with useful agricultural knowledge, skills, advice, research and support services so that they can make informed farming decisions.

**Marking:**
- 1 mark — Identifies extension as an agricultural advisory, education or information service.
- 1 mark — Explains that it connects farmers with knowledge, skills, research or support services.

### P2 — Roles (6 marks)

**Question:** State any six roles of agricultural extension in agricultural development.

**Answer:** Any six: (1) teaching and training farmers in improved methods; (2) demonstrating new practices; (3) sharing research findings and innovations; (4) advising farmers and helping solve production problems; (5) linking farmers with researchers and carrying farmer feedback back to research institutions; (6) connecting farmers with markets and support services; (7) promoting sustainable and climate-resilient farming; (8) providing timely information through radio, phones, helplines and digital platforms.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each distinct, valid role, up to six marks.

### P3 — Applied scenario (4 marks)

**Question:** A maize farmer notices an unfamiliar problem in the field and also has difficulty finding buyers. State four ways an extension agent can help.

**Answer:** The agent can listen to the farmer and record the problem; seek diagnostic advice from a research institute or specialist; explain reliable information or demonstrate an improved practice; connect the farmer with relevant input or market services; and report the farmer's experience back to researchers. Any four are acceptable.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each distinct, relevant action, up to four marks.

### P4 — Two-way research link (4 marks)

**Question:** Explain the two-way link between agricultural extension, farmers and research institutions.

**Answer:** Extension carries research findings, innovations and improved practices from research institutions to farmers. It also gathers farmers' questions, experiences and production problems and passes this feedback back to researchers so that research and advice can respond to farmers' needs.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Explains how extension communicates research information and innovations to farmers.
- 2 marks — Explains how extension carries farmers' experiences, questions or problems back to researchers.
