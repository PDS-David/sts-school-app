## A. Farmstead — recommended school-level decision

### Recommended definition

A **farmstead** is the built-up home and operating centre of a farm: the farmhouse and the associated yards, animal shelters, stores, machinery sheds, workshops, processing/storage structures, service areas and access ways. It is not the whole farm. The whole farm also includes fields, grazing areas, plantations, ponds and other production land.

This distinction is consistent with FAO's farm-structures guidance, which describes the farmstead as the nucleus of farm operations and identifies the dwelling, animal shelters, storage structures, equipment shed and workshop as typical components. The Nigerian University of Nigeria course description also treats farmstead planning as a distinct area of farm layout and management.

### Recommended five planning principles

There is no universal international list of exactly five farmstead-planning principles. The sources present a set of planning factors, which can be grouped into five school-level principles without implying that other factors are unimportant.

1. **Choose a suitable site:** prefer firm, stable ground with suitable soil and a gentle slope; avoid flood-prone or waterlogged locations. Ensure surface and subsurface drainage can carry water away without causing erosion.
2. **Provide access and efficient movement:** connect the farmstead to all-weather roads, fields, markets and service routes. Arrange buildings so workers, vehicles, feed, livestock and produce can move safely with minimum wasted distance.
3. **Ensure water, utilities and sanitation:** provide adequate clean water for people, animals, cleaning and production; plan electricity and other services where needed; include drainage, manure and waste handling.
4. **Orient buildings for climate, ventilation and hygiene:** consider sun path and prevailing winds. Place odour-producing livestock and waste areas downwind from the dwelling and neighbouring homes where practicable; provide ventilation and protect buildings from excessive heat, wind and rain.
5. **Arrange facilities safely and allow expansion:** group related buildings and operations; separate living areas from hazards, waste and high-traffic areas; consider fire safety, security and child/animal safety; leave space for future buildings and services.

**Why this five-part grouping is recommended:** FAO farm-structures guidance explicitly identifies drainage, slope, vehicle manoeuvring, future expansion, prevailing wind, odour control, orientation, clean water, safety and security. FUNAAB farm-structures teaching material identifies utilities, orientation, expansion and efficient building arrangement. A Nigerian study on farmstead wind direction in Ibadan confirms that prevailing winds affect livestock-unit siting and odour management.

## B. Forestry — recommended school-level decision

### Recommended definition of forest and eight uses

A **forest** is an area dominated by trees and associated vegetation, organisms and soils, with ecological processes that support wildlife, water, climate and human livelihoods.

Eight useful categories for an SS1 answer are:

1. **Timber and construction materials:** logs and wood for building, furniture, doors and other products.
2. **Fuel:** fuelwood and charcoal for cooking, heating and other energy needs.
3. **Industrial raw materials:** poles, pulpwood, veneer logs, wood fibre and materials for paper and panels.
4. **Food:** fruits, nuts, seeds, edible leaves, mushrooms and other forest foods.
5. **Medicine and household materials:** medicinal plants, bark, roots, gums, resins, latex, fibres and craft materials.
6. **Wildlife habitat and biodiversity:** shelter, breeding sites and food for wild plants and animals.
7. **Soil and water protection:** tree cover and roots reduce erosion, protect watersheds and influence water cycles.
8. **Climate, recreation and livelihoods:** carbon storage, shade, recreation/ecotourism, employment and income for forest-dependent communities.

The categories combine tangible forest products with ecosystem services. They should not be presented as eight mutually exclusive products.

### Forestry department — recommended functions

For an SS1 answer, teach these core responsibilities of a government forestry department:

1. manage and conserve forest reserves and other public forest resources;
2. regulate harvesting and trade in forest produce through laws, licences, permits and inspections;
3. promote forest regeneration, afforestation, reforestation, nurseries and plantations;
4. protect forests against illegal logging, fire, encroachment, pests and other damage;
5. conserve biodiversity and support wildlife management;
6. monitor forest resources and evaluate forestry programmes;
7. provide technical advice, training and extension to state services, communities and private forestry operators;
8. promote sustainable use, processing and marketing of forest products.

Nigeria's Federal Department of Forestry lists responsibilities including conservation, utilisation and renewal of forest resources, forestry policy, monitoring, timber trade guidance, technical liaison with state forestry services, training, biodiversity and wildlife policy, and forest-resource assessment. Oyo State's official ministry pages identify orderly exploitation, continuous use of forest products, forest protection, tree planting and natural-resource management as state responsibilities.

### Taungya system — recommended definition

**Taungya** is an agroforestry/forest-establishment system in which food crops are cultivated between young forest-tree seedlings during the early years of a plantation. The crops use the open space while the trees are small; as the tree canopy develops and shade increases, annual crop cultivation is reduced or stops.

In Nigeria, documented forms include:
- **individual/traditional taungya:** farmers cultivate crops for a limited period in return for helping establish or care for the tree plantation;
- **departmental/direct taungya:** farmers may be engaged by a forestry department to carry out plantation-establishment work, sometimes for wages.

Do not define taungya as permanent intercropping throughout the life of a mature forest plantation.

### Forest regulation — recommended meaning

In this Week 9 context, **forest regulation** should mean the laws, rules and administrative controls used to protect and manage forests and forest produce. Examples include:
- declaring and demarcating forest reserves and protected trees;
- controlling felling, harvesting, collection, sale and transport of forest produce;
- requiring licences or permits and marking timber;
- regulating sawmills and wood-using industries;
- protecting forests against damage, fire and illegal removal;
- establishing nurseries, regeneration funds and replanting programmes;
- setting penalties and enforcing compliance.

The Oyo State Forestry Law is one local example of statutory controls over forest produce, permits, protected trees, forest reserves, timber marking, sawmills, nurseries, afforestation and forest protection. A lesson should not imply that one state's law is the complete national legal framework.

### Selective exploitation — recommended correction of terminology

The standard forestry term is **selective exploitation** or selective felling, not “selective exploration”. It means removing selected trees—usually chosen by species, size, maturity or condition—rather than clearing every tree in the area.

Selective exploitation can reduce the extent of canopy removal compared with clear-felling, but it is not automatically sustainable. It must be planned to protect regeneration, soil, water, habitat and future tree growth, and to avoid excessive collateral damage.

### Afforestation and reforestation — recommended distinction

- **Afforestation:** establishing forest through planting and/or deliberate seeding on land that was not previously forest.
- **Reforestation:** re-establishing forest on land that was previously forest but has been cleared or degraded.

These terms should not be treated as exact synonyms. FAO's Global Forest Resources Assessment definitions explicitly distinguish afforestation as forest expansion on land under a different prior land use; reforestation concerns re-establishing forest on previously forested land.

### Primary forest product — terminology requiring expert confirmation

The assessment asks for a “primary forest product”. This phrase can be interpreted in more than one way:
- **Raw forest output:** a good obtained directly from forest resources before substantial manufacture, such as roundwood/logs, poles, fuelwood, bamboo/rattan, gums/resins, fruits, nuts, medicinal plants and honey.
- **Primary wood/paper product in FAO statistical classification:** includes roundwood and products from first-stage processing such as sawnwood, veneer, wood pulp and paper; secondary processed products include furniture and packaging.

For a short SS1 answer, the recommended approach is to define a primary forest product as a direct/raw or first-stage forest output and give examples such as timber logs, poles, fuelwood, fruits, nuts, gums and medicinal plants. The lesson should then distinguish these from further manufactured goods such as furniture. However, because FAO uses “primary products” in a formal industrial classification that includes some first-processed products, the exact expected school marking answer needs subject-expert confirmation.
