## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Identify the main structures of the male reproductive system of a domestic chicken and state their functions.
2. Identify the main structures of the female reproductive system and explain how the egg passes through the oviduct.
3. Explain the roles of the infundibulum, magnum, isthmus, uterus/shell gland, vagina and cloaca.
4. Distinguish reproductive organs from nearby digestive and urinary organs shown in a combined diagram.
5. Explain the main differences between the male and female poultry reproductive systems.

## 1. What is the poultry reproductive system?

The reproductive system consists of organs that produce male or female reproductive cells and make fertilisation and reproduction possible.

- The male system produces sperm and transports it towards the female reproductive tract.
- The female system produces ova (egg cells), receives sperm, provides the place where fertilisation may occur and forms the structures around the egg before it is laid.

This lesson uses the domestic chicken as the main example because it is a common farm bird. Ducks, geese, turkeys, guinea fowl and other poultry share many basic features, but some reproductive structures and mating anatomy differ among species.

## 2. Male reproductive system of a chicken

The main male reproductive structures are paired testes, paired sperm ducts (vas deferens), and the cloacal openings through which sperm is transferred during mating.

| Structure | Description | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Testes (singular: testis) | Two oval reproductive organs located inside the body cavity, close to the kidneys near the back of the bird | Produce sperm cells and the male sex hormone testosterone |
| Seminiferous tubules | Very small, coiled tubules within the testes | Site where sperm cells are formed |
| Epididymal region | A small duct system associated with each testis | Helps transport sperm from the testis into the vas deferens; it is less developed than the mammalian epididymis |
| Vas deferens (ductus deferens) | A narrow, paired tube running from each testis towards the cloaca | Transports sperm and also serves as an important sperm-storage region in the chicken |
| Cloacal papillae | Small projections at the openings of the vas deferens on the cloacal wall | Help transfer sperm during mating in the domestic chicken |
| Cloaca | A common chamber into which the reproductive, urinary and digestive tracts open | Receives the reproductive ducts and also receives urinary and digestive wastes; it is not solely a reproductive organ |
| Vent | The external opening of the cloaca | Allows materials to pass out of the bird's body |

### Important features of the male chicken

1. The testes are internal; they do not descend into an external scrotum as mammalian testes usually do.
2. Both testes are normally developed in the mature rooster.
3. Sperm are produced in the testes and move through the vas deferens towards the cloaca.
4. In domestic chickens, the vas deferens opens at cloacal papillae that participate in sperm transfer.
5. The cloaca is shared by the reproductive, urinary and digestive systems.

**Species note:** the domestic chicken is not a perfect anatomical model for every poultry species. Some waterfowl, including ducks, have a developed phallus, while chickens use cloacal contact during mating. Avoid saying that all male birds have exactly the same external mating structures.

## 3. Female reproductive system of a chicken

The female chicken reproductive system has two main parts: the **ovary**, which produces ova/yolks, and the **oviduct**, which receives the ovulated yolk and adds the other parts of the egg.

In the domestic hen, the left ovary and left oviduct are normally functional. The right ovary and oviduct usually regress during development and remain rudimentary. This is a normal feature of the domestic chicken, not an injury.

### A. Ovary

The ovary is a cluster of follicles at different stages of development, located inside the body cavity near the back.

Functions:
- contains developing ova (egg cells);
- supports the growth of follicles and deposition of yolk around the developing ovum;
- releases a mature ovum/yolk during ovulation.

The yellow yolk is not the whole egg. The yolk contains the ovum and stored nutrients for the developing embryo if fertilisation occurs and the egg is incubated.

### B. Oviduct and its regions

The oviduct is a long, coiled tube. In the laying hen, the egg passes through a series of regions. Each region contributes a different structure to the developing egg.

| Oviduct region | Main function |
|---|---|
| Infundibulum (funnel) | Receives or catches the ovulated yolk. If viable sperm are present, fertilisation normally occurs here. |
| Magnum | Adds most of the albumen (egg white) around the yolk. |
| Isthmus | Adds the inner and outer shell membranes around the egg contents. |
| Uterus (shell gland) | Adds water and minerals and forms the hard shell, which consists mainly of calcium carbonate. |
| Vagina | Muscular final section of the oviduct; helps move the completed egg towards the cloaca. The outer cuticle/bloom is associated with the final passage before laying. |
| Cloaca | Receives the oviduct and is the common chamber for reproductive, urinary and digestive tracts. |
| Vent | External opening through which the egg is laid. |

### C. The egg-formation sequence

**Ovary releases yolk → infundibulum receives yolk (fertilisation may occur) → magnum adds albumen → isthmus adds shell membranes → uterus/shell gland forms the shell → vagina moves the egg towards the cloaca → egg passes out through the vent.**

The time spent in each section can vary with the bird, age, strain, laying condition and other factors. The exact timings printed in the original source remain under separate fact-check; they are not repeated here as universal values.

## 4. Nearby urinary and digestive structures in the source diagram

The source's combined male/female diagram also labels structures that are not reproductive organs. They are included because the urinary, digestive and reproductive tracts share the cloaca.

| Structure | System | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Kidneys | Urinary/excretory | Filter the blood and remove nitrogenous waste; birds mainly excrete nitrogen as uric acid/urates. |
| Ureters | Urinary/excretory | Carry urine/urates from the kidneys to the cloaca. |
| Intestine | Digestive | Digests food and absorbs nutrients and water; the lower digestive tract opens into the cloaca. |
| Cloaca | Shared terminal chamber | Receives material from the digestive, urinary and reproductive systems. |
| Vent | External opening | The outside opening of the cloaca. |

The kidney and ureter should not be labelled as reproductive organs simply because they appear beside the reproductive tract in a diagram. The intestine is also a digestive organ, not a reproductive organ.

## 5. Comparing male and female poultry reproductive systems

| Male chicken | Female chicken |
|---|---|
| Has two developed testes | Normally has one functional ovary (left) |
| Testes produce sperm and testosterone | Ovary produces ova and developing yolks |
| Two vas deferens transport sperm towards the cloaca | One functional oviduct receives the ovulated yolk and forms the rest of the egg |
| Cloacal papillae participate in sperm transfer | Oviduct opens into the cloaca; the egg leaves through the vent |
| Main reproductive product is sperm | Main reproductive products are ova and, after oviduct passage, eggs |

Both systems are inside the body cavity in the domestic chicken. Both connect with the cloaca, but their primary functions are different.

## 6. How fertilisation relates to egg formation

A hen can lay an egg without mating. Such an egg is **unfertilised** and will not develop into a chick.

When a hen has mated with a fertile rooster, sperm may be stored in specialised sperm-storage sites in the hen's reproductive tract. When an ovum is released and enters the infundibulum, a sperm may fertilise it. The egg then continues through the oviduct while albumen, shell membranes and shell are added.

The presence of an egg does not by itself prove that it is fertile. Fertility depends on whether fertilisation occurred. A fertile egg also needs suitable incubation conditions for the embryo to develop and hatch.

## 7. Safe interpretation of the original diagrams

The source diagrams at blocks 803–804 and 819 are useful prompts for identification, but some are low-resolution, crowded or have no documented reuse licence. This supplement therefore supplies original descriptions and function tables, not copied or traced pictures.

When a teacher displays an authorised source diagram, identify the structures by name and function. Do not infer that a label belongs to the reproductive system merely because it is close to a reproductive organ. In particular:
- testis and vas deferens are male reproductive structures;
- ovary and oviduct are female reproductive structures;
- kidney and ureter belong to the urinary system;
- intestine belongs to the digestive system;
- cloaca is shared by the digestive, urinary and reproductive systems.

## 8. Quick revision

- The male chicken has two internal testes that produce sperm.
- The vas deferens carries sperm from each testis towards the cloaca.
- The female chicken normally has one functional ovary and one functional oviduct on the left side.
- The ovary produces ova/yolks; the oviduct adds albumen, shell membranes and shell.
- Infundibulum receives the yolk and is the normal site of fertilisation.
- Magnum adds albumen; isthmus adds shell membranes; uterus/shell gland forms the shell.
- Vagina moves the completed egg towards the cloaca; the egg leaves through the vent.
- Kidneys and ureters are urinary organs; the intestine is digestive; the cloaca is shared.

## 9. Check your understanding

### Question 1 (8 marks)
Name the two main parts of the female chicken reproductive system and explain their functions.

**Answer:** The ovary contains and develops ova/follicles and releases the mature yolk during ovulation. The oviduct receives the ovulated yolk and adds albumen, shell membranes and shell before the egg is laid.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Names the ovary and oviduct.
- 3 marks — Explains ovary functions.
- 3 marks — Explains oviduct functions.

### Question 2 (6 marks)
Describe the main parts of the male chicken reproductive system and state their functions.

**Answer:** The paired testes produce sperm and testosterone. The paired vas deferens transport and store sperm. The vas deferens open at cloacal papillae, which participate in sperm transfer during mating. The cloaca receives the reproductive ducts.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Testes and their function.
- 2 marks — Vas deferens and its function.
- 1 mark — Cloacal papillae/sperm transfer.
- 1 mark — Cloaca.

### Question 3 (8 marks)
Describe the route followed by the yolk as it passes through the hen's oviduct and state what is added in each region.

**Answer:** The ovulated yolk is received by the infundibulum, where fertilisation may occur. The magnum adds albumen. The isthmus adds the shell membranes. The uterus or shell gland adds water/minerals and forms the calcium-carbonate shell. The vagina moves the egg towards the cloaca, and the egg leaves through the vent.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Infundibulum and fertilisation/receiving the yolk.
- 2 marks — Magnum and albumen.
- 1 mark — Isthmus and shell membranes.
- 2 marks — Uterus/shell gland and shell formation.
- 1 mark — Vagina/cloaca/vent sequence.

### Question 4 (6 marks)
Explain why the kidney, ureter and intestine may appear in a diagram of the poultry reproductive system even though they are not reproductive organs.

**Answer:** The kidneys and ureters are urinary organs; the ureters carry urates to the cloaca. The intestine is a digestive organ and also empties into the cloaca. The cloaca is a shared terminal chamber for the digestive, urinary and reproductive systems, so these organs may be shown together.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Identifies kidneys and ureters as urinary structures and gives their function.
- 2 marks — Identifies intestine as digestive and gives its function.
- 2 marks — Explains the shared cloaca.

### Question 5 (4 marks)
State two differences between the male and female reproductive systems of a domestic chicken.

**Answer:** The male has two testes, while the female normally has one functional left ovary. The male has vas deferens that transport sperm, while the female has an oviduct that receives the ovulated yolk and forms the egg.

**Marking:** 2 marks for each correct difference.

### Question 6 (4 marks)
State the functions of the infundibulum, magnum, isthmus and uterus/shell gland.

**Answer:** Infundibulum receives the yolk and is the normal site of fertilisation; magnum adds albumen; isthmus adds shell membranes; uterus/shell gland forms the hard shell.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each correct function.

### Question 7 (4 marks)
A hen lays an egg. Does this prove that the egg is fertile? Explain.

**Answer:** No. A hen can lay an egg without mating. An egg is fertile only if a sperm fertilised the ovum, normally in the infundibulum. An unfertilised egg will not develop into a chick.

**Marking:**
- 1 mark — States that laying does not prove fertility.
- 2 marks — Explains that fertilisation by sperm is required.
- 1 mark — States that an unfertilised egg will not develop into a chick.

**Total: 40 marks.** Each marking guide adds up to the marks available for the question.
