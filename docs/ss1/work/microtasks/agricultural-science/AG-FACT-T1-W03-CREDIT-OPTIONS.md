## Evidence for options A–C

### A. Commercial bank — a valid agricultural-credit source
- The CBN's Agricultural Credit Guarantee Scheme Fund (ACGSF) is designed to encourage banks to lend to farmers. Its eligible participating financial institutions include Deposit Money Banks and CBN-regulated microfinance banks.
- The CBN's Commercial Agriculture Credit Scheme (CACS) routes agricultural credit through participating banks; all deposit money banks are approved participants, and credit is for agricultural enterprises.
- Sources:
  - [CBN — Agricultural Credit Guarantee Scheme Fund](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/DFD/agriculture/acgsf.html), especially the objectives and eligible participating financial institutions.
  - [CBN — Guidelines for Commercial Agriculture Credit Scheme](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2010/publications/guidelines/dfd/CACS_GUIDELINES_JAN_2010.pdf), especially sections 2, 5, 7 and 12.

### B. Agricultural bank — a valid agricultural-credit source
- The Bank of Agriculture (BOA) is Nigeria's specialist agricultural development-finance institution.
- Its stated mandate includes providing credit to support agricultural and non-agricultural value-chain activities; its current credit page explicitly lists loan facilities for farmers and agribusinesses.
- Sources:
  - [Bank of Agriculture — Our Drive / Mandate](https://boanig.com/our-drive/).
  - [Bank of Agriculture — Credit facilities](https://boanig.com/credit/).

### C. Thrift and savings society — a valid credit source, subject to membership
- The CBN's *CBN Briefs 2004–2005 Edition*, in its Nigeria microfinance discussion, states that Cooperative Thrift and Credit Societies are a source of funds mobilisation and participatory credit administration in Nigeria, and that these societies/unions have played a significant role in the rural economy.
- The same CBN publication describes savings and credit associations as semi-formal financial institutions with a significant presence in rural Nigeria.
- These are member-based arrangements: a farmer normally needs to be an eligible member of the relevant society to access its loans; they are not universal public lenders.
- Source: [CBN — CBN Briefs 2004–2005 Edition (PDF)](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/OUT/PUBLICATIONS/REPORTS/RD/2007/CBN%20BRIEFS%202004-2005%20EDITION.PDF), pp. 72–73 (PDF pagination), especially the discussion of savings and credit associations and cooperatives.

## Why a primary mortgage bank is the likely exception

- The CBN's final *Revised Guidelines for Primary Mortgage Banks in Nigeria* (2011) define primary mortgage banking business and list mortgage finance, real-estate construction finance, mortgage-focused deposits and related activities as permissible.
- The same CBN guidelines state that primary mortgage banks shall not grant consumer or commercial loans. Ordinary agricultural production credit is a commercial lending activity, not mortgage finance.
- The Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria's own mandate is likewise centred on housing delivery and long-term credit to mortgage institutions.
- Sources:
  - [CBN — Revised Guidelines for Primary Mortgage Banks in Nigeria (2011)](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/out/2013/ofisd/revised%20guidelines%20for%20primary%20mortgage%20banks%20in%20nigeria.pdf), section 1.2–1.3 (PDF pp. 5–6; extracted lines 212–250).
  - [FMBN — Our Mandate](https://fmbn.gov.ng/about/our-mandate).

## Important wording distinction: mortgage bank vs mortgage security
- A **primary mortgage bank** is an institution. Under the CBN's mortgage-bank guidelines, it is not an ordinary source of agricultural production credit.
- A **mortgage** is a form of secured lending/security arrangement, not a financial institution.
- The CBN's CACS guidelines expressly allow a charge on land (including land on which the borrower has a legal interest or right to farm), fixed assets, crops or livestock as collateral for an agricultural loan.
- Therefore, if option D means “mortgage bank”, it is the likely exception. If it means “mortgage” as a security/charge, the option is a category error: a mortgage can be used as collateral for agricultural credit, but it is not the lender.
- Source: [CBN — Guidelines for Commercial Agriculture Credit Scheme](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2010/publications/guidelines/dfd/CACS_GUIDELINES_JAN_2010.pdf), section 9, “Acceptable Collateral” (PDF p. 5; extracted lines 185–193).

## Finding and recommendation
- Options A, B and C can all be legitimate sources of agricultural credit in Nigeria (with the usual eligibility, borrower assessment and membership conditions).
- The likely intended exception is **D, if rewritten as “primary mortgage bank”**. CBN's regulatory guidance restricts primary mortgage banks to mortgage/real-estate finance and prohibits them from granting ordinary consumer or commercial loans.
- The present option “mortgage” is imprecise because it names a financing/security arrangement rather than an institution, and it could be confused with a mortgage used as collateral for an agricultural loan.

**Recommendation: correct and retain the item**, rather than withdraw it, with parallel institutional options:

> Farmers may obtain agricultural credit from any of the following institutions except:
> A. Commercial bank
> B. Bank of Agriculture
> C. Cooperative thrift and credit society
> D. Primary mortgage bank

The intended answer to this corrected version is **D. Primary mortgage bank**. This recommendation is evidence-based, but no answer key has been changed and no teacher/owner decision is presumed.
