## Correct anatomical sequence

The mature yolk (ovulated ovum) leaves the functional ovary and is captured by the infundibulum at the beginning of the left oviduct. It then passes through the magnum, isthmus, shell gland/uterus and vagina, before being laid through the cloaca/vent.

| Order | Oviduct region | Main function | Approximate time in a laying hen |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Infundibulum (funnel) | Captures the ovulated yolk; fertilization occurs here if viable sperm are present | About 15–17 minutes |
| 2 | Magnum | Secretes most of the albumen (egg white); albumen layers and chalazae form around the yolk | About 3 hours |
| 3 | Isthmus | Forms the inner and outer shell membranes | About 1 hour 15 minutes (75 minutes) |
| 4 | Uterus (shell gland) | Adds water/ions and deposits the hard shell, mainly calcium carbonate; shell pigment may be deposited | About 20–21 hours (often described as 20 hours or more) |
| 5 | Vagina | Muscular passage that helps move/lay the completed egg; the egg then exits through the cloaca/vent | A few seconds to less than 1 minute in common references |

### Stage-by-stage assessment

**Infundibulum — 15 minutes.** The source value is supported. University of Kentucky Extension gives 15–17 minutes; other poultry references commonly use 15 minutes. The infundibulum receives/captures the ovulated yolk, and fertilization, when it occurs, takes place here. It is not correct to imply that every egg is fertilized: table eggs laid without access to a male are unfertilized.

**Magnum — 3 hours.** Supported. This is the longest glandular section and secretes the albumen (egg white) around the yolk. The source's description of albumen formation is correct. Chalazae are associated with the albumen structure; the main teaching point is that albumen is added in the magnum.

**Isthmus — 1 hour 15 minutes.** Supported. The inner and outer shell membranes are added here. This is a well-supported approximate value: University of Kentucky Extension specifies 75 minutes and Penn State Extension gives 1.25 hours. Some references round it to about 1 hour.

**Uterus/shell gland — 18–21 hours.** The function is correct; the lower end of the source range is less well supported by the references reviewed. Common teaching references report about 20–21 hours, or 20 hours or more. Water and salts are added, and the shell is deposited mainly as calcium carbonate; pigment may be added in the shell gland. Recommend “about 20–21 hours” or “approximately 20 hours or more,” rather than treating 18 hours as the standard value.

**Vagina — 1–15 minutes.** The passage function is broadly correct, but the upper end of this duration is not supported by the main references reviewed. University of Kentucky Extension describes the vagina as a muscular passage for oviposition and the Poultry Site handbook gives less than 1 minute. Recommend “a few seconds to less than 1 minute” or simply “brief passage; no substantial egg component is formed here.” The vagina and cloaca are separate structures: the vagina connects the oviduct to the cloaca, through which the egg is laid via the vent.

## Is “almost 26 hours” correct?

**Yes, as a qualified approximation for the laying hen.** University of Kentucky Extension describes the time to transform a yolk into a fully developed egg and lay it as about 25–26 hours. The Poultry Site handbook gives a total of about 25 hours. Penn State Extension describes the complete cycle as a little more than 24 hours, and the Washington State University 4-H guide's narrative gives a broad 22–26-hour range. The total is not a fixed stopwatch value: the duration varies with the hen and laying cycle, and the shell-gland stage accounts for most of it.

The source's “almost 26 hours” should therefore not be rejected as wrong. It should be rewritten as “A hen usually takes about 25–26 hours to form and lay an egg; the time varies.” The source's 18–21-hour shell-gland range plus its other stage estimates can produce a broad total; using the more common 20–21-hour shell-gland estimate yields a total close to 25 hours.

## Recommended corrected school wording

“Egg formation in a laying hen takes about 25–26 hours. After ovulation, the yolk is caught by the infundibulum (about 15 minutes), where fertilization may occur if sperm are present. The magnum adds the albumen or egg white (about 3 hours). The isthmus forms the two shell membranes (about 1 hour 15 minutes). In the uterus or shell gland, water and salts are added and the hard shell, mainly calcium carbonate, is formed (about 20–21 hours). The vagina is the short muscular passage through which the completed egg is laid via the cloaca/vent.”

## Species and interpretation limits

- The figures above are typical reference values for the domestic chicken/laying hen, not universal timings for all birds.
- The functional sequence is broadly comparable across birds, but the duration spent in the oviduct differs by species. A published guinea-fowl study, for example, measured different residence times in the infundibulum and magnum from standard hen values.
- The ovary produces the yolk; the oviduct adds albumen, membranes and shell. Do not say that the whole egg is made in the ovary.
- The infundibulum is the fertilization site when sperm are present; fertilization is not required for an egg to be laid as a table egg.
- The uterus in this context means the avian shell gland, not a mammalian pregnancy uterus.
- The cloaca is the common terminal chamber/opening for digestive, urinary and reproductive tracts; it is not an additional egg-forming oviduct section.

## Sources consulted

1. **University of Kentucky Extension / Poultry Extension**, Jacquie Jacob, “Avian Reproductive System – Female.” Describes the hen's ovary/oviduct, the five oviduct regions, stage functions and approximate times: infundibulum 15–17 minutes, magnum 3 hours, isthmus 75 minutes, shell gland 20+ hours, and a short vagina passage; states the full cycle is about 25–26 hours. https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-anatomy/avian-reproductive-female/
2. **Penn State Extension**, “Hen Reproduction.” Describes yolk capture, albumen, membranes, shell and the usual total cycle of a little more than 24 hours; gives stage times of 15 minutes, 3 hours, 1.25 hours and 20.75 hours for the shell gland. https://extension.psu.edu/hen-reproduction
3. **State of Queensland, Department of Primary Industries and Fisheries / DSM Nutritional Products (2007)**, *Optimum Egg Quality: A Practical Approach*, “Formation of the egg.” Gives the hen sequence and approximate times: infundibulum 15 minutes, magnum 3 hours, isthmus 1 hour, shell gland 21 hours and vagina/cloaca less than 1 minute; describes shell as mainly calcium carbonate. https://www.thepoultrysite.com/publications/egg-quality-handbook/2/formation-of-the-egg
4. **Washington State University Extension**, *4-H Poultry Leader Notebook, Poultry Science Level III*, “Reproduction and Fertilization of Poultry.” Its leader notes describe the egg-forming organs and a 22–26-hour total. Its separate handout's isthmus entry appears to say 15 minutes, which conflicts with its narrative and with University of Kentucky Extension/Penn State values of 75 minutes/1.25 hours; that isolated handout entry was not adopted as the best-supported value. https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-ecommerce/uploads/sites/2/product-3260-sku-EM083E.pdf
5. **“Management of Breeding in Small Poultry Production Units” (2018)**, review of small-scale poultry breeding. Describes the oviduct sequence and typical times: infundibulum 15 minutes, magnum 3 hours, isthmus 1 hour, shell gland/uterus 20 hours and vagina a few seconds. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7149392/
6. **Ogawa, H., Kuwayama, T. & Tanaka, K. (1996)**, “The Timing of Ovulation after Oviposition and the Time Spent by the Ovum in Each Portion of Oviduct in the Guinea Fowl,” *Japanese Poultry Science* 33(2):118–122. Demonstrates that timings are species-specific and can differ from chicken values. DOI: 10.2141/jpsa.33.118. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpsa1964/33/2/33_2_118/_article
