## National prevalence evidence

### NBS General Household Survey–Panel Wave 4 (2018/19)
- The NBS 2019 GHS-Panel report states that the most common means of acquiring agricultural land was **family inheritance**.
- Nationally, **62.7% of male-managed plots** and **63.4% of female-managed plots** were acquired through family inheritance.
- The report describes Wave 4 (2018/19) as a nationally representative survey of approximately 5,000 households. This is the closest NBS national plot-acquisition evidence to the source document's 2018/2019 context.
- The survey measures **how agricultural plots were acquired** (outright purchase, rental, free use, community distribution, family inheritance, etc.); it is not a classification of the statutory land-tenure system.
- Sources:
  - [NBS — LSMS-Integrated Panel Survey Report / GHS-Panel Wave 4 (2018/19)](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/pdfuploads/LSMS_Integrated_Panel_Survey_Report.pdf), executive summary lines 136–138 and section 6.2 / Table 6.2, lines 2304–2329.
  - [World Bank — Nigeria GHS-Panel survey design and Wave 4 coverage](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/lsms/initiatives/lsms-ISA), nationally representative panel, national/urban/rural coverage.

### NBS General Household Survey–Panel Wave 5 (2023/24)
- The latest NBS panel survey confirms the pattern:
  - **64.9%** of male-managed plots were acquired through family inheritance.
  - **59.7%** of female-managed plots were acquired through family inheritance.
  - Rental was reported for 12.1% of male-managed and 16.6% of female-managed plots; purchase for 10.7% and 8.0%, respectively.
- The survey interviewed 4,715 households across 518 enumeration areas and is designed to be nationally representative.
- It notes regional differences: for example, purchase is more common in the North West than elsewhere, rental is more common in the South South, and customary/community authority allocation is more common in the South East. The national inheritance result should not be presented as identical in every region.
- Sources:
  - [NBS — General Household Survey–Panel Wave 5 (2023/24), Survey Report](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/pdfuploads/GHS-Panel%20Wave%205%20-%20Survey%20Report.pdf), executive summary lines 328–343 and 459–464; Chapter 6, lines 2572–2600.
  - [NBS — GHS-Panel Wave 5, Land Tenure section](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/pdfuploads/GHS-Panel%20Wave%205%20-%20Survey%20Report.pdf), section 6.2.1, pp. 62–63.

## Legal framework: Land Use Act 1978
- **Section 1:** all land comprised in each State is vested in the Governor of that State, to be held in trust and administered for the use and common benefit of Nigerians.
- **Section 5:** the Governor may grant statutory rights of occupancy.
- **Section 6:** local governments may grant customary rights of occupancy over land outside urban areas, including for agricultural and grazing purposes.
- **Section 24:** on the death of an occupier, devolution of a customary right of occupancy is regulated by the customary law of the locality; devolution of a statutory right of occupancy is generally regulated by the customary law of the deceased occupier, unless another applicable law applies.
- This means **inheritance is a common mode of acquiring/devolving rights over agricultural plots**, but it is not itself the whole statutory land-tenure system. The legal framework is the Land Use Act and its statutory/customary rights of occupancy.
- Source: [Land Use Act 1978 — Official Gazette copy hosted by Adamawa State Lands Department](https://lands.adamawastate.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/NIGERIA-LAND-USE-ACT-GAZZETED-1978.pdf), sections 1, 5, 6 and 24 (especially lines 277–298, 363–376, 414–431 and 819–840).

## Finding
- If “land tenure” in the source questions means **mode of acquiring agricultural plots**, family inheritance is the most common national mode in both NBS Wave 4 (2018/19) and Wave 5 (2023/24). The MCQ's intended answer **A. inheritance** is therefore supported.
- If “land tenure system” means the **legal framework**, inheritance is not the correct umbrella answer. The Land Use Act establishes statutory and customary rights of occupancy, with land vested in the State Governor and customary agricultural/grazing rights granted by local governments in non-urban areas.
- The source's list mixes communal arrangements, acquisition modes (inheritance, free gift), contractual modes (leasehold) and state ownership/administration. The items can overlap: a plot inherited within a family may be held under a customary right of occupancy.
- NBS reports plot-level acquisition modes; it does not establish why inheritance is prevalent. The Act's devolution provisions explain how rights can pass on death under customary law, but they do not by themselves prove the social causes of the prevalence.
