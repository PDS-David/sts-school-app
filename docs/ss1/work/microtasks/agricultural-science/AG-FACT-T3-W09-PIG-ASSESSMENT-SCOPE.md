## 1. Correct term for a pig house: “sty” / “pigsty”

**Finding:** “Sty” is a valid common English term for an enclosure where pigs are kept; “pigsty” is the compound form. Cambridge English Dictionary defines *pigsty* as an enclosed area where pigs are kept and lists *sty* among the related animal-dwelling terms. “Pigpen” is also a standard term, particularly in North American English. In the Week Nine source body, pigs are described as being housed in **pens** (blocks 1038–1039). These terms are not mutually exclusive: a sty/pigsty is a type of pig enclosure, while “pen” is the general housing term used by the source.

**Assessment decision:** The weekend MCQ answer “sty” is factually acceptable. The problem is instructional alignment: the source teaches “pen” but does not introduce “sty” before testing it.

**Recommended disposition:** Keep “sty” as a valid answer only if the lesson includes a short vocabulary clarification: “A pig house or enclosure may be called a sty or pigsty; the source also refers to the individual housing units as pens.” Alternatively, revise the MCQ to use the taught word “pen.” A dependent supplement is justified if the existing MCQ is to remain unchanged.

## 2. Second disadvantage of intensive pig rearing

**Source coverage:** The source identifies “capital intensive” as a disadvantage of the intensive system (block 1032). Its later evaluation and theory questions ask learners for two disadvantages, but the source body supplies only one explicit disadvantage.

**Finding:** A second disadvantage is supported by authoritative pig-production teaching material. The Federal University of Agriculture, Abeokuta (FUNAAB) *Pig Production Training Manual*, in its section “Possible Problems with Intensified Confinement Production,” lists intensified disease problems and the greater importance of disease control. It also identifies the need for adequate diets, high production costs, manure/waste-management problems and animal-welfare concerns. An Australian Centre for International Agricultural Research (ACIAR) report on intensive pig/poultry production similarly lists high cost and high management-skill requirements as disadvantages.

**Recommended second disadvantage:** “Disease problems can become more serious in intensive confinement systems, so strict disease prevention and control are required.” This is distinct from the initial capital investment disadvantage and is directly supported by the FUNAAB training manual. The statement should be qualified: intensive housing does not automatically cause an outbreak, and sound biosecurity and management can reduce risk.

**Alternative supported wording:** “Intensive systems also have high recurring operating costs and require skilled, continuous management, especially for feed, housing and animal health.” This is also supported by the FUNAAB and ACIAR materials, but it may overlap conceptually with “capital intensive.” For a two-answer question, the disease-control disadvantage is the clearer distinct second point.

**Assessment decision:** The request for two disadvantages is not unsupported; a second, independently supported disadvantage exists. The source is incomplete because it does not teach that disadvantage before testing it.

**Recommended disposition:** A short assessment supplement is justified. Add the disease-control disadvantage with the qualification above, then key the two answers as (1) high initial capital investment and (2) greater disease-control demands/risk under intensive confinement. Do not add a broad claim that intensive systems always have more disease; the risk depends on biosecurity and management.

## 3. “Fatheners” versus “fatteners”

**Finding:** “Fatheners” at block 998 is a source spelling error. The standard livestock term is **fatteners** (also called fattening pigs): pigs reared for meat production between the grower stage and slaughter. The same source uses “fatteners” correctly in blocks 1042 and 1068. FAO's AGROVOC vocabulary identifies “fattening pigs” as the preferred concept and “fattener” as an entry term; the KTBL livestock glossary defines “FATTENER” as “FATTENING PIG.”

**Recommended disposition:** Record a delivery-only spelling correction from “Fatheners” to “fatteners” at the affected display location, preserving the original source bytes and source transcript. Do not create a new lesson or change the original document.
