- Week Five block 429 states that improved crop varieties and animal breeds with desirable qualities (disease resistance, fast growth, early maturity and high yield) have been developed “through genetics and breeding”.
- Weekend Assignment Q2, block 454, asks: “The aspect of science and technology that aids the development of new crop varieties and breeds of animals is known as A. Genetic engineering B. Reproduction engineering C. Animal health D. Breeding engineering”.
- The source paragraph uses the broad phrase **genetics and breeding**; the MCQ substitutes the narrower term **genetic engineering**.

## Evidence and terminology

### Genetic engineering is a real crop/animal improvement technique
- The US FDA defines genetic engineering as a method that can copy a gene associated with a desired trait in one organism and put it into another. Its Bt corn example inserts a bacterial gene into corn DNA to confer insect resistance.
- The FDA explains that genetic engineering is used in combination with traditional breeding to produce some genetically engineered plant varieties; it also describes genetic engineering's effects on plant and animal breeding.
- FAO says gene/genome editing techniques can be used to breed new plant varieties and animal breeds for agricultural purposes.
- Sources:
  - [FDA — Types of Genetic Modification Methods for Crops](https://www.fda.gov/food/agricultural-biotechnology/types-genetic-modification-methods-crops), especially “Traditional Crop Modification” and “Genetic Engineering”.
  - [FDA — Science and History of GMOs and Other Food Modification Processes](https://www.fda.gov/food/agricultural-biotechnology/science-and-history-gmos-and-other-food-modification-processes), especially “How has genetic engineering changed plant and animal breeding?” and the definition of how GMOs are made.
  - [FAO — Gene editing](https://www.fao.org/food-safety/scientific-advice/biotechnology--gmo-and-gm-foods/gene-editing/en), lines 29–32.

### Genetic engineering is not the whole of genetics and breeding
- Traditional selective breeding and crossbreeding have been used for millennia to develop plants and animals with desirable traits. They do not require genetic engineering.
- FDA explicitly distinguishes traditional plant modification (selective breeding and crossbreeding) from genetic engineering as separate methods.
- FAO describes animal genetic improvement (animal breeding) as selecting genetically superior animals to become the parents of the next generation. This is broader than direct genetic engineering.
- Sources:
  - [FDA — Types of Genetic Modification Methods for Crops](https://www.fda.gov/food/agricultural-biotechnology/types-genetic-modification-methods-crops), lines 68–84.
  - [FDA — Science and History of GMOs and Other Food Modification Processes](https://www.fda.gov/food/agricultural-biotechnology/science-and-history-gmos-and-other-food-modification-processes), lines 69–75 and 122–126.
  - [FAO — Why is animal genetics important?](https://www.fao.org/animal-genetics/background/why-is-ag-important/en/), lines 22–28.
  - [National Academies — Genetically Engineered Crops: Experiences and Prospects (2016), Summary](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/23395/chapter/3), on the complementary use of conventional breeding and genetic engineering.

## Finding
- Option **A. Genetic engineering** is scientifically defensible as one modern technique for producing improved crop varieties and animal breeds.
- It is not a precise synonym for the source's **genetics and breeding**. Conventional selection, hybridisation/crossbreeding and animal breeding also produce improved varieties and breeds without genetic engineering.
- The current stem says “the aspect ... that aids the development” without identifying direct gene transfer, DNA modification or another feature unique to genetic engineering. Therefore it is too broad and could describe several forms of genetic improvement.
- Options B (“reproduction engineering”) and D (“breeding engineering”) are not standard names for the broad source concept; C (“animal health”) is a different area. A is likely the intended key, but the stem should not imply that all genetics and breeding is genetic engineering.

## Recommendation — no teacher decision presumed
**Correct and retain the item with a narrower stem** if the intended answer is genetic engineering. For example:

> Which modern biotechnology technique involves copying a gene for a desired trait and inserting it into another organism to develop a new variety or breed?
> A. Genetic engineering
> B. Reproduction engineering
> C. Animal health
> D. Breeding engineering

For that revised wording, **A. Genetic engineering** is unambiguous.

If the intended learning objective is to test the exact Week Five teaching in block 429, change option A to **“genetics and breeding”** or **“genetic improvement”** and revise the stem to ask for the broad process by which desirable inherited traits are developed through selection and breeding. Do not treat “genetic engineering” as an exact synonym for all genetics and breeding.

No answer key has been changed and no teacher/owner decision is presumed.
