## 1. Relative humidity and its measurement

**Humidity** describes the amount of water vapour in the air. **Relative humidity (RH)** expresses how close the air is to saturation with water vapour at the same temperature. It is expressed as a percentage.

Relative humidity is affected by temperature. Air can hold different amounts of water vapour at different temperatures, so RH is not simply the amount of water vapour in the air.

### Instrument used to measure relative humidity

A **hygrometer** is an instrument used to measure humidity. There are different types, including:

- **Wet-and-dry-bulb hygrometer (psychrometer):** uses two thermometers to estimate relative humidity.
- **Digital hygrometer:** uses an electronic humidity sensor to measure and display relative humidity.

### How a wet-and-dry-bulb psychrometer works

A psychrometer has two thermometers placed side by side:

1. The **dry-bulb thermometer** is exposed to the air and measures the air temperature.
2. The **wet-bulb thermometer** has its bulb covered with a clean, damp wick or sleeve.
3. Water evaporates from the wet wick and cools the wet-bulb thermometer.
4. The dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures are read.
5. The difference between the two readings is used with a psychrometric table, chart or calculator to estimate relative humidity.

**Interpretation:** when the air is dry, evaporation from the wet bulb is greater and the wet-bulb temperature is lower than the dry-bulb temperature. The difference is larger. When the air is already humid, evaporation is slower and the two readings are closer together.

The temperature difference alone is not the RH percentage. The dry-bulb temperature and wet-bulb depression must be interpreted using the appropriate psychrometric table or calculation.

### Digital hygrometer

A digital hygrometer contains a sensor whose electrical properties change as it absorbs or releases water vapour. The instrument's electronics convert the sensor response into a relative-humidity reading, often displayed as %RH. Some models also display temperature.

### Why farmers measure relative humidity

Relative humidity measurements can help farmers and agricultural technicians monitor:

- conditions in poultry houses, livestock housing and hatcheries;
- the environment during incubation and brooding;
- drying conditions for grain and other produce;
- storage conditions that may favour mould growth or product deterioration;
- environmental conditions that can affect animal comfort and disease risk.

Humidity is only one environmental factor. Temperature, ventilation, stocking density, sanitation and the health of the animals also matter.

### Care when using a psychrometer

- Keep the wet-bulb wick clean and properly moistened.
- Use clean water and replace a dirty or damaged wick.
- Allow the readings to stabilise before recording them.
- Ensure the instrument has suitable air movement; poor ventilation can make readings inaccurate.
- Keep the instrument away from direct sunlight, heaters and other sources of radiant heat.
- Check calibration and follow the manufacturer's instructions.

## 2. Two airborne respiratory diseases of farm animals

An airborne or aerosol-transmitted disease can spread when susceptible animals inhale infectious droplets or particles released by an infected animal. Coughing and breathing can release droplets into the air. Crowding, close contact and poor ventilation can increase exposure.

The word **airborne** should not be understood to mean that ordinary wind itself creates disease. The infectious agent must come from an infected animal or another infectious source. For the two examples below, transmission occurs mainly through inhalation of droplets/aerosols from infected animals.

### A. Bovine tuberculosis (mammalian tuberculosis)

- **Causative organism:** bacteria in the Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex; Mycobacterium bovis is a common cause in cattle.
- **Animals affected:** cattle and several other mammals, including goats, pigs and wildlife.
- **Main respiratory transmission:** susceptible animals may inhale infectious droplets/aerosols released in the cough or breath of infected animals.
- **Other routes:** young animals may become infected through contaminated milk or colostrum; other routes can include contact with infected body fluids or contaminated material.
- **Why it matters:** the disease can develop slowly, and infected animals may spread it before obvious signs are seen. Bovine tuberculosis is zoonotic, which means it can also infect people.

Do not diagnose tuberculosis from a cough alone. Suspected cases require veterinary investigation and official testing. Because bovine tuberculosis can affect people, milk safety and public-health advice are important.

### B. Contagious bovine pleuropneumonia (CBPP)

- **Causative organism:** Mycoplasma mycoides subsp. mycoides.
- **Animals affected:** cattle and water buffalo.
- **Main transmission:** a susceptible animal becomes infected by inhaling droplets released when an infected animal coughs. Close contact between animals is important.
- **Signs that may occur:** fever, reduced appetite, cough, nasal discharge and difficult or rapid breathing.
- **Why it matters:** CBPP is highly contagious and is an important cattle disease in Africa.

CBPP is not the same disease as bovine tuberculosis. They have different causative organisms and disease processes, although both can spread through inhalation of infectious respiratory droplets.

### Comparison of the two diseases

| Feature | Bovine tuberculosis | Contagious bovine pleuropneumonia |
|---|---|---|
| Main cause | Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex, commonly M. bovis in cattle | Mycoplasma mycoides subsp. mycoides |
| Main animals affected | Cattle and several other mammals | Cattle and water buffalo |
| Respiratory spread | Inhalation of infectious droplets/aerosols from infected animals | Inhalation of droplets released by coughing infected animals |
| Important additional point | Zoonotic: can infect humans | WOAH states humans are not known to be susceptible to CBPP |
| Control principle | Veterinary testing, biosecurity and official control measures | Early reporting, veterinary control, movement control and vaccination programmes where approved |

### How farmers reduce the spread of respiratory disease

1. Avoid overcrowding and provide suitable ventilation.
2. Isolate animals suspected of having an infectious respiratory disease and seek veterinary advice.
3. Avoid unnecessary mixing of herds and control the introduction of new animals.
4. Clean and maintain housing, feed and water areas.
5. Follow veterinary testing, vaccination and movement-control programmes where required.
6. Report suspected notifiable diseases to the appropriate veterinary authority.

Students should not attempt to diagnose or treat these diseases. A veterinarian or animal-health authority must investigate suspected cases.

## 3. Immunity and disease resistance

**Immunity** is the ability of an animal's body to recognise, resist and help remove infectious agents or other harmful foreign substances. Disease resistance is the animal's ability to prevent infection or limit the effects of disease.

The immune system uses several layers of defence. Two main types are **innate immunity** and **adaptive immunity**.

### A. Innate immunity

Innate immunity is the body's natural, general defence. It acts rapidly and is not directed at only one specific disease-causing organism.

Examples include:

- **Skin:** forms a physical barrier that makes it difficult for many microbes to enter the body.
- **Mucous membranes:** line the respiratory and digestive tracts and help trap invading particles.
- **Mucus and cilia:** trap inhaled particles and move them out of the airways.
- **Stomach acid and other secretions:** help destroy or inhibit some microbes.
- **Inflammation and fever:** are responses that help the body react to infection or tissue damage.
- **Phagocytic white blood cells:** engulf and destroy many invading microbes.

### B. Adaptive immunity

Adaptive immunity is a more specific defence. It responds to particular antigens and can develop memory.

- **B lymphocytes (B cells):** can develop into plasma cells that produce antibodies.
- **Antibodies:** bind to particular antigens and help neutralise or mark pathogens for removal.
- **T lymphocytes (T cells):** help coordinate immune responses or destroy infected cells.
- **Memory cells:** remain after an immune response and help the animal respond more quickly if it meets the same pathogen again.

### C. Active and passive immunity

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Natural active immunity | The animal's own immune system responds after natural exposure to an antigen | An animal develops an immune response after recovering from an infection |
| Vaccine-induced active immunity | A vaccine exposes the immune system to a safe form or component of an antigen so that memory can develop | A veterinarian gives a recommended vaccine to stimulate specific immune protection |
| Passive immunity | The animal receives ready-made antibodies from another source; protection is temporary | A newborn calf receives maternal antibodies through good-quality colostrum soon after birth |

Vaccination helps the adaptive immune system develop a specific response and memory. A vaccine may reduce the risk or severity of disease, but it does not guarantee that infection is impossible. Vaccination schedules must follow veterinary advice and the relevant disease-control programme.

### D. Factors that influence disease resistance

Disease resistance can be affected by:

- balanced nutrition and adequate clean water;
- age and stage of development;
- genetic differences among animals;
- stress, overcrowding and poor housing;
- parasite burden and other illnesses;
- vaccination and previous immune exposure;
- good hygiene, ventilation and biosecurity.

Good immunity does not replace farm hygiene or disease control. A well-nourished, vaccinated animal can still become infected if exposed to a sufficiently strong disease challenge.

## 4. How humidity, airborne disease and immunity relate to farm management

These three topics are connected to animal health, but they are not interchangeable:

- A hygrometer measures relative humidity; it does not diagnose disease.
- Humidity and ventilation influence the environment in animal housing, but the presence of humidity alone does not prove that an airborne disease is present.
- Respiratory diseases spread when animals are exposed to infectious agents; reducing crowding and improving ventilation can reduce exposure.
- Immunity helps animals resist or limit infection, but it does not make biosecurity unnecessary.
- Vaccination can stimulate adaptive immune memory for selected diseases, but it must be used according to veterinary guidance.

## 5. Quick revision

- A hygrometer measures humidity.
- A psychrometer uses wet- and dry-bulb thermometers; their temperature difference is interpreted with a psychrometric table or calculation.
- A digital hygrometer uses an electronic sensor to display relative humidity.
- Bovine tuberculosis and CBPP are two livestock diseases that can spread when animals inhale infectious respiratory droplets/aerosols.

- Innate immunity is rapid and general; adaptive immunity is specific and can form memory.
- Vaccination can stimulate active adaptive immunity; colostrum gives young mammals temporary passive protection.
- Biosecurity, ventilation, good nutrition and veterinary care complement immunity.

### Question 5 (6 marks)
Define immunity and distinguish between innate and adaptive immunity.

**Answer:** Immunity is the body's ability to recognise, resist and help remove infectious agents. Innate immunity is a rapid, general defence such as skin, mucus and inflammation. Adaptive immunity is a specific defence involving B cells, T cells, antibodies and immune memory.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Correct definition of immunity.
- 2 marks — Explains innate immunity.
- 2 marks — Explains adaptive immunity.

### Question 6 (6 marks)
Explain how vaccination can improve disease resistance in farm animals.

**Answer:** A vaccine presents an antigen or safe component of a disease-causing organism to the immune system. B and T lymphocytes respond, antibodies may be produced and memory cells can form. If the animal later encounters the same pathogen, the adaptive immune system can respond more rapidly and effectively. Vaccination reduces risk or severity but does not guarantee that infection cannot occur.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Explains that a vaccine stimulates a specific immune response.
- 2 marks — Explains antibodies and/or memory cells.
- 2 marks — Explains the benefit and avoids claiming complete guaranteed protection.

### Question 7 (6 marks)
Give three factors, apart from vaccination, that can affect the disease resistance of farm animals and explain each briefly.

**Answer:** Balanced nutrition supports normal immune function; clean water supports health and normal body processes; low stress and suitable housing reduce stress-related susceptibility; good ventilation reduces respiratory exposure; parasite control reduces additional disease burden.
