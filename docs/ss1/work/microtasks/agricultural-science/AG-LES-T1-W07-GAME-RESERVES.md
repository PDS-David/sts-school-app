## Lesson body

Week 7 Supplement: Nigerian Game Reserves and Their Locations

## 1. What is a game reserve?

A game reserve is a protected area set aside to conserve wild animals and the habitats they depend on. It helps protect wildlife, plants, water sources and natural landscapes. The activities allowed in a reserve are controlled by the responsible authority and the rules for that protected area.

Game reserves are important for more than seeing wild animals. They can support conservation research, environmental education and carefully managed nature tourism. They also help protect habitats and biodiversity for future generations.

## 2. Five Nigerian game reserves and their locations

The following are five documented Nigerian game reserves. Learn both the name and the state in which each is located.

1. **Yankari Game Reserve — Bauchi State.** It is in north-eastern Nigeria. It is known for its savanna woodland and wildlife, including elephants and other large mammals. It is managed by the Bauchi State Government.
2. **Falgore Game Reserve — Kano State.** It is in the southern part of Kano State. The Kano State Zoological and Wildlife Management Agency identifies it as one of the facilities under its responsibility.
3. **Lame-Burra Game Reserve — Bauchi State.** It is in Lame District, Toro Local Government Area, Bauchi State.
4. **Sambisa Game Reserve — Borno State.** It is in southern Borno State, between Gwoza and Bama Local Government Areas.
5. **Kwiambana Game Reserve — Zamfara State.** A Federal Government environmental-management report identifies it in Zamfara State, where it shares an ecosystem boundary with Kamuku National Park in Kaduna State.

These examples show that game reserves occur in different parts of Nigeria. A reserve's name, legal status and management arrangements may change over time, so the responsible government authority should be consulted for current access or visitor information.

## 3. Checking the four examples in the original source

The original Week 7 notes list four places using these exact spellings: **“Kanji National Park”, “Borgu Game Reserve”, “Zurguma Game Reserve” and “Yankari Game Reserve”.** These original spellings are retained here as evidence of what the source says. The corrections below are based on the cited official records; they do not alter the original source document.

- **“Kanji National Park”** should be identified as **Kainji Lake National Park**, the official name used by the Nigeria National Park Service. The park is located in Niger and Kwara States.
- **“Borgu Game Reserve”** and **“Zurguma Game Reserve”** refer to former game reserves. The Nigeria National Park Service records that the former Borgu and **Zugurma** game reserves were amalgamated to form Kainji Lake National Park in 1976. Borgu and Zugurma are now the names of the park's two sectors, not two separate current game reserves.
- The source's spelling **“Zurguma”** differs from the official spelling **“Zugurma”** used by the Nigeria National Park Service.
- **Yankari Game Reserve** is in Bauchi State. It was upgraded to a national park in 1991 and responsibility for its management was returned to the Bauchi State Government in 2006. It is again referred to as Yankari Game Reserve.

Therefore, the original list contains four names, but it does not provide five distinct current game reserves. The corrected Kainji history also shows why Borgu and Zugurma should not be counted separately as current reserves.

## 4. Why game reserves are important

Game reserves:

- protect wild animals and the places where they live;
- help conserve biodiversity and natural habitats;
- provide places for scientific research and environmental education;
- can support tourism and income for nearby communities when tourism is properly managed; and
- help preserve natural resources for future generations.

A game reserve is a **conservation or protected-area use of land**. It is not the same as land used directly to grow crops or keep farm livestock. In the standard FAO/United Nations statistical definition, agricultural land includes arable land, permanent crops, and permanent meadows and pastures. Nature-reserve activities are classified separately. This distinction matters because the source lists wildlife conservation among agricultural uses, while its assessment also treats a game reserve as land not used for farming. Learners should understand the difference between the broad idea of land use and the narrower statistical category of agricultural land.
