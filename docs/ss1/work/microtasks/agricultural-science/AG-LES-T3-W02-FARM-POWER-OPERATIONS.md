## Implemented lesson

# Third Term, Week Two: Farm Power — Matching Power Sources to Farm Operations

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Explain farm power and distinguish a power source from the machine or implement it drives.
2. Identify farm operations that use mechanical, electrical, wind and solar power.
3. Give at least two accurate farm-operation examples for each of the four power sources.
4. Explain the difference between a solar photovoltaic pump and a solar-thermal crop dryer.
5. Select a suitable power source for a farm task and state relevant limitations and safety precautions.

## 1. Meaning of farm power

Farm power is the energy or force used to carry out work on a farm. It enables a farmer to prepare land, plant crops, irrigate, care for livestock, harvest, process and transport produce.

A **power source** supplies the energy. A **machine or implement** receives or uses that energy. The **farm operation** is the job being done.

For example:

- Power source: mechanical power from a diesel engine.
- Machine: tractor pulling a plough.
- Farm operation: primary tillage, which cuts and turns the soil.

Do not confuse the machine with the power source. A tractor is a machine; its engine supplies mechanical power. A water pump is a machine; it may be driven by an electric motor, a diesel engine, a windmill or a solar-powered motor.

## 2. Mechanical power

Mechanical power on a farm is commonly supplied by an engine, such as a petrol or diesel engine. The engine produces motion that can drive a machine directly or through a transmission system. A tractor may pull an implement using its drawbar or drive suitable equipment through a power take-off (PTO).

### Farm operations using mechanical power

| Operation | Machine or equipment | How mechanical power is used |
|---|---|---|
| Land preparation | Tractor with plough | The tractor pulls the plough through the soil to cut and turn it. |
| Secondary tillage | Tractor with harrow | The tractor pulls the harrow to break soil clods and prepare a finer seedbed. |
| Ridging | Tractor with ridger | The tractor pulls the ridger to form ridges for crops that require them. |
| Planting or sowing | Tractor-mounted or tractor-drawn planter/seed drill | The tractor moves and powers the implement that places seed at the required spacing and depth. |
| Harvesting | Combine harvester or tractor-powered harvesting equipment | The engine powers cutting, threshing and crop-handling mechanisms. |
| Post-harvest processing | Engine-driven thresher, sheller or mill | The engine drives rotating or reciprocating parts that separate grain or process produce. |

**Two clear examples for the assessment:** (1) a tractor pulling a plough to prepare land; and (2) an engine-driven thresher separating grain after harvest. Other valid examples include harrowing, ridging, mechanised planting, harvesting and milling.

The National Agricultural Extension and Research Liaison Services (NAERLS) identifies tractor services for land clearing, ploughing, harrowing and ridging, and also works with planters, threshers/shellers and dryers. The exact machine selected depends on the crop, field conditions, farm size and available service support.

## 3. Electrical power

Electrical power is energy supplied through an electrical system, such as the public grid, a generator or a suitable renewable-electricity system. An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical motion. Other electrical equipment converts it into heat, light, cooling or controlled movement.

### Farm operations using electrical power

| Operation | Electrical equipment | How electricity helps |
|---|---|---|
| Irrigation and water supply | Electric water pump | An electric motor drives a pump that lifts or moves water through pipes or channels to the farm. |
| Milking | Milking machine | An electric motor and vacuum system operate equipment used to extract milk hygienically. |
| Brooding | Electric brooder or heating equipment | Electricity supplies controlled heat to help maintain suitable conditions for young chicks. |
| Crop and livestock-product processing | Electric motor driving a grinder, sheller, mixer or conveyor | The motor supplies motion to process feed or agricultural produce. |
| Storage and preservation | Refrigerator or cold room | Electrical refrigeration removes heat and helps maintain a suitable storage temperature. |
| Ventilation | Electric fans | Fans move air in poultry houses, greenhouses or storage areas where ventilation is required. |

**Two clear examples for the assessment:** (1) operating an electric pump to supply irrigation water; and (2) operating a milking machine. Brooder heating, feed grinding, shelling, refrigeration and ventilation are additional examples.

Nigeria's National Board for Technical Education curriculum specifically identifies brooder-pen heating, milking equipment, irrigation pumping and processing machines as farm uses of electricity.

### Electrical safety

Electricity can cause electric shock, burns and fire. Farm electrical installations and repairs must be handled by a qualified electrician. Keep electrical connections protected from water, use suitable earthing and circuit protection, keep cables away from animals and machinery, and never touch switches or equipment with wet hands. Disconnect and isolate equipment before cleaning or servicing it.

## 4. Wind power

Wind power is energy from moving air. A windmill can convert wind movement into mechanical motion. A wind turbine converts wind movement into electricity.

### Farm operations using wind power

| Operation | Equipment | How wind power is used |
|---|---|---|
| Lifting water | Windmill or windpump | Wind turns the blades; a mechanical linkage drives a pump that raises water from a well or other suitable source. The water may be stored for livestock or irrigation. |
| Grinding grain | Traditional windmill and millstones | Wind turns the sails and drive shaft, which turns millstones to grind grain. |
| Winnowing | Natural wind or a wind-assisted winnower | Moving air separates lighter chaff from heavier grain. A manually operated winnower is not automatically a wind-powered machine; the source of the airflow must be identified. |
| Drying produce | Sun and moving air, or a wind-assisted drying arrangement | Air movement helps moisture leave produce. This may use natural wind rather than a windmill, so do not describe every open-air drying activity as mechanically powered by a windmill. |

**Two clear examples for the assessment:** (1) using a windpump to lift water for livestock or irrigation; and (2) using a windmill to drive a grain mill. These are established mechanical uses of windmills.

Wind is not constant. A windpump or windmill works best at a site with a suitable and sufficiently reliable wind resource. A farm may need water storage or a backup pump for periods when the wind is weak.

## 5. Solar power

Solar power is energy from sunlight. Farms can use it in two main ways:

- **Solar photovoltaic (PV) systems** convert sunlight into electricity. The electricity can operate a pump, fan, light or other suitable electrical equipment.
- **Solar-thermal systems** capture the sun's heat directly. A solar crop dryer uses this heat to warm air and remove moisture from produce.

### Farm operations using solar power

| Operation | Solar equipment | How solar energy is used |
|---|---|---|
| Irrigation and water supply | Solar PV water-pumping system | Solar panels generate electricity for a motor that drives a water pump. The water is delivered to a storage tank or irrigation system. |
| Crop drying | Solar crop dryer | Sunlight heats the drying chamber or air collector. Warm air passes around produce and removes moisture. |
| Crop and livestock-product preservation | Solar-assisted dryer or hybrid dryer | Solar heat provides drying energy; some systems use a PV-powered fan to move air through the drying chamber. |
| Farm lighting or small equipment | Solar PV panels, charge controller and battery/inverter where required | Solar electricity can supply suitable lights and small electrical loads. The system must be correctly sized for the equipment. |

**Two clear examples for the assessment:** (1) using solar panels to power a water pump for irrigation; and (2) drying suitable crops in a solar dryer. These are different uses of solar energy: the first uses photovoltaic electricity, while the second uses solar heat.

A World Bank case study describes solar-powered pumps being used by a farmer in Niger to irrigate fields and diversify crops. In Nigeria, FAO reported solar-powered tomato-drying centres and solar-powered pumps in a Kaduna State project. The Nigerian Stored Products Research Institute also provides guidance on operating solar dryers safely and hygienically.

## 6. Compare the four power sources

| Power source | Two or more farm operations | Main equipment | Important limitation or condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanical | Ploughing; threshing; planting; harvesting | Tractor, plough, planter, thresher or engine-driven mill | Requires capital, fuel or energy supply, maintenance and trained operators. |
| Electrical | Irrigation pumping; milking; brooding; feed grinding | Electric pump, milking machine, brooder, motor-driven grinder | Needs a safe and dependable electrical supply; shock and fire hazards must be controlled. |
| Wind | Water pumping; grain milling | Windpump or windmill | Depends on a suitable wind site and sufficiently reliable wind. |
| Solar | Water pumping; crop drying | Solar PV pump; solar-thermal dryer | Output varies with sunlight, weather, system design and storage; equipment has an initial cost. |

## 7. Choosing the right power source

A farmer should not select a power source only because it is modern or renewable. The choice should fit the operation and local conditions.

Consider:

1. **The operation:** Is the task ploughing, pumping water, milking, drying or grinding?
2. **The required power:** How much work must be done, and how quickly?
3. **The site:** Is there a reliable electrical connection, suitable wind, adequate sunlight or a suitable water source?
4. **The scale:** Is the system suitable for a small plot, a medium-sized farm or a large commercial operation?
5. **The cost:** Consider purchase, installation, fuel or electricity, repairs and replacement parts.
6. **Maintenance and skills:** Are spare parts, trained operators and repair services available?
7. **Safety and the environment:** Can the equipment be operated safely without unacceptable pollution, noise, soil damage or risks to people and animals?

### Worked examples

**Example A — A farm needs to lift water and has a suitable windy site but no grid connection.** A windpump may be appropriate if wind measurements and water needs justify it. A solar pump or engine-driven pump may be considered as an alternative or backup. The decision depends on the site and the cost of maintaining the system.

**Example B — A tomato cooperative wants to dry produce while protecting it from dust, insects and rain.** A suitable solar dryer can use sunlight to heat air in a protected chamber. Trays, airflow, loading depth and cooling before packaging affect the quality of the dried produce. A solar dryer is not the same as a solar-powered irrigation pump.

**Example C — A dairy farm needs to milk several cows.** A properly installed electric milking machine can reduce manual effort and support regular milking. The farm must provide safe electricity, clean equipment, maintenance and hygienic milk handling.

**Example D — A farmer must prepare a large field for planting within a short period.** A tractor with a suitable plough, harrow or ridger can carry out the relevant land-preparation operations. The implement must match the soil and operation; a tractor alone does not perform every task.

## 8. Important distinctions and source limitations

- A tractor is a machine; mechanical power from its engine drives or pulls implements.
- A pump is a machine, not a power source. It may be driven by an electric motor, diesel engine, windmill or solar-powered motor.
- A solar PV pump uses electricity generated by solar panels. A solar-thermal dryer uses heat from sunlight. They are not the same technology.
- A windmill that drives a grain mill or pump supplies mechanical power. A modern wind turbine that drives a generator supplies electricity.
- Water used for irrigation, washing produce or seed germination is a farm resource. It is not automatically hydropower. Hydropower specifically involves energy captured from moving or falling water to drive a turbine or waterwheel.
- The original Week Two weekend question asking for the “most common” grain-drying power source in West Africa is not answered by the source's examples. Solar drying and wind-assisted drying are both mentioned, but that does not establish regional prevalence. Do not guess an answer from the examples; the separate regional fact-check remains open.

## 9. Safety reminders

- Operate tractors, harvesters and engine-driven machines only after proper instruction. Keep guards in place and stay away from moving belts, shafts and PTO parts.
- Never refuel a hot engine. Keep fuel away from flames and provide ventilation to prevent exhaust-gas exposure.
- Do not attempt electrical wiring or repairs without a qualified electrician. Isolate equipment before cleaning or maintenance.
- Keep people away from moving windmill blades, shafts and pump linkages. Do not climb a windmill tower.
- Install and maintain solar panels, batteries, controllers and pumps according to the manufacturer's instructions. Protect electrical components from water and accidental contact.
- Never bypass a safety guard or operate equipment with a damaged cable, exposed wire or loose moving part.

## 10. Check your understanding

### Question 1 (4 marks)
Define farm power and explain the difference between a power source and a farm machine.

**Answer:** Farm power is the energy or force used to carry out work on a farm. A power source supplies energy, while a machine uses or converts that energy to perform a task. For example, electricity is the source, an electric pump is the machine, and irrigation pumping is the operation.

**Marking:** 2 marks for a correct definition; 1 mark for explaining a power source; 1 mark for explaining a machine with a suitable example.

### Question 2 (4 marks)
State two farm operations that use mechanical power and name the machine used for each.

**Answer:** Tractor ploughing for land preparation; engine-driven threshing to separate grain. Other valid answers include harrowing, ridging, mechanised planting, combine harvesting and engine-driven milling.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each valid operation and 1 mark for the matching machine, up to 4.

### Question 3 (4 marks)
State two farm operations that use electrical power and name the equipment used.

**Answer:** Pumping water for irrigation using an electric pump; milking using an electric milking machine. Other valid examples include brooder heating, electric feed grinding, refrigeration and ventilation fans.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each valid operation and 1 mark for the matching electrical equipment, up to 4.

### Question 4 (4 marks)
State two farm operations that can be performed using wind power and explain how wind supplies the energy.

**Answer:** A windpump can lift water for irrigation or livestock; a windmill can drive millstones to grind grain. Wind turns the blades or sails, which transfer motion through a shaft or linkage to the pump or mill.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each correct operation; 1 mark for explaining the wind-to-machine motion; 1 mark for correctly connecting the motion to the pump or mill.

### Question 5 (4 marks)
State two farm operations that use solar power and distinguish how solar energy is used in each.

**Answer:** A solar PV system generates electricity to drive an irrigation pump. A solar-thermal dryer captures sunlight as heat to warm air and dry crops.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each correct operation; 1 mark for correctly explaining the PV/electricity use; 1 mark for correctly explaining direct solar heat in drying.

### Question 6 (4 marks)
Explain the difference between a windmill and a wind turbine.

**Answer:** A windmill uses wind to provide mechanical motion directly, for example to pump water or grind grain. A wind turbine uses wind to drive a generator and produce electricity.

**Marking:** 2 marks for the windmill explanation; 2 marks for the wind-turbine explanation.

### Question 7 (8 marks)
Match each operation to the most suitable power source from mechanical, electrical, wind or solar:
a. A tractor pulls a plough to turn the soil.
b. An electric motor drives a milking machine.
c. A windpump raises water from a well.
d. A solar dryer warms air to dry tomatoes.
e. A tractor-powered thresher separates grain.
f. A solar PV pump supplies irrigation water.
g. A windmill turns millstones to grind grain.
h. An electric pump moves water through irrigation pipes.

**Answer:** a. Mechanical; b. Electrical; c. Wind; d. Solar; e. Mechanical; f. Solar; g. Wind; h. Electrical.

**Marking:** 1 mark for each correct source.

### Question 8 (8 marks)
A farmer in a remote area wants to pump water for irrigation. The site has a suitable wind resource and good sunlight, but no grid connection. The farmer also belongs to a cooperative that wants to dry tomatoes. Recommend a suitable power source and equipment for each task. Give two factors the farmer should consider before choosing the equipment.

**Answer:** For irrigation, the farmer could compare a windpump with a solar PV water-pumping system. A windpump uses wind to drive a pump; a solar PV system uses panels to generate electricity for a pump. For the cooperative's tomatoes, a suitable solar dryer can use sunlight as heat to dry the produce in a protected chamber. Factors include local wind and sunlight availability, water demand, equipment and installation cost, maintenance and spare parts, storage/backup requirements, and operator skills.

**Marking:** 2 marks for a suitable wind or solar pumping option and explanation; 2 marks for identifying a suitable solar dryer for tomatoes; 2 marks for two relevant selection factors; 2 marks for explaining how the chosen equipment matches the operation and local conditions.
