## Evidence that labour influences agricultural land use

### Nigeria-specific land-use research
- Taiwo, Olalekan John (2014), **“Determinants of peri-urban and urban agricultural locational choice behaviour in Lagos, Nigeria”**, *Land Use Policy*.
- The study surveyed 300 farmers across ten Lagos localities and identified six broad factors influencing agricultural site selection. One was **availability of labour**.
- It reports that urban farmers prefer sites where labour is relatively available and total production costs are lower.
- The FAO AGRIS record reproduces the study abstract and bibliographic details:
  - [FAO AGRIS — Determinants of peri-urban and urban agricultural locational choice behaviour in Lagos, Nigeria](https://agris.fao.org/search/zh/records/65de5c2d0f3e94b9e5ce543d), lines 18–24 and 31–41.
  - DOI: [10.1016/j.landusepol.2014.02.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landusepol.2014.02.003).

### FAO farming-systems evidence
- FAO's chapter on social and economic issues in semi-arid farming systems identifies labour as an endogenous household input alongside land and capital.
- It states that where population pressure is low, the amount of land farmed may be a function of household size (the labour force) and land quality.
- It further explains that in many parts of West Africa labour rather than land can be the greater constraint on expansion of production, and that seasonal labour bottlenecks can critically influence the level of agricultural activity.
- Source: [FAO — Social and economic issues, Chapter 6](https://www.fao.org/4/v9926e/v9926e08.htm), lines 28–34, 103–108 and 137–156.

## Finding
- Climate, topography and population are source-supported influences.
- Labour availability, labour cost and household labour supply also influence whether land is selected, how much land can be cultivated, which crops can be managed and whether cultivation can expand.
- The Nigeria-specific Lagos study directly identifies labour availability as a determinant of agricultural site selection. FAO's regional evidence confirms that household labour supply and seasonal labour constraints affect the area farmed and expansion in West Africa.
- Therefore, **A. Labour is not a defensible factual answer** to “The use of land for agriculture is not influenced by…”. All four options can influence agricultural land use.
- Labour is simply omitted from the Week Seven source list; this omission does not establish that it has no effect.
