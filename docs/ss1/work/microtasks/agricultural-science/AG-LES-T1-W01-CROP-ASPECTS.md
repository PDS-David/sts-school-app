## Lesson body

Week 1 Supplement: Three Crop-Science Disciplines

The Week 1 source lists agronomy, crop pathology and entomology as aspects of crop production but does not define them. This supplement explains what each discipline studies and how each helps farming.

## 1. Agronomy

Agronomy is the science of crop production and soil management. It studies how crops grow and how soil and field conditions affect them. Agronomists consider matters such as crop choice, soil properties, planting practices, crop rotation, weed management and ways to maintain productive soil. Their work helps farmers make informed decisions about growing crops and caring for the land.

## 2. Crop Pathology

Crop pathology, also called plant pathology when the subject is plants generally, is the study of diseases that affect crops. It investigates what causes plant diseases, how they develop and spread, the damage they cause, and how they can be prevented or managed. Causes may include fungi, bacteria, viruses, plant-parasitic nematodes and harmful non-living conditions such as nutrient imbalance or air pollutants.

For example, when maize or cassava plants show signs of disease, crop pathology helps identify the cause and understand how the disease affects the crop.

**Important distinction:** Crop pathology deals with diseases of plants and crops. Diseases of farm animals belong to veterinary pathology. The source's broader description of pathology as covering both crops and animals should not be used as the definition of crop pathology.

## 3. Entomology

Entomology is the scientific study of insects. Agricultural entomology applies this knowledge to farming. Some insects damage crops by feeding on leaves, stems, roots, flowers or stored produce. Other insects are useful: bees and other pollinators help some crops produce fruits and seeds, while predatory insects feed on crop pests.

An entomologist studies insects, their identification, life cycles, behaviour and effects on crops. This knowledge helps farmers recognise harmful insects and protect beneficial ones.

## How the Three Disciplines Work Together

Consider a maize field:
- An agronomist considers the crop, soil and field practices needed for good growth.
- A crop pathologist investigates a disease affecting the maize plants.
- An entomologist identifies insects feeding on the crop or insects that help control those pests.

The disciplines are related, but they ask different questions: agronomy focuses on crop production and soil management; crop pathology focuses on crop diseases; entomology focuses on insects.

## Key Points to Remember

- Agronomy: science of crop production and soil management.
- Crop pathology: study of diseases affecting crops and other plants.
- Entomology: scientific study of insects, including harmful and beneficial insects.
- Veterinary pathology, not crop pathology, is the specialist field concerned with animal diseases.

## Objectives

1. Define agronomy as the science of crop production and soil management.
2. Define crop pathology and explain that it studies diseases affecting crops and other plants.
3. Define entomology and describe its relevance to harmful and beneficial insects in agriculture.
4. Distinguish crop pathology from veterinary pathology.

## Pathology scope decision

The supplement teaches **crop pathology as the study of diseases affecting crops and other plants only**. Animal diseases belong to veterinary pathology. This distinction is supported by the American Phytopathological Society's definition of plant pathology and the American College of Veterinary Pathologists' definition of veterinary pathology. The broad statement at source blocks 131–132 is retained in the archived source but is not repeated as the definition of crop pathology in this supplement.
