## Lesson body

Week 6 Supplement: Agricultural Storage Structures and the Supply Chain

The Week 6 source explains that industries provide storage facilities, but it does not fully match particular structures with the produce stored in them. This original supplement supplies those examples and explains why storage is important between harvesting, processing and sale. It adds teaching support; it does not rewrite the original source.

## 1. Meaning and purpose of agricultural storage

An agricultural storage structure is a building, enclosure or designed storage system used to keep harvested crops for a period before they are processed, transported, sold or used. A suitable structure helps protect produce from avoidable damage and deterioration.

Storage is an important link in the agricultural supply chain:

1. Farmers harvest crops.
2. Produce is handled and taken to a suitable storage facility.
3. The produce is kept until it is needed by a processor, trader, retailer or consumer.
4. It is then moved to processing, distribution or sale.

Good storage helps to maintain produce quality, reduce post-harvest losses and make supplies available beyond the exact time of harvest. It can also help farmers and cooperatives collect produce from different farms before sending it to a market or processing industry.

## 2. Examples of storage structures and the produce stored in them

### A. Improved inert-atmosphere silo

A silo is a large storage structure used mainly for bulk grain. An improved inert-atmosphere silo is designed for longer-term storage of dry grains. Examples of suitable produce include maize, rice, sorghum and wheat. NSPRI identifies improved inert-atmosphere silos as a technology for long-term grain storage.

### B. Improved ventilated crib

A ventilated crib is a structure that allows air to move around produce stored inside it. NSPRI identifies its improved ventilated crib for drying and storing unthreshed grains, particularly maize still on the cob. This is different from a silo that stores bulk grain after it has been separated from the cob.

### C. Improved yam barn

A yam barn is a structure used to store yam tubers after curing. Curing is the post-harvest process that allows harvested yams to develop a protective surface before storage. NSPRI explains that its improved yam-barn design allows stored yams to be monitored closely, helping to prevent sprouting and spoilage. FAO also describes the yam barn as a traditional West African structure for storing yams after curing.

### D. Cassava warehouse

A cassava warehouse is a storage system intended for freshly harvested cassava roots. NSPRI lists a cassava-warehouse technology for fresh roots. The exact storage method and period depend on the designed system and its operating conditions; learners should not assume that every ordinary warehouse can preserve fresh cassava in the same way.

### E. Cold room

A cold room is an insulated room with a controlled cool environment. It is used for perishable produce that deteriorates quickly under unsuitable conditions. Examples include many fruits and vegetables. Nigeria's Postharvest Systems Transformation Programme identifies cold rooms as part of the storage infrastructure planned for the country. A cold room is not suitable for every crop: the storage conditions must match the produce.

### F. Community warehouse

A community warehouse is a shared storage facility serving farmers, cooperatives or a local produce-trading community. It can receive bagged dry produce such as grains, legumes and seeds for aggregation, temporary storage and later distribution. The exact commodities accepted depend on the warehouse design and management. Nigeria's Postharvest Systems Transformation Programme identifies community-level warehouses and strategic national silos as parts of the national storage system.

## 3. How industries provide storage

Storage infrastructure can be provided in different ways:

- Agricultural storage companies may build and operate warehouses or cold rooms.
- Engineering and equipment businesses may supply silo systems and other storage infrastructure.
- Cooperatives and farmer groups may organise shared warehouses for members.
- Public research institutions such as NSPRI develop and demonstrate post-harvest technologies.
- Government and private organisations may work together to finance or manage storage infrastructure.

The Nigerian Government's post-harvest programme describes community-level warehouses, cold rooms and strategic national silos managed through public-private partnerships. This is an example of how public institutions and private businesses can work together to provide storage services.

A traditional yam barn is a farm-level storage structure; an improved yam barn is a developed storage technology. Not every storage structure is a factory-made machine. The important point is that suitable structures and storage services can be provided by agricultural industries, research institutions, cooperatives and public-private programmes.

## 4. Choosing a suitable storage structure

Different crops have different storage needs. Dry grains, unthreshed maize cobs, cured yams, fresh cassava roots and fresh vegetables should not automatically be placed in the same type of store.

When selecting a facility, consider the type of produce, whether it is dry or fresh, how easily it spoils, the need for ventilation or a controlled cool environment, how long it is expected to remain in storage, and whether the facility can protect it from moisture, pests and physical damage.

FAO explains that storage quality depends on factors such as temperature, relative humidity and air circulation. Produce stored together should also be compatible with the conditions in the store. Therefore, the correct facility depends on the crop and the purpose of storage.

Remember: a miller or sheller processes produce; it does not store it. A storage structure keeps produce for a period before its next stage in the supply chain.

## 5. Quick revision

- Silo — bulk dry grains such as maize, rice, sorghum and wheat.
- Improved ventilated crib — unthreshed maize, especially maize on the cob.
- Improved yam barn — cured yam tubers.
- Cassava warehouse — fresh cassava roots in a system designed for that purpose.
- Cold room — suitable perishable fruits and vegetables.
- Community warehouse — bagged grains, legumes, seeds and other accepted durable produce.

The structures are not interchangeable. A farmer, cooperative or storage operator should select a facility that suits the produce and its intended storage period.

### P2 — Supply-chain roles (6 marks)

**Question:** Explain three ways in which agricultural storage structures support the supply chain.

**Answer:** They help maintain produce quality; reduce avoidable post-harvest losses; and allow produce to be collected and held until processors, traders or consumers need it, helping supplies become more regular.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Explains that storage helps maintain produce quality.
- 2 marks — Explains that suitable storage helps reduce post-harvest losses.
- 2 marks — Explains aggregation or planned supply to processors, traders or consumers.

### P3 — Select a suitable structure (10 marks)

**Question:** Choose a suitable storage structure for each produce item and give a reason: (a) dry maize grain in bulk; (b) unthreshed maize cobs; (c) cured yam tubers; (d) fresh cassava roots; (e) fresh tomatoes.

**Answer:** (a) Improved inert-atmosphere silo, designed for bulk dry-grain storage. (b) Improved ventilated crib, used for unthreshed maize on the cob. (c) Improved yam barn, used for cured yam tubers and monitoring them. (d) Cassava warehouse, an NSPRI-listed system for fresh cassava roots. (e) Cold room, where designed and managed for tomatoes, because they are perishable and require suitable storage conditions.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — Selects an improved inert-atmosphere silo for bulk dry maize and gives a suitable reason.
- 2 marks — Selects an improved ventilated crib for unthreshed maize cobs and gives a suitable reason.
- 2 marks — Selects an improved yam barn for cured yams and gives a suitable reason.
- 2 marks — Selects a cassava warehouse for fresh cassava roots and gives a suitable reason.
- 2 marks — Selects a suitable cold room for fresh tomatoes and gives a suitable reason.

### P4 — Storage compatibility (4 marks)

**Question:** Why should different agricultural products not automatically be stored together in one type of facility? Give two reasons.

**Answer:** Different products have different storage requirements. Dry grains, fresh vegetables and cured yams do not all need the same temperature, humidity or ventilation. A facility that suits one product may cause another product to deteriorate, so the structure and conditions must match the produce.

**Marking:**
- 2 marks — States that different products have different storage requirements, such as temperature, humidity or ventilation.
- 2 marks — Explains that unsuitable shared conditions may cause quality loss or spoilage.
