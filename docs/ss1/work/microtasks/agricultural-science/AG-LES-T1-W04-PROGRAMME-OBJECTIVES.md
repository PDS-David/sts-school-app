## Lesson body

Week 4 Supplement: Nigerian Agricultural Programmes and Their Objectives

A government agricultural policy states broad goals for the country. An agricultural programme is a planned way of working towards one or more of those goals. A programme has its own specific objectives; the broad goals of agricultural policy should not be copied as if they were the objectives of every programme.

## Five Nigerian Agricultural Programmes

1. **Farm Settlement Scheme (historical):**
   - Encourage educated young people and school leavers to choose modern farming as a career.
   - Establish planned farm settlements that demonstrate viable farming and support rural progress. The historical Western Region scheme included farm-institute training, allocation of farm holdings and supervised financial assistance.

2. **Agricultural Development Projects (ADPs):**
   - Increase agricultural production.
   - Increase rural incomes and improve the living standards of farming families through affordable, adaptable and proven technologies, extension and training.

3. **River Basin Development Authorities (RBDAs):**
   - Develop and manage Nigeria's surface and underground water resources.
   - Provide irrigation infrastructure and related water-management works, including flood control, to support agriculture and rural development.

4. **National Agricultural Insurance Scheme (NAIS), administered through the Nigerian Agricultural Insurance Corporation (NAIC):**
   - Protect farmers against losses to crops and livestock caused by natural hazards by providing financial support or indemnity.
   - Encourage financial institutions to provide credit to farmers and promote agricultural production.

5. **Agricultural Credit Guarantee Scheme Fund (ACGSF):**
   - Guarantee loans granted by banks for agricultural purposes, with the aim of increasing bank credit to the agricultural sector.
   - Reduce the risk to banks that lend to agriculture; the Central Bank of Nigeria states that the scheme guarantees 75 per cent of the net amount in default under the scheme's rules.

## Resolving the Farm Settlement Scheme Question

The Week 4 weekend question asks which programme was designed to attract young educated people to take up farming. The correct option is **B. Farm Settlement Scheme**. A historical account written by the Western Region Ministry of Agriculture describes the scheme as designed to appeal to educated young men and school leavers. Trainees attended farm institutes and were then allocated farm holdings with supervised financial support. A later appraisal identifies rural progress, viable farms operated by educated young farmers and reducing school-leaver unemployment among the scheme's objectives.

## Programme Objectives and Broad Policy Goals

Broad agricultural policy goals in the source include increasing food production, improving export-crop quality, creating employment, supplying raw materials to local industries and increasing livestock production. These are national goals.

Programme-specific objectives are narrower and linked to the programme's purpose. For example:
- Increasing water resources and irrigation infrastructure is a specific RBDA objective.
- Protecting farmers from natural hazards is a specific NAIS/NAIC objective.
- Guaranteeing agricultural bank loans is a specific ACGSF objective.
- Attracting educated young people into planned farm settlements is a specific Farm Settlement Scheme objective.

The five programmes above are examples from the programme categories named in the source. ACGSF is a named example of the agricultural loan and credit-facility category. The historical Farm Settlement Scheme is not presented as a current programme.

## Programme-specific evidence and source distinction

- ADP: the FCT Administration identifies increased agricultural production, rural income and farm-family living standards as project objectives.
- RBDAs: the Lower Benue RBDA identifies water-resource development and irrigation infrastructure for agriculture.
- NAIS/NAIC: the Nigerian Agricultural Insurance Corporation Act states farmer protection against natural hazards, financial support for crop/livestock losses, encouraging credit and promoting production.
- ACGSF: the Central Bank of Nigeria states that it guarantees agricultural loans to increase credit and mitigate banks' lending risk.
- Broad policy goals (food production, export quality, employment, industrial raw materials and livestock protein) are not treated as the objectives of every named programme.
