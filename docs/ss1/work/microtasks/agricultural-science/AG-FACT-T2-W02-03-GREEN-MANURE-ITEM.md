## Agronomic timing evidence

### Missouri Extension — just before full flowering
- Missouri Extension says legumes should be ploughed/incorporated **just before full flowering** for maximum nitrogen content.
- This supports the source's “just before flowering” phrasing, interpreted as just before full bloom rather than long before the crop has developed sufficient biomass.
- Source: [University of Missouri Extension — Organic Vegetable Gardening Techniques](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g6220), cover-crop management section.

### Alberta Agriculture — full bloom
- Alberta Agriculture's legume green-manure guidance recommends incorporation at **full bloom** to maximize top growth and nitrogen fixation while minimizing soil-moisture depletion.
- It explains that after full bloom the material becomes tougher and takes longer to decompose; very young material may decompose too quickly, making released nitrogen more vulnerable to leaching and volatilization.
- Source: [Government of Alberta — Legume Green Manuring](https://www1.agric.gov.ab.ca/$department/deptdocs.nsf/all/agdex133), “Timing of incorporation”.

### Washington State University Extension — late vegetative/early flowering
- WSU Extension identifies **late-vegetative and early-flowering stages** as the “sweet spot” for nitrogen supply: sufficient plant material has developed, but tissue nitrogen has not yet declined substantially with maturity.
- Source: [WSU Extension — Methods for Successful Cover Crop Management in Your Home Garden](https://pubs.extension.wsu.edu/product/methods-for-successful-cover-crop-management-in-your-home-garden-home-garden-series/).

### FAO — timing depends on objective and crop
- FAO technical guidance says legume cover crops/green manures may be incorporated at **mid-flowering or grain-filling** to maximize nitrogen contribution in some systems. It also notes that the precise timing and method depend on the cropping system and require local adaptation.
- FAO terminology defines green manure as a crop grown to maintain soil organic matter and increase nitrogen availability, often a legume, whose biomass is incorporated into the soil.
- Sources:
  - [FAO — Integrated soil and plant nutrient management, Annex 2](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/nr/images/resources/pdf_documents/misc27.pdf), p. 94.
  - [FAO TERM — Green manure definition](https://faoterm.fao.org/viewEntry.html?entryId=182817&language=en).

## Finding
- The practical recommendation is not “incorporate at any point after the first flower appears” or “plough in long before flowering”. It is to incorporate the crop around **late vegetative to early/full flowering**, once sufficient biomass has developed and before seed formation/advanced maturity.
- The best stage varies with species, maturity, soil moisture, biomass and the intended benefit:
  - For maximum nitrogen concentration and rapid nutrient release, many extension sources favour just before or around full bloom.
  - If the objective is maximum biomass/organic matter, some systems allow the stand to reach mid-flowering or later, while avoiding seed set and excessively woody material.
- Therefore block 175's “just before flowering” and block 268's “as soon as it flowers” are close enough to be reconciled as the early-flowering/full-bloom window. The phrase “as soon as it flowers” is too casual for a precise agronomic instruction, but it does not invalidate the concept being tested.
- The correct answer to MCQ 268 remains **C. Green manuring**.

## Recommendation — no teacher decision presumed
Keep the concept and answer key, but harmonize the timing language in both places. Suggested wording:

> **Green manure is a crop, usually a legume, grown and then incorporated into the soil at or just before full bloom (early flowering), before seed formation, to add organic matter and nutrients. The exact stage depends on the species and whether the main objective is nitrogen supply or organic matter.**

For the MCQ, replace “as soon as it flowers” with **“at or just before full bloom, before seed formation”**. This preserves the intended answer C while avoiding the impression that any flowering stage is equally suitable.
