## Evidence by option

### A. Quarantine — a valid preventive pest-management measure
- The International Plant Protection Convention's FAO-published ISPM 5 defines **plant quarantine** as all activities designed to prevent the introduction or spread of quarantine pests or ensure their official control.
- Quarantine is principally a regulatory/preventive measure rather than a field treatment for an established infestation. It is still a recognised plant-protection measure.
- Source: [FAO/IPPC, ISPM 5 — Glossary of phytosanitary terms (2019)](https://www.ippc.int/static/media/files/publication/en/2019/06/ISPM_05_2019_En_Glossary_2019-06-26_PostCPM-14-Fixed.pdf), “plant quarantine” definition, PDF p. 18.

### B. Crop rotation — a valid cultural pest-management measure
- FAO's Integrated Pest Management guidance identifies crop rotation/intercropping as measures for prevention or suppression of harmful organisms.
- It explains that crop rotation can reduce pest and disease pressure by breaking host continuity, reducing inoculum and interrupting infection chains between successive crops.
- The source's Week Six statement only mentions soil fertility, yield and land use; the external FAO reference confirms the additional pest-management function.
- Source: [FAO — How to practice Integrated Pest Management](https://www.fao.org/agriculture/crops/thematic-sitemap/theme/compendium/tools-guidelines/how-to-ipm/en/), lines 17–23 and 31–38.

### C. Fumigation — a valid chemical pest-control method
- FAO's *Manual of Fumigation for Insect Control* states that fumigation is used to prevent or control insect infestations and remains a valuable technique in pest-control operations.
- The manual discusses fumigation for insects above ground and stored-product pests. Its scope is specific; it is not a universal treatment for every field pest.
- Source: [FAO — Manual of Fumigation for Insect Control](https://www.fao.org/4/x5042e/x5042e01.htm), preface and introduction, especially paragraphs on the role of fumigation in pest control.

### D. Mulching — can be a valid physical/cultural pest-control method
- “Mulching” is broad; not every mulch or application controls insects. However, **reflective mulch** is a recognised pest-management technique.
- UC Agriculture and Natural Resources' Statewide IPM Program says reflective mulch delays or prevents certain flying insects from infesting crops and reduces colonisation by winged aphids, leafhoppers, thrips and whiteflies. It can also reduce insect-vectored disease infection.
- This establishes that mulching cannot categorically be marked as “not a method of controlling pests”. The material and pest must be specified; some other mulches can have different effects.
- Source: [UC Statewide IPM Program — Reflective Mulches](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/agriculture/floriculture-and-ornamental-nurseries/reflective-mulches/), updated March 2021.

## Finding
All four options can be used in pest management, though they act differently and target different problems:
- quarantine — regulatory prevention/exclusion of regulated pests;
- crop rotation — cultural suppression of pests and diseases tied to particular host crops;
- fumigation — chemical control of susceptible insects and other pests, especially in stored products;
- reflective mulching — physical deterrence of certain flying insects and their associated diseases.

The stem does not identify a pest, crop, production stage, mulch type or control objective. Because each option can be valid in a defined context, none is categorically the one method that is “not” used to control pests. A generic mulch is not always effective, but the same contextual limitation applies to the other methods.
