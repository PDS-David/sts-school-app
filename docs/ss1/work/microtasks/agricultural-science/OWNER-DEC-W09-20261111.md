## Approved decisions

| No. | Evidence-brief recommendation | Recorded decision |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Farmstead means the built-up home and operating centre of a farm; it is distinct from the whole farm, which includes production land and facilities. | APPROVED |
| 2 | Use the five grouped farmstead-planning principles: suitable site; access and efficient movement; water/utilities/drainage/sanitation; climate-aware orientation, ventilation and hygiene; safe arrangement and provision for expansion. The five headings are a school-level grouping, not a universal exhaustive list. | APPROVED |
| 3 | Teach the eight listed school-level functions of a government forestry department: forest management/conservation; harvest and trade regulation; regeneration and planting; protection; biodiversity/wildlife; monitoring; advice/training/extension; sustainable use and renewal. Distinguish federal and state responsibilities. | APPROVED |
| 4 | Define taungya as temporary food-crop cultivation among young forest-tree seedlings during plantation establishment. Include the individual/traditional and departmental/direct forms as Nigerian examples, without describing it as permanent cropping under a mature closed canopy. | APPROVED |
| 5 | Teach the eight forest-use categories: timber/construction; fuel; industrial raw materials; food; medicinal/household materials; wildlife habitat/biodiversity; soil/water protection; climate/recreation/livelihoods. These categories overlap and include both products and ecosystem services. | APPROVED |
| 6 | Define forest regulation as laws, rules and administrative controls governing forest protection, harvesting, movement and trade in forest produce. Use state law only as a local example, not as the entire national framework. | APPROVED |
| 7 | Correct the learner-facing term “selective exploration” to the standard forestry term **selective exploitation** (or selective felling). Explain that selective felling is not automatically sustainable. The archived source wording remains unchanged. | APPROVED |
| 8 | Distinguish afforestation (establishing forest on land not previously forest) from reforestation (re-establishing forest on previously forested land). | APPROVED |
| 9 | For the short SS1 answer, define primary forest products as direct/raw forest outputs and use examples such as logs, poles, fuelwood, fruits, nuts, gums and medicinal plants. Distinguish these from further manufactured products such as furniture. Note that FAO statistical classification may include certain first-stage processed products as primary products. | APPROVED |
