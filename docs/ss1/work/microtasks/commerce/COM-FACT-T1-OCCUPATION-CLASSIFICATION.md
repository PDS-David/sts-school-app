## Independent authoritative evidence
1. **NCERT Business Studies, Chapter 1, reprint 2026–27**: the official text classifies tertiary industries as services supporting primary and secondary industries and activities relating to trade. It explicitly includes transport, banking, insurance, warehousing, communication and advertising. Its Commerce section defines Commerce as trade plus auxiliaries to trade.
   - URL: https://www.ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/kebs101.pdf
   - Accessed: 2026-11-13.
2. **Jharkhand Ministry of Higher Education, Science and Technology, Commerce for SS1, Chapter 1**: the official SS1 Commerce text describes productive activities as industrial sector, commerce and direct-services sector; it calls commerce the third category and lists transport, communication, warehousing, advertising, banking and insurance as supportive services to trade.
   - URL: https://www.mohest.jg.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Commerce-for-SS1-TXT.pdf
   - Accessed: 2026-11-13.

## Finding
- In the standard primary–secondary–tertiary model, commercial activities and auxiliaries to trade belong to the **tertiary** sector, not the secondary sector.
- The source's own list separates extractive activity (primary-type), manufacturing and construction (secondary-type), and then commercial/direct/indirect services. Its label “secondary occupation” for commercial occupation is inconsistent with the standard three-sector classification and with the official curriculum evidence above.
- The source does not establish a distinct, defined two-way “primary vs secondary occupation” system that would make this label unambiguous. The defensible teaching correction is therefore to explain that the source's label is non-standard/inconsistent and teach commerce and supporting services as tertiary in the three-sector model.
- Do not silently alter the original school wording. Preserve the quote and teach the correction in a visibly separate note.
