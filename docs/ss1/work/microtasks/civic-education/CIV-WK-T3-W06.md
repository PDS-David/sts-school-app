## Factual, rights and neutrality flags

1. “Respect” must not be translated into unconditional obedience. Future teaching should distinguish lawful authority, constitutional limits, rights and safe/responsible dissent.
2. The statement that appointed/elected leaders necessarily hold authority with the consent of the people needs careful explanation; appointment, election, recognition and legitimacy are not interchangeable in every context.
3. The examples of traditional and religious authorities are culturally/religiously specific and should be framed inclusively, without implying that every learner belongs to the named tradition or faith.
4. The label “legal authority” and the claim that authority emanates from offices rather than officeholders need constitutional/institutional examples and precise scope before being taught as a legal rule.
5. The international-organisation category (United Nations, African Union, ECOWAS) needs an explanation of the limited, treaty-based or delegated scope of any authority; do not imply these bodies are Nigerian domestic constitutional authorities.
6. The importance claims at p271–276 are broad propositions. In particular, p273's public-money/facilities claim and p275–276's traditional/religious roles require accurate context and should not be presented as exhaustive.
7. Preserve the source's spelling `CONSTITUED` at p270 in evidence; do not silently correct the source text.
8. The reading assignment references an external SS2 textbook not supplied in this archive.
