## Findings against the enacted texts

### 1. Registration and voter qualification — source L111

**Applicable text**
- Constitution sections 77(2) and 117(2): a Nigerian citizen who has attained 18 years and is resident in Nigeria at the time of registration is entitled to be registered for the relevant legislative election.
- Electoral Act 2026 section 12(1): a registrant must be a Nigerian citizen, at least 18, ordinarily resident in, work in, and originate from the Local Government / Area Council / Ward covered by the registration centre, present personally for registration, and not be subject to a legal incapacity to vote. Section 12(2) prohibits multiple registrations.
- Electoral Act 2026 sections 9–10 provide for the National Register of Voters and continuous registration, subject to the statutory pre-election cut-off.

**Assessment**
The source's age-18 point is directionally correct but materially incomplete. The statutory test is not simply “18 years and of sane mind”: citizenship, the local connection in section 12(1)(c), personal appearance and absence of legal incapacity are also stated. “Of sane mind” is not the wording of section 12(1). Do not silently replace the source wording in this task.

### 2. Public display of voters' register — source L113

**Applicable text**
- Electoral Act 2026 section 19(1): not later than 90 days to a general election, INEC appoints a seven-day period for copies of the voters' register for each Local Government, Area Council or Ward to be displayed/published for public scrutiny at registration areas and on the Commission's website.
- Section 19(2) allows objections, complaints and correction requests within 14 days after display; section 19(3) describes objections and claims.

**Assessment**
Public display is supported, but the source omits the statutory timing, seven-day display period, publication channels and 14-day claims/objections window. It should not be presented as an unqualified continuous display obligation.

### 3. Voter card, accreditation and voting — source L105, L127, L131, L135

**Applicable text**
- Electoral Act 2026 section 16: INEC designs, prints and controls issuance of voters' cards.
- Section 47(1): a person intending to vote presents a Permanent Voter's Card (PVC) for accreditation at the polling unit in the constituency in which the person's name is registered.
- Section 47(2): the Presiding Officer uses BVAS or another technological device prescribed by INEC to verify, confirm or authenticate the intending voter's particulars.
- Section 50(1)–(4): voting is by open secret ballot; the voter marks the ballot in the manner prescribed by INEC; ballots are deposited in the ballot box in public view.
- Section 52(1)–(2): a ballot paper bearing an identifying mark is rejected, but a print caused by thumb staining in the voting compartment is not deemed an identifying mark; INEC is to use indelible ink for any thumb mark by voters on ballot papers.
- Section 55 requires personal attendance to record a vote; section 56 confines voting to the polling unit to which the voter is allotted.

**Assessment**
The card-and-accreditation sequence is supported, but the current statute specifies a PVC and technology-based accreditation (BVAS or another INEC-prescribed device). A thumb mark on the ballot is contemplated by section 52; it is not the statutory description of voter accreditation. “Secretly drops it” needs the distinction in section 50: the ballot is secret, while the deposit of the ballot paper into the box is in open view. The source's reference to party names/logos on the ballot is not assessed as a complete ballot-design specification here; section 50 leaves the prescribed marking procedure to INEC.

### 4. INEC's constitutional status, election remit and local-government distinction — source L193

**Applicable text**
- Constitution section 153(1) and Third Schedule Part I, Item F: INEC is a federal constitutional commission. Third Schedule Part I, Item F, paragraph 14 sets its composition and constitutional powers.
- Paragraph 14(2)(a) empowers INEC to organise, undertake and supervise elections for President/Vice-President, State Governors/Deputy Governors, Senate, House of Representatives and State Houses of Assembly.
- Constitution sections 78 and 118 place voter registration and conduct of elections under INEC's direction and supervision.
- Constitution Third Schedule Part II, Item B, paragraphs 3–4 establishes the State Independent Electoral Commission (SIEC) in each State and empowers it to organise, undertake and supervise elections to local government councils within that State.
- Electoral Act 2026 Part VI governs FCT Area Council elections. Section 153 addresses the procedure for those elections and requires procedural equivalence with State Commission local-government election procedures.

**Assessment**
INEC is a permanent constitutional body, but the source overstates its state/local-government remit. INEC conducts the federal and State-level elections listed in the Constitution and the FCT Area Council elections under the 2026 Act. Elections to local government councils within a State are assigned constitutionally to that State's SIEC, not generally to INEC.

### 5. INEC functions and constituency delimitation — source L199, L201, L203

**Applicable text**
- Constitution Third Schedule Part I, Item F, paragraph 14(2)(b)–(i): INEC registers political parties; monitors their organisation and operation, including finances, conventions, congresses and primaries; arranges annual examination/audit of party funds and accounts; monitors political campaigns and provides party rules; registers qualified voters and maintains/revises the register; and performs other functions conferred by law.
- Electoral Act 2026 sections 75, 82–84 and 90–91 add statutory detail on party registration, notice/observation of conventions and congresses, monitoring of party activities and primaries, annual financial statements and contribution limits.
- Constitution sections 71–74 govern Senatorial districts and Federal constituencies, including INEC's division and periodic review; sections 112–115 govern State constituencies and their periodic review.

**Assessment**
The source's voter registration and delimitation themes are supported, with the constitutional boundaries and review duties stated more precisely in sections 71–74 and 112–115. “Control political parties” is an imprecise shorthand: the Constitution and 2026 Act specify registration, monitoring, audit, campaign and compliance powers; they do not use that broad phrase as a complete statement of INEC's role.

### 6. SIEC remit, composition and appointment — source L211, L213, L215, L217

**Applicable text**
- Constitution section 197(1)–(2): establishes a SIEC for each State and refers its composition and powers to Part II of the Third Schedule.
- Section 198: except where the Constitution provides otherwise, the SIEC chairman and members are appointed by the Governor of the State, subject to confirmation by resolution of that State's House of Assembly.
- Section 200(1)(a): a SIEC member must not be a member of a political party.
- Third Schedule Part II, Item B, paragraph 3: a SIEC comprises a Chairman and **not less than five but not more than seven** other persons.
- Third Schedule Part II, Item B, paragraph 4(a): SIEC organises, undertakes and supervises all elections to local government councils within the State.
- Paragraph 4(b): SIEC may render advice to INEC on compilation of and the voters' register insofar as that register applies to local government elections in the State.

**Assessment**
The source is correct that SIEC conducts State local-government council elections and may advise INEC about the relevant voter register. The composition and appointment statement at L217 is incorrect in two material respects: the Constitution prescribes a range of five to seven other persons, not invariably seven; and the Governor appoints the Chairman and members subject to State House of Assembly confirmation. INEC does not appoint SIEC members.
