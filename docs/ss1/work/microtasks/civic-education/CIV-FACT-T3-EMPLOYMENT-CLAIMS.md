## Current official labour-market definitions

The National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) Nigeria Labour Force Survey (NLFS) FAQ (April 2023) states:
- **Employed:** work for pay or profit for at least one hour in the previous seven days, or temporary absence from such work.
- **Unemployed:** not employed, actively searching for work and available to start.
- **Active search:** specific job/business-search actions in the previous four weeks.
- **Availability:** available to start last week or within the next two weeks.
- **Underemployment:** employed for one hour or more but less than 40 hours per week, while willing and available to work more.

The NBS definitions follow the ILO labour-force framework and its 19th International Conference of Labour Statisticians (ICLS) resolution (2013). This is materially more precise than “no paid job” or “involuntary idleness”.

**Assessment of p308–309:** The source definitions are incomplete for current labour statistics. A person without a job is not automatically counted as unemployed under the NLFS: they must also be actively searching and available. People outside the labour force are a separate category. The source does not explain the one-hour employment criterion, temporary absence, active search, availability, working-age population or time-related underemployment.

- [ILO, Labour force and the 19th ICLS international guidelines](https://www.ilo.org/resource/labour-force)
- [NBS, Nigeria Labour Force Statistics Report Q2 2024 (published 12 November 2024)](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/pdfuploads/NLFS_Q2_2024.pdf)

## Poverty: definition and measurement

The source's p320 wording is a broad conceptual description, not an official Nigerian poverty threshold or measurement method.

NBS's 2022 National Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) uses a defined multidimensional framework. It includes indicators across health, education, living standards, work and shocks, with specified deprivation cut-offs and weights. The NBS report was released on 17 November 2022 and draws on a survey conducted from November 2021 to February 2022. It is not interchangeable with a monetary poverty line or a general statement about “material needs”.

**Assessment of p320:** Classify it as an informal conceptual description only. Any student-facing definition or statistic must name the chosen measure (for example, monetary poverty or the NBS MPI), its methodology and reference period. No poverty rate is inserted in this record.

## Important distinctions for later lesson work

1. **Employment vs unemployment:** Use the current NBS/ILO statistical framework. “No paid job” alone is insufficient to classify someone as unemployed.
2. **Unemployment vs underemployment:** Underemployment concerns employed people who want and are available to work more hours (NBS time-related measure); it is not the same as unemployment.
3. **Outside the labour force:** People not employed and not actively searching/available are not automatically unemployed under the NLFS.
4. **Full employment:** It does not mean every person must be working. It is a macroeconomic labour-market objective/condition, not an assertion that all children, retirees, carers, students or people unavailable for work are employed.
5. **Poverty vs unemployment:** They are related but not identical. NBS notes that poverty is closely associated with job quality, hours, occupation and formality; its FAQ also cautions that state-level poverty and unemployment may not move together simply.
6. **Full employment vs job guarantee:** Full employment is a macroeconomic condition; a job guarantee is a specific policy commitment to offer eligible people work. The source does not describe such a programme.
7. **Job security vs guaranteed employment:** Protection against arbitrary dismissal is different from guaranteeing that a person will always have a job or that the government will provide a job.
8. **Job-creation measures vs guarantee:** Free education, health care, rural development, agriculture, small businesses and local hiring may be policy inputs; they do not by themselves establish a right to a job or guarantee employment outcomes.
