## Legal sources and jurisdiction/date boundaries

1. **Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999, updated with First–Fifth Alterations (PLAC consolidated third revision, October 2023)** — section 33(2)(a), lines 1040–1045. This is the national constitutional right-to-life provision and includes a lawful-force exception for defence of a person/property; it is not a complete self-defence procedure or a substitute for the applicable criminal law.
2. **Criminal Code Act, Cap C38, Laws of the Federation of Nigeria 2004** — sections 286–288, PLAC consolidated text lines 3932–3943. The Act's original commencement is 1 June 1916 (Act front matter lines 1393–1405). Section 1A expressly saves the Penal Code (Northern States) Federal Provisions Act; section 2 describes the Code's state-law extent and federal matters (lines 1406–1423). The PLAC consolidation is useful statutory text but its crawl is old; confirm later Gazette amendments before final release.
3. **Kaduna State Penal Code Law, 2017, Law No. 5 of 2017** — official Kaduna State Government PDF. Section 2 states commencement on 29 May 2017 (lines 450–467); sections 22–30 address private defence, necessity limits, public-authority limits and circumstances involving serious harm (lines 686–736). This is a state-specific example, not a single Penal Code that can be attributed to every Northern State.

## Required content boundary

- Keep the original definition and advice distinct from any future legally reviewed explanation.
- Do not treat night-club avoidance, friend selection, clothing or gift decisions as statutory conditions for a defence.
- Do not infer or silently correct the garbled word in paragraph 77.
- Do not convert this register into legal advice or encourage physical confrontation.
- Any future lesson correction must be separately sourced, jurisdiction-aware and approved; it must explain that criminal law differs by applicable jurisdiction and statute.
