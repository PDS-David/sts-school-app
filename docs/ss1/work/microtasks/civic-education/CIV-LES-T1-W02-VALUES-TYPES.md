## Canonical lesson body
VALUES IN EVERYDAY CHOICES: FIVE TYPES EXPLAINED

What you will learn: the meaning of hard work, discipline, love, contentment and integrity; the qualities that help you recognise each value; and ordinary actions that show each value in school, at home and in the community.

The source scheme names seven types of value. This supplement concentrates on the five names that the body lists but does not explain: hard work, discipline, love, contentment and integrity. It supplies original teaching for those five only. It does not reproduce or repair the source's separate paragraphs on selflessness or justice.

A value is a principle a person uses to decide what is worth doing and how to treat other people. Values guide choices; they are not just words to memorise. A person shows a value through repeated decisions and actions.

1. HARD WORK

Hard work means applying steady, purposeful effort to a worthwhile task, even when the task takes time or becomes difficult. It is not the same as rushing, pretending to be busy or refusing all rest. A person who works hard plans, practises, checks the quality of the work and keeps going responsibly.

Attributes of hard work:
- Diligence: giving proper attention to the task instead of doing it carelessly.
- Perseverance: continuing after a setback and trying a better method when the first attempt fails.
- Preparation: organising the time, materials and steps needed for the task.
- Care: checking that the finished work is complete and accurate.

Everyday manifestations:
- A student follows a revision plan, attempts difficult questions and checks mistakes rather than copying an answer.
- A group member completes an agreed part of a project and tells the group early if help or more time is needed.
- Someone learning a practical skill practises safely and improves through feedback.

2. DISCIPLINE

Discipline means managing one's behaviour and choices so that responsibilities and fair rules are respected. It includes the ability to pause, think about the result and choose an appropriate action. Discipline is not blind obedience. A person may respectfully question a rule that is unfair or unsafe while still behaving responsibly.

Attributes of discipline:
- Self-management: organising one's time and behaviour without waiting for someone to give every instruction.
- Consistency: following a sensible plan and agreed standards regularly, not only when someone is watching.
- Restraint: pausing before an impulsive action that could harm a person or disrupt a task.
- Accountability: accepting a reasonable consequence and putting a mistake right.

Everyday manifestations:
- A student arrives prepared, keeps to an agreed study timetable and submits work by the agreed date.
- During a practical lesson, a student follows safety instructions and puts equipment away properly.
- In a discussion, a student waits for a turn to speak and disagrees without insults.

3. LOVE

Love, as a civic and everyday value, means showing genuine care and respect for another person's dignity and well-being. It is expressed through considerate actions, not simply through saying that one likes someone. It does not mean controlling another person or allowing harmful behaviour.

Attributes of love:
- Care: noticing when another person needs support and responding appropriately.
- Respect: treating people as worthy of dignity, even when they differ from you.
- Empathy: trying to understand how a situation may feel from another person's position.
- Patience and kindness: responding without humiliation, cruelty or unnecessary anger.

Everyday manifestations:
- A student welcomes a classmate who is often left out and includes them in a group activity.
- A family member listens carefully when someone is worried instead of laughing at them.
- A pupil helps a classmate understand a difficult instruction without doing the whole task for them or making them feel small.

4. CONTENTMENT

Contentment means appreciating what one has and using it responsibly, without allowing envy or pressure to control one's choices. It does not mean refusing to learn, improve or work towards a worthwhile goal. A contented person can aim higher while remaining grateful and fair.

Attributes of contentment:
- Gratitude: recognising and appreciating support, opportunities and useful resources.
- Moderation: avoiding waste and resisting pressure to acquire things only to compete with others.
- Freedom from envy: being able to respect another person's success without wishing them harm.
- Responsible use: taking care of what is available instead of treating it carelessly.

Everyday manifestations:
- A student looks after school materials and uses them carefully rather than damaging them to impress friends.
- A pupil congratulates a classmate who receives an award and continues working on their own goals.
- A young person refuses pressure to take another person's property just to own the same item.

5. INTEGRITY

Integrity means keeping one's actions consistent with the principles one says one follows, including when no one is checking. A person with integrity takes responsibility for choices and does not claim another person's work or advantage as their own.

Attributes of integrity:
- Consistency: applying the same fair standard in private and in public.
- Responsibility: accepting one's part in a mistake and taking steps to correct it.
- Reliability: keeping reasonable commitments or explaining promptly when they cannot be kept.
- Moral courage: choosing the right course even when an easier shortcut is available.

Everyday manifestations:
- A student returns an item found in the classroom instead of keeping it.
- A group member gives credit to the classmate who completed a piece of the project.
- A pupil admits that an answer was copied and agrees to redo the work.

6. TELLING THE FIVE VALUES APART

The values can work together, but they answer different questions:
- Hard work asks: am I applying purposeful effort to the task?
- Discipline asks: am I managing my behaviour and meeting fair responsibilities?
- Love asks: am I treating other people with care and respect?
- Contentment asks: am I appreciating what I have without being driven by envy?
- Integrity asks: do my actions match the principles and commitments I claim to follow?

Example: a student preparing for a test may show hard work by practising, discipline by following a study timetable, contentment by not envying another student's materials, love by helping a classmate respectfully, and integrity by submitting their own work. One action may show more than one value, but the meanings are not identical.

## Assessment questions, complete answers and marking criteria
### 1. Explain the difference between hard work and discipline. Give one school example of each.

**Answer:** Hard work is steady, purposeful effort towards a task, including preparation, practice and checking the quality of the work. Discipline is managing one's behaviour and choices so that fair rules and responsibilities are respected. A student who practises difficult Mathematics questions and checks errors shows hard work. A student who follows an agreed study timetable, arrives prepared and submits the work by the agreed date shows discipline.

**Marks:** 4

- Defines hard work as purposeful effort towards a task — 1
- Defines discipline as responsible management of behaviour and choices — 1
- Gives a relevant school example of hard work — 1
- Gives a relevant school example of discipline — 1

### 2. What do love and contentment mean as everyday values? Give one practical manifestation of each.

**Answer:** Love means showing care and respect for another person's dignity and well-being. One manifestation is including a classmate who is often left out and helping them take part without embarrassing them. Contentment means appreciating what one has and using it responsibly without allowing envy or pressure to control one's choices. One manifestation is looking after school materials and refusing pressure to take another person's property just to own the same item.

**Marks:** 4

- Defines love in terms of care and respect for another person's dignity — 1
- Gives a practical manifestation of love — 1
- Defines contentment in terms of appreciation and responsible use — 1
- Gives a practical manifestation of contentment — 1

### 3. Define integrity and describe two actions that show integrity in school.

**Answer:** Integrity means keeping one's actions consistent with the principles one says one follows, including when no one is checking. A student shows integrity by returning an item found in the classroom instead of keeping it, and by giving a group member credit for the work that person completed. A student also shows integrity by admitting a copied answer and agreeing to redo the work.

**Marks:** 4

- Defines integrity as consistency between stated principles and actions — 1
- Gives one relevant school action that shows integrity — 1
- Gives a second distinct relevant school action — 1
- Explains how at least one action shows responsibility or consistency — 1

### 4. Discuss any four of the five values taught in this supplement. For each value, give its meaning, two attributes and one everyday manifestation.

**Answer:** Hard work is steady, purposeful effort towards a worthwhile task. Its attributes include diligence and perseverance. A student shows it by following a revision plan, practising difficult questions and checking mistakes. Discipline is managing behaviour and choices so that responsibilities and fair rules are respected. Its attributes include consistency and accountability. A student shows it by following an agreed study timetable and completing work by the agreed date. Love is genuine care and respect for another person's dignity and well-being. Its attributes include empathy and kindness. A student shows it by including a classmate who is often left out and helping them without humiliation. Contentment is appreciating what one has and using it responsibly without being driven by envy. Its attributes include gratitude and moderation. A student shows it by caring for school materials and respecting another pupil's success without trying to compete through harmful choices. Other accurate combinations using integrity are also acceptable if the learner gives a correct meaning, two relevant attributes and a clear everyday example.

**Marks:** 16

- For each of four values: gives an accurate meaning (1 mark per value) — 4
- For each of four values: gives two relevant attributes (1 mark per value) — 4
- For each of four values: gives one clear everyday manifestation (1 mark per value) — 4
- For each of four values: explains how the example demonstrates the value rather than merely naming an action (1 mark per value) — 4
