## Legal reference base

PLAC consolidated Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, updated through the First–Fifth Alterations (third revision October 2023). Sections and provisions reviewed:

- Federal structure: sections 2–3; local government system: section 7 and Fourth Schedule.
- Legislative, executive and judicial powers: sections 4–6.
- National Assembly and state legislature: sections 47–49 and 90–91.
- Bills/assent and appropriation: sections 58–59 and 80–81; state bills: section 100.
- Legislative investigations: sections 88–89.
- President, Vice-President and Ministers: sections 130–148; state Governor/Deputy Governor/Commissioners: sections 176–196.
- Armed forces and Commander-in-Chief: sections 217–218.
- Supreme Court, CJN and original jurisdiction: sections 230–232.
- State High Court and Chief Judge: sections 270–272.

## Claim-by-claim review

| Source claim | Source locator | Constitutional comparison | Review status / flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nigeria has three tiers: Federal, State and Local Government. | DOCX p164 | Constitution ss2–3 establish the Federation, States and FCT; s7 guarantees democratically elected local government councils; Fourth Schedule lists their functions. Section 3(6) lists 768 LGAs and six FCT Area Councils. | **Broadly consistent educational model, but simplified.** The FCT/Area Council distinction should not disappear from a detailed legal explanation. |
| The Federal Executive consists of the President, Vice-President and Ministers. | DOCX p165 | Constitution s5(1) vests federal executive powers in the President and allows exercise through the Vice-President, Ministers or federal public-service officers; ss130–148 cover these offices and responsibilities. | **Simplified / incomplete.** The source presents a short leadership list as if it were the whole executive structure. |
| The Federal Legislature is bicameral: Senate and House of Representatives. | DOCX p166 | Constitution ss4(1), 47–49. | **Consistent.** The source's basic structural claim is supported. |
| State legislature is a unicameral House of Assembly. | DOCX p167 | Constitution ss4(6)–(7), 90–91. | **Consistent.** |
| The Judiciary is headed by “Chief Justice of the Federation” and “Chief Justice of the State”. | DOCX p190 (preceding judiciary description in p189) | Constitution s231 uses the office title Chief Justice of Nigeria; s271 uses Chief Judge of a State. | **Incorrect constitutional titles.** Preserve the original wording in source records; a separate approved correction is required before teaching the titles as current. |
| Executive function 1: formulate and implement policies. | DOCX p172 | Constitution s5 describes executive power and execution/maintenance of the Constitution and laws. | **Broad summary.** “Policy formulation” is a general executive/governance description, not a separately enumerated s5 phrase. |
| Executive function 2: appoint heads of government corporations/agencies, ministers and commissioners. | DOCX p173 | Constitution ss147, 154, 171, 176–196 and relevant commission provisions. | **Partly supported but overgeneralized.** Appointment authority, recommendation and confirmation vary by office; the source collapses distinct constitutional processes. |
| Executive function 3: prepare national/state budgets through finance ministers/commissioners. | DOCX p174 | Constitution s81(1) for federal estimates; s120–121 for state revenue/expenditure/appropriation. | **Broadly supported but compressed.** The constitutional process is more specific than the source wording. |
| Executive function 4: chief executive assents to bills after the legislative process. | DOCX p175 | Constitution ss58–59 (federal bills) and s100 (state bills). | **Broadly supported.** The federal and state assent processes should not be conflated. |
| Executive function 5: protect the country against external aggression through the military. | DOCX p176 | Constitution ss217–218. | **Broadly supported.** The source is a short summary of the constitutional defence/command framework. |
| Executive function 6: protect citizens' lives and property. | DOCX p177 | Constitution s5 and wider constitutional obligations; this phrase is not a discrete exhaustive list of executive powers. | **Broad / not exclusive to the Executive.** Avoid presenting it as a complete or exclusive constitutional enumeration. |
| Legislature function 1: make laws. | DOCX p179 | Constitution s4; federal bill process s58; state bill process s100. | **Consistent.** |
| Legislature function 2: confirm/ratify appointments of heads of parastatals/agencies, ministers and commissioners. | DOCX p180 | Examples include Senate confirmation of Ministers under s147 and certain judicial/commission appointments under their respective provisions. State appointments have separate procedures. | **Overgeneralized.** Not every appointment is confirmed by the legislature, and federal/state procedures differ. |
| Legislature function 3: amend the Constitution. | DOCX p181 | Constitution s9. | **Consistent in principle**, subject to the special constitutional amendment thresholds and approvals. |
| Legislature function 4: approve expenditure sent by the Executive as bills. | DOCX p182 | Constitution ss80–81 (federal) and ss120–121 (state). | **Broadly supported.** The source should not imply that all executive expenditure is approved through one generic bill. |
| Legislature function 5: approve treaties before the Executive signs them. | DOCX p183 | Constitution s12(1)–(2). | **Misleading shorthand.** Section 12 concerns the domestic legal effect/implementation of treaties; the source's “approval before signing” formulation should not be treated as a precise constitutional process. |
| Legislature function 6: serve as a link between people and government as representatives. | DOCX p184 | Constitution ss48–49 and 90–91 establish representative composition/election. | **Broad civic explanation**, not a discrete constitutional power. |
| Legislature function 7: provide checks and balances. | DOCX p185 | Separation of powers across ss4–6 and specific oversight/approval provisions. | **Broad constitutional principle**, not a single enumerated function. |
| Legislature function 8: investigate past and serving public officers. | DOCX p186 | Constitution ss88–89. | **Overbroad unless qualified.** The investigation power is tied to defined legislative purposes and matters within legislative competence; it is not an unrestricted general inquiry power. |
| Legislature function 9: investigate any government/public department on grounds of corruption. | DOCX p187 | Constitution s88(1)–(2), s89. | **Overbroad and duplicative of function 8.** The source's two investigation bullets overlap; constitutional purpose and jurisdictional limits must be retained in any later correction. |
| Judiciary symbol is a blindfolded woman with sword and balance. | DOCX p190 | Not specified as a constitutional symbol in the Constitution. | **Conventional iconography, not a constitutional office/function.** The source says “stature”; retain source wording in the archive. |
| Judiciary interprets law. | DOCX p192 | Constitution s6(6), plus jurisdiction provisions for the relevant courts. | **Broadly supported.** |
| Judiciary settles disputes among associates/groups. | DOCX p193 | Constitution s6(6)(b) and court-specific jurisdiction provisions. | **Vague / oversimplified.** The phrase “associates, group etc.” does not describe jurisdiction or court competence. |
| Judiciary determines guilt or innocence. | DOCX p194 | Criminal jurisdiction is assigned to courts under the Constitution and relevant laws. | **Broadly true for criminal adjudication, but not a universal function of every judicial matter.** |
| Judiciary settles disputes among other arms of government. | DOCX p195 | Constitution s232 gives the Supreme Court original jurisdiction in specified disputes between the Federation and States or between States. Other court jurisdiction depends on the Constitution and laws. | **Overgeneralized.** This is not a generic function of every court; the source does not identify the relevant jurisdictional limits. |

## Source-count / structure observations

- Six executive functions are listed at original DOCX paragraphs 172–177.
- Nine legislative bullets are listed at paragraphs 179–187. Bullets 8 and 9 both describe investigations and overlap.
- Four judicial functions are listed at paragraphs 192–195.
- The source's “Chief Justice of the Federation/State” wording is not the constitutional office nomenclature.
- The Judiciary “symbol” paragraph is descriptive/iconographic, not a constitutional statement.
