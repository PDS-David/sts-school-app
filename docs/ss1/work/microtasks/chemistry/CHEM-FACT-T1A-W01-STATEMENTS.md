## Findings
1. **Definition of chemistry:** the source's short definition is a simplified classroom description. The IUPAC Gold Book defines specific chemical concepts such as a chemical reaction as interconversion of chemical species; it does not establish the source's exact one-sentence definition as a formal IUPAC definition. Preserve the source wording and label any later definition as an authored teaching clarification, not an IUPAC quotation.
2. **Branches:** the three named branches (physical, organic, inorganic) are a traditional introductory grouping, not an exhaustive modern list. The physical-chemistry description “quantum theory, heat and electricity” is too narrow as a complete description; physical chemistry also encompasses thermodynamics, kinetics, quantum chemistry, spectroscopy and statistical mechanics. No replacement paragraph is supplied here.
3. **Scientific-method stages:** the source presents a fixed linear sequence including “Pattern”. National Academies guidance explicitly cautions that science does not follow one universal linear method; scientists use observation, experimentation, modelling, inference, argument and revision in varied and iterative ways. “Pattern” may describe a useful reasoning activity but is not a universally fixed stage in a single required sequence.
4. **Theory and law:** the source's statement that a theory becomes a scientific law is scientifically incorrect if it implies a theory matures into a law. A theory is an explanatory framework; a law describes a regularity or relationship. They serve different roles and do not transform into one another through accumulated evidence.
5. **Career statistic:** the statement that chemical industries employ “about 66% of all the chemists” has no date, country, denominator or cited statistical source in the source passage. It cannot be presented as a current universal statistic. Preserve as historical source wording; omit from new teaching unless a dated, jurisdiction-specific labour-market source is identified.
6. **Bibliographic reference:** “O.Y Ababio” is consistent with the author name Osei Yaw Ababio. The LAUTECH library catalogue identifies *New School Chemistry for Senior Secondary Schools* by Osei Yaw Ababio, Africana First Publishers, 2013; Google Books also lists *New School Chemistry* by Osei Yaw Ababio, Africana-FEP, 1985. The source's author abbreviation is plausible, but the precise edition/page reference must be matched to the copy actually assigned.
7. **Typographical issue:** “Collegs” at line 219 is a source spelling error for “Colleges”; do not silently correct the archived source.
