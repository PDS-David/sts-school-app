## Findings
1. **Eye protection:** the American Chemical Society's student laboratory safety guidance recommends chemical-splash goggles for people in the laboratory. Ordinary spectacles are not an equivalent substitute for splash goggles. The exact PPE required must still follow the chemical hazards and the institution's safety plan.
2. **Gloves:** gloves are not a universal one-material solution. ACS guidance says glove selection must be appropriate to the chemical; glove materials differ in resistance and permeation. The source's general glove instruction should not be interpreted as “any glove protects against every chemical.”
3. **Aprons/coats and clothing:** protective clothing should be selected for the hazard and must fit. Tie back long hair, remove snagging jewellery, and use footwear that protects the feet. Local school/workplace policy determines the exact required garment and material.
4. **Mouth pipetting:** never pipette by mouth. This is explicitly prohibited in ACS and OSHA laboratory guidance.
5. **Heating closed containers:** do not heat a sealed/capped container unless a specifically approved procedure and equipment are designed for that operation. Pressure can build and cause rupture. The source's prohibition is consistent with standard laboratory safety principles; this record adds no operating procedure.
6. **Fume cupboard / ventilation:** a fume hood is an engineering control for hazardous vapours, not a general storage cupboard or a substitute for safe procedure. OSHA's Chemical Hygiene Plan requires suitable, functioning protective equipment and workplace-specific standard operating procedures.
7. **Apparatus descriptions and uses:** the general functions in the source (for example, measuring/transfer glassware, support stands, heating vessels and condensers) are consistent with introductory chemistry apparatus guidance. However, the source's physical descriptions and some conflated names need their own statement-level review; this task does not silently correct spelling or merge entries.
8. **Emergency equipment:** the source only names locations/items learners should know. It does not describe how to operate a fire extinguisher, eyewash or safety shower, or what first aid to administer. No such instructions are invented here.
