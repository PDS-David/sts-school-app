## Safety findings register
| Source locator | Topic/claim | Finding and disposition |
|---|---|---|
| 413-425 | Goggles, latex gloves and apron | PPE selection must follow the specific chemical, concentration, task and exposure route. Safety glasses/goggles, protective clothing and suitable chemical-resistant gloves may be required, but “latex gloves” is not a universal chemical-protection specification. Latex allergy and glove-material compatibility matter. The source list is incomplete without a documented task risk assessment. |
| 459, 471, 485 | Never heat a capped flask or test tube | This is a sound critical warning: heating a sealed vessel can cause pressure build-up and rupture. Retain the warning, but do not infer that an uncapped vessel is automatically safe to heat. |
| 641-643 | Bunsen burner “utmost safety” | The phrase is not an operational control. A student lesson needs a teacher-approved, institution-specific risk assessment, equipment checks, supervision and controls for flames, hot glass and flammable materials. No new procedure is added here. |
| 771-797 | Concentrated aqueous ammonia opened in a room and odour detected from a distance | Concentrated ammonia releases irritating/corrosive vapour and can injure the eyes and respiratory tract. The NIOSH Pocket Guide identifies ammonia as a toxic inhalation hazard and provides exposure limits; ATSDR/NIOSH guidance treats ammonia releases as hazardous. A classroom activity that deliberately releases concentrated ammonia vapour into a room must not be treated as a routine student activity. The source's “smell from a distance” wording is not a substitute for containment and a risk assessment. |
| 773-825 | Potassium permanganate dilution and hot-water crystal activity | Potassium permanganate is a strong oxidiser; the ILO/WHO International Chemical Safety Card warns that it reacts with combustible/reducing materials and can create fire/explosion hazards. The activity's concentration, quantities, incompatibilities, heating method and supervision must be assessed before any practical use. Do not infer a safe procedure from the source description. |
| 2943, 3269 | Distillation of alcohol / heating fermented mash through copper coils | Ethanol is highly flammable; PubChem and OSHA describe ignition and vapour-explosion hazards. Distillation equipment also involves hot surfaces, pressure, glassware and vapour containment. The source's industrial description is not a student practical procedure and must not be used to author an open-flame classroom activity. |
| 3105-3107 | Activated charcoal described as a “universal antidote for poisoning” and water-purifier claims | “Universal antidote” is medically inaccurate and unsafe. Activated charcoal is used only in selected poisonings under medical/poison-centre guidance; it is not suitable for all substances and can cause serious harm if aspirated. The American Academy of Clinical Toxicology/European poison-centre position statement says it should not be administered routinely; Poison Control warns not to use it at home. Water-filter use is a separate adsorption application and does not imply universal removal of contaminants. |
| 3179 | Hot concentrated phosphoric acid washing | NIOSH identifies phosphoric acid exposure routes through inhalation, ingestion and skin/eye contact, with burns and irritation. Heating concentrated acid increases the severity of potential exposure. This wording requires a separate approved procedure and risk assessment; no student instruction is supplied here. |
