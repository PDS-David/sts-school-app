## Findings
1. **Compound definition:** a compound is a pure substance consisting of two or more different elements chemically combined in a fixed composition. It can be decomposed by chemical change, not separated into its constituent elements by ordinary physical separation. OpenStax distinguishes pure substances (elements and compounds) from mixtures.
2. **Formation and heat:** the claim that compound formation “often requires large amount of heat” is not a general chemical rule. Reactions may be exothermic or endothermic; some occur without external heating, while others require an input of energy or activation. Do not use “large amount of heat” as a defining property of compounds.
3. **Homogeneity:** a pure compound has uniform composition, so describing a pure compound as homogeneous is reasonable. The source's repeated spelling “homogenous” should be standardised to “homogeneous” only in a separately authored/corrected layer. Do not confuse a pure compound with a homogeneous mixture (solution).
4. **Formulae and names:** the source's formula table is a source transcription and its subscripts are subject to the separate run-format preservation record. “Lead II chloride” should be styled as lead(II) chloride in modern Stock nomenclature; the Roman numeral indicates the oxidation state of lead. This is a nomenclature clarification, not permission to rewrite the archived source formula.
5. **Roman numeral case:** the source varies between “(VI)”, “(vi)” and “(iv)”. Roman numerals in Stock names should be consistently uppercase and enclosed in parentheses. The source's inconsistent case is a formatting/editorial defect.
6. **Mixtures:** a mixture contains two or more substances physically combined in variable proportions and can be separated by physical methods. A homogeneous mixture has uniform composition; a heterogeneous mixture does not. The source's comparison should not imply that every mixture is visibly heterogeneous.
7. **“Alkanol”:** this is an established systematic class term for alcohols in substitutive nomenclature; it is not automatically an error. Retain the source term unless the surrounding context shows it was intended to be “alcohol”.
8. **“Chemogeneous”:** this is not the standard term in the context of mixture classification and is likely a typographical error for “homogeneous”. Preserve it in the source archive and correct only in a separate authored layer.
9. **Worked examples and questions:** the source's formula-writing rules/examples are not all verified by this review. Formula arithmetic, charges, valencies and subscripts require separate checking against primary nomenclature references; no missing answer is inferred.
10. **Reference spelling:** “Collegs” in the assigned reading reference is a typographical error; verify the assigned edition and pages before publication.
