## Findings register
| Block(s) | Source safety issue | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| 975 | Sodium reacts violently with water and produces flames; chlorine described as poisonous | Sodium-water reaction is highly exothermic and can ignite hydrogen; chlorine is acutely toxic by inhalation. These are hazard demonstrations, not unsupervised classroom activities. Any mention of chlorine requires explicit containment, exposure prevention and teacher approval. |
| 916, 945 | Heating mercuric oxide | Mercury compounds and mercury vapour are toxic. Heating mercuric oxide can release mercury vapour and oxygen; this is not an acceptable student practical without specialist risk review and containment. |
| 940-942 | Heating copper in oxygen; copper with nitric acid; igniting cupric nitrate | These involve hot materials, oxidising/corrosive reagents and potentially toxic nitrogen oxides. No step-by-step procedure is supplied. |
| 961 | Reducing zinc oxide in a stream of hydrogen | Hydrogen is highly flammable and forms explosive mixtures with air; hot reduction requires specialist gas handling and ignition controls. Not suitable as a casual student demonstration. |
| 2137, 2143 | White phosphorus catches fire easily, stored under water and smoulders in air | White phosphorus is pyrophoric and highly toxic; storage/handling is a specialist hazardous-materials matter. No handling instructions are added. |
| 2130-2137 | Burning copper, lead, magnesium and phosphorus | Burning metals can produce intense heat and hazardous fumes; lead compounds and phosphorus present serious toxic hazards. This list must not be presented as a routine student practical. |
| 2142-2145 | Alkaline pyrogallol / benzene-1,2,3-triol | Pyrogallol and alkaline solutions present chemical exposure hazards; the source's procedure must be reviewed against a current SDS and specialist lab risk assessment. |
| 2150-2159 | Limewater, concentrated KOH/NaOH and solid NaOH handling | Concentrated alkalis and solid sodium hydroxide are corrosive and can cause severe skin/eye burns. No handling, dilution or first-aid steps are invented here. |
| 2161 | Concentrated tetraoxosulphate(VI) acid | Concentrated sulfuric acid is strongly corrosive and mixing/dilution can release substantial heat. The source provides no risk assessment or first aid. |
| 2202 | Asbestos paper across a flame | Asbestos is a serious carcinogenic inhalation hazard. An asbestos-containing material must not be used in a school demonstration; substitute materials require independent safety approval. |
| 2202, 2212 | Bunsen burner strike-back, “too much oxygen”, green flame and burner safety | The source explanation is incomplete and may mislead. Burner operation requires correct equipment, supervision and a local risk assessment; “too much oxygen” is not a sufficient technical explanation of flashback. No ignition procedure is added. |
