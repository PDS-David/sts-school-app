| 1411-1419 | Five statements of Dalton's theory, including atoms “exactly alike in aspect” | Dalton's historical postulates included the idea that atoms of one element are identical. The absolute identical-atom statement requires historical framing, not presentation as fully correct modern science. |
| 1427 | Rutherford attributed discovery of “protons, electron and neutrons” | Rutherford's gold-foil work established the nuclear model and he identified the hydrogen nucleus as the proton. | 1429 | Nuclear fission “destroys the atoms involved” | Fission splits a heavy atomic nucleus into smaller nuclei; it does not mean matter/atoms simply vanish. The process changes the identity of the nuclei/products. “Destroys the atoms” is imprecise and requires correction in a separate lesson layer. |
| 1431 | Chlorine isotope description: “two atoms with different nucleus content” | Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons, hence different mass numbers.
