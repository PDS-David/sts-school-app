## Findings register
| Topic / source span | Finding and required review |
|---|---|
| Relief regions, lines 166-275 | Nigeria is commonly described in broad relief groupings of lowlands and plateaux/highlands. Official environmental reports describe high plateaux generally around 300 m to above 900 m and lowlands generally below 300 m, but the boundary is a descriptive convention, not a universal elevation contour. Map each named plateau, escarpment and plain to an authoritative relief map. |
| Rivers, basins and lakes, lines 276-392 | The Niger and Benue are the major river system; their confluence is at Lokoja. The UNFCCC Second Biennial Update Report lists major hydrological source areas including the North Central Plateau, Western Uplands, Eastern Highlands and Udi Plateau. Check every named tributary, lake and basin against the hydrological map; distinguish a river basin from a river channel and reservoir. |
| Climate zones, lines 393-516 | A broad north-to-south pattern from semi-arid north through savanna to humid forest/coastal south is supported, but rainfall/temperature values vary by period, station and dataset. Any numerical climate statement must name the station/area, observation period and source. Do not present a single national rainfall number as uniform across Nigeria. |
| Rainfall/temperature claims | Compare with Nigeria Meteorological Agency station records or a dated official climate dataset. A historical monthly/annual mean must not be presented as a current normal without the reference period. |
| Seasons and Harmattan | Wet/dry season timing varies by latitude and year. Harmattan is a dry north-easterly continental air flow, most evident in the dry season; its onset/duration should not be stated as identical in every state. |
| Vegetation zones, lines 517-560 | Broad categories include mangrove/freshwater swamp and rainforest in the south, and Guinea/Sudan/Sahel savanna farther north, with derived savanna and highland vegetation transitions. Map boundaries are gradual and influenced by rainfall, soils, land use and fire; avoid sharp universal state boundaries. |
| Soil distribution, lines 561-605 | Soil groups and fertility vary with parent material, climate, vegetation, slope and land use. Do not assign one soil type to an entire state without a soil map. Laterite is a broad field term, not a single uniform soil class. |
