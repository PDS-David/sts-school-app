## Findings
1. **Food shortage and population:** limited food can increase competition, reduce growth and reproduction, increase mortality, and cause emigration. The exact population response depends on species, mobility, alternative food sources and other limiting factors.
2. **Cannibalism:** can occur in some animal species under particular conditions, including food stress, but it is not a universal or inevitable consequence of food shortage. It should not be listed as a general outcome for all populations.
3. **Population size:** sustained resource shortage can reduce population size or slow population growth through reduced birth rates, increased death rates and emigration. A population may also fluctuate or remain stable depending on other ecological factors.
4. **Food storage:** appropriate storage can reduce post-harvest losses, improve availability between harvests and help moderate seasonal price changes. It does not automatically prevent famine or guarantee stable prices; transport, access, purchasing power, conflict, climate and markets also matter.
5. **“Biggest factor affecting food production in Africa”:** the question is not scientifically answerable as a single Africa-wide ranking without defining a country, crop, period and measure. Drought/climate variability, soil fertility, land access, pests/diseases, inputs, infrastructure, technology, conflict, storage and market access all matter and vary by region. Do not key one universal factor from the four options without teacher-approved scope and evidence.

## Safe answer-key guidance
- Effects of food shortage: competition, emigration and increased mortality are valid general effects; cannibalism must be qualified as species-specific, not universal.
- Food shortage can cause population decline or slower growth, not an automatic single response in every population.
- Food storage may improve availability and reduce seasonal price fluctuations, but its impact depends on infrastructure and access.
- The Africa-wide “biggest factor” question should be rewritten to specify a region/crop/time period or removed from single-answer marking.
