# Supplementary lesson: Advantages and disadvantages of sexual reproduction (SS 1 Biology, Second Term, Week 3)

> The Week 3 theory question asks learners to state advantages and disadvantages of sexual reproduction, but the source body does not teach these points. This lesson supplies the missing explanation and keyed practice without changing the original school note.

## Learning objectives
Explain sexual reproduction and state its advantages and disadvantages.

## 1. Meaning of sexual reproduction
Sexual reproduction involves the combination of genetic material from haploid reproductive cells (gametes), usually by fertilisation. The gametes fuse to form a zygote. The offspring are genetically different from one another and from their parents in many respects.

## 2. Advantages
1. **Genetic variation:** offspring inherit different combinations of genes. This creates variation within a population.
2. **Potential adaptation:** when conditions change, some individuals may possess inherited characteristics that improve survival and reproduction.
3. **Disease response:** genetic differences mean a disease that affects some individuals severely may not affect every individual in exactly the same way.
4. **Removal or masking of harmful variants:** recombination and selection can help populations change over generations, although sexual reproduction does not guarantee that every offspring is healthier.

## 3. Disadvantages
1. **A mate is usually needed:** finding a suitable mate can take time and energy.
2. **Reproduction may be slower:** producing gametes, finding a mate and completing fertilisation can take longer than some forms of asexual reproduction.
3. **Energy expenditure:** courtship, mating and reproductive structures may require energy.
4. **Only some individuals directly produce offspring in many species:** for example, in species with separate sexes, each individual does not necessarily produce offspring on its own.

## 4. Worked example
Imagine a population of insects living in a habitat where the climate becomes drier. Sexual reproduction produces offspring with different gene combinations. Some may inherit traits that help them conserve water and reproduce under the new conditions. This variation can help the population over generations. It does not mean every offspring will be better adapted.

## 5. Compare sexual and asexual reproduction
| Feature | Sexual reproduction | Asexual reproduction |
|---|---|---|
| Gamete fusion | Usually involves fusion of gametes | No fusion of gametes |
| Parents | Usually two genetic contributors | One parent |
| Offspring | Usually genetically varied | Usually genetically very similar to parent |
| Speed | Often slower | Often faster under favourable conditions |
| Mate required | Usually | No |

The comparison describes common patterns; biology has exceptions, including organisms that can use both methods.

## 6. Practice
1. Define sexual reproduction. (2)
2. State three advantages of sexual reproduction. (3)
3. State two disadvantages of sexual reproduction. (2)
4. Explain how genetic variation may help a population when its environment changes. (2)
