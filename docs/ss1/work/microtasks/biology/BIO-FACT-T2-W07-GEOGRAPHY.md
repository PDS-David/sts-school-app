## Findings

### 1. Nigerian vegetation zones
The broad pattern in the source is supported: wetter forest and swamp types occur in the south/coastal belt, grading northward through Guinea and Sudan savanna to the drier Sahel in the far north. The Federal Government's National Communications to the UNFCCC and FAO sources describe mangrove/coastal and freshwater swamps, lowland rainforest, Guinea savanna, Sudan savanna and Sahel savanna. Some classifications also identify derived savanna, Jos Plateau/montane and separate forest subtypes. Boundaries are gradual and vary by classification; do not present a single state list as an exact immutable boundary.

The source's local zone labels are broadly useful, but its state examples should be treated as approximate. Official and research maps show zones can cross state boundaries and even vary within one state.

### 2. Rainfall and temperature figures
- The source's mangrove rainfall “above 250 cm” is plausible for parts of the Niger Delta/eastern coastal belt, but it is not a universal value for every mangrove locality.
- The source's tropical rainforest rainfall of about 200 cm is a simplified average. FAO references describe the coastal forest belt as generally above 1,600 mm, exceeding 2,500 mm in the Niger Delta and eastern coast.
- The source's general savanna rainfall “50–150 cm” is too broad to distinguish subzones. FAO gives approximate ranges: southern Guinea 1,150–1,500 mm; median/northern Guinea 1,000–1,250 mm; Sudan 500–1,500 mm depending on subzone; Sahel 250–500 mm. These are regional ranges, not universal annual guarantees.
- Fixed annual temperatures such as 26°C, 27°C, 29°C and 18°C vary by location, season, altitude and measurement period. Do not use one number as a universal defining criterion for an entire Nigerian biome. Rainfall seasonality and vegetation structure are more useful at this introductory level.

### 3. Afro-alpine altitude: 3,000 m versus 300 m
The source body says Afro-alpine vegetation occurs above **3,000 m** (block 656); the weekend question gives **300 m** (block 676). The 300 m option is a typographical/scale error. Afro-alpine vegetation is a high-mountain zone, generally at several thousand metres above sea level; **300 m is not a valid Afro-alpine threshold**.

Important qualification: Nigeria's highest mountain, Chappal Waddi, is about 2,419 m, so a general statement that Afro-alpine vegetation occurs above 3,000 m describes high mountains in Africa/world geography, not a Nigerian local biome above that altitude. Nigerian highland vegetation should be described using its own altitude and ecological context, not by assuming that the 3,000 m Afro-alpine zone occurs within Nigeria.

### 4. Tundra versus montane vegetation
The source's tundra description (treeless vegetation with dwarf shrubs, grasses, lichens and mosses, very cold conditions) belongs under the separate **world tundra biome**. It should not be used as a defining description of all montane vegetation. High-altitude environments can contain alpine/tundra-like vegetation, but the categories should remain distinct in a student table:
- **Tundra:** cold, treeless biome, often with permafrost in Arctic regions.
- **Montane vegetation:** vegetation zoned by elevation on mountains; it varies with altitude, slope and regional climate. Afro-alpine is a high-elevation subtype in some tropical mountains.
