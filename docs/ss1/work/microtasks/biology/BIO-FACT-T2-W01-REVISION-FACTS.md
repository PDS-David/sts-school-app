### Block 76 — “Describe sporulation in amoeba.”

**Finding:** This terminology is supported by the CBSE Class XII Biology material and the NCERT supplementary material. It describes an Amoeba encysting under unfavourable conditions; when conditions improve, the encysted cell undergoes multiple fission and releases many small daughter amoebae (also termed pseudopodiospores in the cited material). The prompt is biologically defensible, but the Week 1 teaching body only introduces spore formation generally and does not explain this Amoeba-specific sequence. If retained, teach it in a short supplement rather than implying the sequence is already covered.

References:
- CBSE Academic, Class XII Biology, “Reproduction in Organisms”, sporulation: https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2012/79_Biology.pdf
- NCERT supplementary Biology material, Unit VI, Chapter 1: https://n20.ncert.org.in/desm/pdf/desm_biology.pdf

### Block 85 — Paramecium zygote/eight-nuclei question

**Source wording issue:** The question says “During asexual reproduction in paramecium” and then asks how many times the zygote divides to produce eight nuclei.

**Finding:** The eight-nuclei count is supported: after fertilisation during conjugation, the zygotic nucleus (synkaryon) undergoes three rounds of mitosis, producing eight nuclear products. However, conjugation is a sexual process, not asexual reproduction. The question should therefore be reworded in a supplement as: “During conjugation (sexual reproduction) in Paramecium, how many times does the zygotic nucleus divide to produce eight nuclei?” The supported answer is **three times (option C)**.

References:
- OpenStax Biology 2e, 23.3 “Groups of Protists”: https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/23-3-groups-of-protists
- Zhang et al. (2022), “Timing and characteristics of nuclear events during conjugation and genomic exclusion in Paramecium multimicronucleatum”, *Marine Life Science & Technology*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10077201/

### Block 87 — perennating-organ multiple-choice item

**Source options:** Rhizome, Sucker, Bulbil, Bulb.

**Finding:** The source's preceding paragraph lists all four as natural vegetative-propagation structures and says that the plant part involved may “sometimes” act as a perennating organ; it does not identify which option is excluded. The references consulted do not provide a single consistent classification:
- A school Biology resource explicitly includes suckers among examples of perennating organs: https://education.giakonda.org.uk/Biology/BIOLOGY%28BORNIFACE%29.pdf
- UACE plant-reproduction notes distinguish vegetative-propagation organs from perennating organs and give a narrower list of storage structures: https://mandelasshoima.ac.ug/documents/S.6%20REPRODUCTION%20IN%20PLANTS.pdf

Because the classification differs and the source gives no species-specific context or adopted definition, the multiple-choice item has no securely established unique answer from the evidence available. It is not keyed here. A teacher must select the approved reference/definition or revise the options and wording.
