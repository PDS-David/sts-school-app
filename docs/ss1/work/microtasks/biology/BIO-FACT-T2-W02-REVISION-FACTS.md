## Findings

| Prompt | Evidence-based finding | Safe answer basis / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| “Describe conjugation in Mucor.” | Mucor can reproduce sexually by gametangial copulation between compatible mating types. Opposite compatible hyphae form contacting branches; the terminal gametangia fuse and form a thick-walled zygospore. The term conjugation is used in school texts, while **gametangial copulation** is a more precise process name. | Describe compatible +/− hyphae, progametangia/gametangia, fusion and zygospore formation. Do not call it Paramecium-type nuclear exchange. |
| “Describe sexual reproduction in Hydra.” | Hydra can reproduce asexually by budding and, in some species/conditions, sexually. Testes and ovaries develop on the body; sperm are released into water and fertilise an egg. The fertilised egg develops a protective covering and may rest before development continues. | Explain that Hydra is not exclusively asexual. Do not present sexual reproduction as its only or routine method in all conditions. |
| “Mention two structures each where meiosis takes place in plants and animals.” | In animals, meiosis occurs in germ cells in the **testes** and **ovaries**. In plants, meiosis occurs in spore-producing cells in **microsporangia** and **megasporangia** (for flowering plants, these are within anthers and ovules). | Do not say mature pollen grains or mature ovules themselves are where meiosis occurs. Microsporocytes produce microspores; megasporocytes produce megaspores. |
| “Outline four life processes involving meiosis.” | The source's examples (spermatozoa, ova, pollen grains and ovules) are oversimplified. Meiosis produces haploid gametes in animals and haploid spores in the diploid sporophyte generation of plants. | A safe four-item answer is: sperm formation; egg formation; microspore formation in anthers; megaspore formation in ovules. Explain that pollen grains and embryo sacs develop from spores; they are not themselves direct products of meiosis. |
| “From which cell organelle do spindle fibres originate in animal cells?” | In typical animal cells, the spindle is organised by centrosomes, which contain centrioles and pericentriolar material. The source's multiple-choice answer **centrosome** is the best option. | Answer: centrosome. Avoid saying that all eukaryotic cells use centrioles; many plant cells form spindles without centrioles. |
| “In animals meiosis comes …” | In the typical animal life cycle, meiosis produces haploid gametes before fertilisation; fertilisation then restores the diploid chromosome number. | The correct option is **before fertilisation**. The timing differs across life cycles in plants and fungi, so qualify the answer as the animal life cycle. |
| “Which excretory structure is not found in amphibians: gills, lungs, skin, flame cell?” | Amphibians may use gills (especially larvae), lungs and skin in gas exchange; kidneys are their main excretory organs. Flame cells are characteristic of flatworms, not amphibians. | The best answer is **flame cell**. The option list mixes respiratory surfaces with an excretory structure; do not imply gills, lungs or skin are all equivalent kidney-like organs. |
