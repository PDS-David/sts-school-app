# Supplementary lesson: Effects of overpopulation in a community (SS 1 Biology, Second Term, Week 8)

> The source's Week 8 general evaluation asks for five effects of overpopulation but does not teach them as a separate topic. This supplement answers that question using the ecological idea of carrying capacity; it does not replace the source's population lesson.

## Learning objectives
Define overpopulation in ecological terms and explain effects on organisms and their environment.

## 1. Meaning of overpopulation
A population is a group of organisms of the same species living in a particular area at a particular time. **Overpopulation** occurs when the number of individuals becomes too large for the available resources and environmental conditions to support them without harmful effects. The idea is related to **carrying capacity**—the population size an environment can support over time with its available resources.

A large population is not automatically overpopulated. The effect depends on the size and quality of the habitat, food, water, shelter and other limiting factors.

## 2. Effects of overpopulation
1. **Competition for resources:** organisms compete more strongly for food, water, light, nesting places and space.
2. **Food shortage and poor growth:** limited food can lead to malnutrition, slower growth, reduced reproduction and starvation.
3. **Spread of disease and parasites:** close contact and crowding can make some infections and parasites spread more easily.
4. **Habitat damage:** heavy grazing, trampling, burrowing or overuse can damage vegetation, soil and shelter sites.
5. **Waste accumulation and pollution:** large amounts of faeces and other waste can contaminate water and soil if not broken down or dispersed adequately.
6. **Higher mortality and emigration:** competition, disease and shortage of resources can increase death rates or cause organisms to move elsewhere.
7. **Food-web changes:** changes in the abundance of one population can affect predators, prey, competitors and decomposers.
8. **Reduced biodiversity:** a population that uses resources heavily may contribute to the decline of other species in the same habitat.

These effects do not occur in every population in exactly the same way. They depend on the species and habitat.

## 3. Worked example
A pond has a limited amount of dissolved oxygen and food. If too many fish are introduced, they may compete for food and oxygen. Waste can accumulate, water quality may decline, disease may spread more readily and some fish may die or leave if they can. This is an example of a population exceeding the pond's carrying capacity.

## 4. Link to population size
Population size changes through births (natality), deaths (mortality), immigration and emigration. If births and immigration remain high while resources are limited, population pressure may increase. A population may then decline because of higher mortality or emigration, or because reproduction decreases.
