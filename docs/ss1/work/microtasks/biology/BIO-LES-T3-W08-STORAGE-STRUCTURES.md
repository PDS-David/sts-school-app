# Supplementary lesson: Food storage structures — silos and barns (SS 1 Biology, Third Term, Week 8)

> The Week 8 weekend question lists silos and barns as food-storage options, but the teaching body does not explain them. This supplement supplies the missing definitions and a short keyed practice.

## Learning objectives
Define a silo and a barn, explain what each can store and state basic principles of safe crop storage.

## 1. Why store harvested food?
Food storage helps make harvested crops available after the harvest season, reduces losses and waste, supports food supply during shortages and may reduce extreme seasonal price changes. Good storage must protect produce from moisture, pests, contamination and physical damage.

## 2. Silos
A **silo** is a storage structure designed mainly for bulk agricultural produce.
- Grain silos store dry grains such as maize, rice, sorghum and wheat.
- Some silos store chopped green forage (silage) for livestock feed.
- Grain silos are designed to protect grain from rain, excess moisture, rodents and insects and to allow loading/unloading.
- Silage silos are designed to exclude air sufficiently for controlled fermentation of forage.

Do not confuse a grain silo with a silage silo; their stored materials and storage conditions differ.

## 3. Barns
A **barn** is an agricultural building used for storing harvested crops, hay, straw, farm equipment or housing livestock, depending on its design.
- Hay barns keep dried forage protected from rain and ground moisture.
- Crop barns may hold harvested produce or equipment.
- Livestock barns provide shelter and space for animals.

A barn is a general agricultural building; it is not automatically airtight or suitable for long-term storage of every grain.

## 4. Principles of safe crop storage
- Store only produce that is sufficiently dry for the approved storage method.
- Keep storage areas clean and repair leaks.
- Protect produce from insects, rodents, birds and other pests.
- Use suitable containers/structures and prevent re-wetting.
- Inspect stored produce for mould, pests and spoilage.
- Follow agricultural extension advice and food-safety standards. Fumigation and pesticide application are not student activities and must be carried out only by authorised professionals.

## 5. Compare silos and barns

| Feature | Silo | Barn |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Bulk storage of grain or silage | General agricultural storage and/or livestock shelter |
| Structure | Purpose-built vertical or horizontal storage vessel | Agricultural building with open or enclosed storage space |
| Typical contents | Dry grain or fermented forage | Hay, straw, harvested crops, equipment or animals |
| Key storage concern | Moisture, aeration/fermentation conditions, pests | Rain protection, ventilation, moisture, pests and cleanliness |
