# Supplementary lesson: Advantages and disadvantages of asexual reproduction (SS 1 Biology, Second Term, Week 1)

> The source's Week 1 theory question asks for three advantages and two disadvantages of asexual reproduction, but its teaching body does not supply this list. This lesson fills that specific gap; it is supplementary and does not alter the original source.

## Learning objectives
Explain asexual reproduction and give three advantages and two disadvantages, using examples.

## 1. Meaning
**Asexual reproduction** is reproduction in which a new organism is produced from one parent without fusion of male and female gametes. In many cases the offspring are genetically very similar or identical to the parent; such offspring are called **clones**. Mutations can still create differences.

Examples include binary fission in bacteria and Amoeba, budding in yeast and Hydra, fragmentation in Spirogyra, spore formation in many fungi, and vegetative propagation in plants.

## 2. Advantages
1. **Only one parent is needed.** An organism does not need to find a mate.
2. **Reproduction can be rapid.** Many offspring may be produced in a short time under favourable conditions.
3. **Successful characteristics are retained.** Offspring are usually genetically very similar to a parent that is already well suited to a stable environment.
4. **Less energy may be used to find a mate.** Energy can be directed towards growth or producing offspring.
5. **Useful traits can be multiplied.** Farmers can propagate a plant with desirable fruit or other characteristics using vegetative methods.

Any three of these are suitable advantages.

## 3. Disadvantages
1. **Little genetic variation among offspring.** If the environment changes, many offspring may share the same weakness.
2. **A disease or pest may affect many offspring.** Similar genetic make-up can make a population vulnerable to the same pathogen or environmental stress.
3. **Adaptation to change may be limited.** A population with little variation may have fewer individuals with traits suited to new conditions.
4. **Harmful mutations can be passed on.** A mutation in a parent cell may be inherited by descendants.

Any two of these are suitable disadvantages.

## 4. Worked comparison
A farmer has one high-yielding cassava plant. Propagating it vegetatively can produce many plants with similar characteristics, without waiting for seed production. This is useful if the environment remains suitable. However, if all the plants are genetically very similar, one disease may affect many of them. The farmer should not assume that clones are automatically disease-proof.

## 5. Practice
1. Define asexual reproduction. (2)
2. State three advantages of asexual reproduction. (3)
3. State two disadvantages of asexual reproduction. (2)
4. Explain why a field of genetically similar crop plants may be vulnerable to a new disease. (2)
