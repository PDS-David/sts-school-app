## 1. HIV and AIDS terminology
- **HIV** means **human immunodeficiency virus**. The source table's “Human immune-deficiency virus” is close but should use the standard expansion; the body phrase “Acquired immune deficiency virus” is wrong.
- **AIDS** means **acquired immunodeficiency syndrome**. HIV is the virus; AIDS is the advanced clinical syndrome that can occur if HIV is untreated or not adequately controlled.
- HIV is a retrovirus. It uses reverse transcriptase to make DNA from its RNA genome and can integrate viral DNA into the host cell's DNA. It primarily targets CD4 T lymphocytes and other immune cells.
- Effective antiretroviral treatment can suppress HIV to an undetectable viral load and allow people to live long, healthy lives. HIV should not be described as inevitably progressing to AIDS.

## 2. Transmission claims
HIV can be transmitted through:
- anal or vaginal sex when effective prevention measures are not used;
- blood exposure, including sharing contaminated injection equipment or unscreened infected blood;
- pregnancy, childbirth and breastfeeding (perinatal transmission).

Important corrections:
- **Casual kissing, open-mouth kissing, saliva, hugging, sharing utensils and ordinary social contact do not transmit HIV.** HIV is not transmitted by mosquitoes or other insects.
- Oral sex carries a much lower risk than anal or vaginal sex; the source should not list it as an equivalent route without qualification.
- Do not teach that HIV is transmitted by every sexual contact or that an infected person is necessarily visibly ill.

## 3. HIV prevention and control
A safe student-level list includes: correct and consistent condom use; HIV testing and counselling; PrEP for people with ongoing risk and PEP after a possible exposure under urgent medical guidance; not sharing needles or injection equipment; screened blood; antiretroviral treatment; and prevention of parent-to-child transmission through appropriate health services. “Keep one faithful partner” is not a complete prevention message unless both partners know their HIV status and other risk factors are addressed.

The source also names herpes and thrush as STIs in the introductory list:
- **Genital herpes** is caused by herpes simplex viruses (HSV-1 or HSV-2) and spreads through skin-to-skin contact with infected areas, including when symptoms are absent. Antiviral medicines can manage outbreaks but do not eradicate latent virus.
- **Thrush (vulvovaginal/oral candidiasis)** is usually an overgrowth of *Candida* yeast and is **not generally classified as an STI**, although sexual activity can sometimes trigger symptoms or rarely transmit yeast. Do not list thrush as equivalent to gonorrhoea or syphilis.

## 5. Assessment corrections
- “Which of the following statements is wrong? All STIs are caused by viruses / bacteria / protozoans / all of the above.” Each of the first three universal statements is false: STIs have viral, bacterial, protozoal and ectoparasitic causes. Therefore “all of the above” is the intended answer only if the stem means all three statements are wrong.
- The “great imitator” description is associated with syphilis because its symptoms can resemble those of other conditions. This is a teaching phrase, not a diagnostic rule.
- Do not use AIDS symptoms as proof of HIV; testing is required.
