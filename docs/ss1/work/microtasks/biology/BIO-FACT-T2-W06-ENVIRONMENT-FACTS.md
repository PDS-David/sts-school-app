## Findings

### 1. Earth surface coverage
The source's “lithosphere 30%” and “hydrosphere 70%” is a rough school-level approximation of the proportion of Earth's surface covered by land and ocean. NASA states that the global ocean covers about **71%** of Earth's surface, leaving about **29%** as land. However, lithosphere and hydrosphere are not simply interchangeable with land and ocean: the lithosphere is the rigid outer part of Earth (crust and uppermost mantle), while the hydrosphere includes water in oceans, lakes, rivers, ice and water vapour. For a precise student statement, write: **about 71% of Earth's surface is covered by ocean water and about 29% is land**. If retaining the source's earth-system spheres, define each sphere rather than assigning the land/ocean percentages to the lithosphere and hydrosphere as whole systems.

### 2. Dry atmospheric gas percentages
The source lists nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21%, carbon dioxide 0.03% and rare gases 0.77%. This is an outdated and misleading grouping.

NOAA's current dry-air reference values are approximately:
- Nitrogen (N₂): **78.084%**
- Oxygen (O₂): **20.946%**
- Argon (Ar): **0.934%**
- Carbon dioxide (CO₂): **0.042%**

Other gases occur in much smaller trace amounts. Water vapour is variable and is not included in the dry-air percentages. Argon is a noble gas and is the main component of the “rare gases” group; it is not 0.77% in the modern reference composition. Carbon dioxide varies over time and should be identified as an approximate current value, not a permanent fixed percentage.
