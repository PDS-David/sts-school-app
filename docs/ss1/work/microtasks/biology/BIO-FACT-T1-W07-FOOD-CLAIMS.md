## Carbohydrate and lipid statements

| Locator | Source form/claim | Evidence-based disposition for a supplement |
|---|---|---|
| 841 | General monosaccharide formula `C6H10O6`; examples include glucose, fructose, ribose and galactose | There is no single fixed molecular formula for every monosaccharide. Common hexoses such as glucose, fructose and galactose are `C6H12O6`; ribose is `C5H10O5`. A general empirical pattern is often written `(CH2O)n`, with exceptions. |
| 844 | Sucrose is described as formed from a glucose molecule and then repeats glucose in the sentence | The wording is ambiguous/corrupted. Sucrose is a disaccharide made from glucose and fructose, joined by a glycosidic bond with water released in condensation. Do not silently rewrite the source sentence. |
| 871 | Tristearin formula `C57H10O6` | The molecular formula is `C57H110O6`; the source has omitted two digits in the hydrogen count. |

## Mineral table — functions and deficiency claims

| Mineral | Verified function / evidence-based qualification |
|---|---|
| Calcium | Important for bones/teeth, muscle contraction, nerve signalling and blood clotting. Low calcium can contribute to poor bone mineralisation, including rickets/osteomalacia in relevant contexts. Tooth decay is not a specific diagnostic sign of calcium deficiency and should not be listed as a direct deficiency disease. |
| Phosphorus | Part of bones/teeth, ATP and nucleic acids; important in energy metabolism. Deficiency is uncommon but can cause muscle weakness and bone pain/softening. Rickets/osteomalacia can occur in phosphate disorders; tooth decay is not a specific phosphorus-deficiency sign. |
| Magnesium | Cofactor for many enzymes; supports muscle/nerve function, protein synthesis and bone structure. The source's phrase about “utilization of iron” is not a standard primary function. “Nervous disorder” is too vague; describe neuromuscular symptoms only when properly sourced. |
| Potassium | Essential for cell-fluid balance, nerve impulses and muscle contraction. Deficiency commonly causes weakness/cramps; paralysis and dangerous rhythm disturbances are severe outcomes, not the ordinary single symptom. |
| Sulfur | Present in the sulfur-containing amino acids methionine and cysteine and in several biological molecules. The source's generic “vitamin B” wording is too broad; name a specific compound only with evidence. The claim “poor growth” is not established as a unique sulfur-deficiency symptom by the references reviewed; do not present it as a specific deficiency disease. |
| Sodium/chloride | Major electrolytes involved in fluid balance and nerve/muscle function. “Dehydration” is not a precise deficiency symptom; low sodium/chloride is an electrolyte/fluid-balance disturbance. Muscle cramps may occur but are nonspecific. |
| Iron | Required for haemoglobin and oxygen transport. Iron deficiency can cause anaemia; the source's core claim is supported. |
| Iodine | Required to make thyroid hormones. Deficiency can cause goitre and impaired thyroid function; the source's core claim is supported. |
| Manganese | Enzyme cofactor involved in metabolism and bone formation. Human deficiency is rare; do not invent a characteristic deficiency disease where the source gives none. |
| Copper | Cofactor for enzymes involved in energy production, connective-tissue formation and iron metabolism. Copper deficiency can cause anaemia and other abnormalities. “Catalyses the use of iron” is an imprecise shorthand; use “helps iron metabolism”. |

| Source entry | Verified name/function | Deficiency disposition |
|---|---|---|
| A — retinol | Retinol; supports vision, cell growth and immune function. | Night blindness is a well-established deficiency sign; reduced resistance to infection is a reasonable broader description. |
| B1 | Thiamin (thiamine); coenzyme in energy metabolism and normal nerve function. | Deficiency causes beriberi and can cause serious neurological/cardiac effects. Dermatitis is not the characteristic primary deficiency description; replace it in the supplement. |

| B12 | Cobalamin; needed for DNA synthesis, red blood cell formation and nerve function. | Deficiency can cause megaloblastic anaemia and neurological problems. Pernicious anaemia is one specific cause of B12 deficiency, not the generic name for every B12-deficiency state. |

## Amylase and digestive-enzyme table

| Source locator/entry | Verified disposition |
|---|---|
| 916 — amylase described as digesting starches and sugars to glucose | Too broad. Amylases act on starch/glycogen; they do not digest all sugars or directly convert all carbohydrate to glucose. Salivary amylase begins starch digestion in the mouth. |
| 917 — pancreatic amylase said to produce maltose, sucrose and lactose and then digest those disaccharides | Incorrect. Pancreatic amylase breaks starch into shorter oligosaccharides, maltose and related products. Sucrase, lactase and maltase are separate brush-border enzymes that digest sucrose, lactose and maltose respectively. |
| Ptyalin | Salivary amylase from salivary glands; acts in the mouth on starch, producing maltose and shorter carbohydrate fragments. |
| Rennin | The source's milk-clotting description points to chymosin. IUBMB gives **chymosin** as the accepted name and warns that “rennin” can be confused with renal **renin**. If retained for school history, label it chymosin (historically called rennin) and explain its milk-clotting role; do not confuse it with renin. |
| Pepsin | Gastric protease; acts in the stomach on proteins and produces shorter peptides. “Peptones” is older school terminology; “peptides” is clearer modern wording. |
| Pancreatic lipase | Secreted by the pancreas and acts in the small intestine on triglycerides. The principal products are free fatty acids and monoacylglycerols; “glycerol and fatty acids” is a simplified description. |
| Pancreatic amylase | Acts in the small intestine on starch; does not form or digest sucrose/lactose. |
| Trypsin | Pancreatic protease active in the small intestine; breaks proteins/large peptides into smaller peptides. The source's product label “polypeptide” is too vague. |
| Erepsin | Historical collective term for intestinal peptidase activity, not one single modern enzyme. Use “intestinal peptidases” for the group that completes peptide digestion to smaller peptides and amino acids. |
