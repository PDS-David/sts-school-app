## Review of instrument/function pairs

| Instrument in source | Source-stated use | Verified function / delivery decision |
|---|---|---|
| Photometer | Light intensity | Broadly correct: a photometer measures light intensity/illuminance. For plant growth research, a quantum sensor may be used to measure photosynthetic photon flux density. |
| Hydrometer | “Light intensity in water” | **Incorrect.** A hydrometer measures the relative density/specific gravity of a liquid by floating at a level determined by liquid density. It does not measure light. |
| Wind vane | Wind direction | Correct. |
| Anemometer | Wind speed | Correct. |
| Rain gauge | Amount of rainfall | Correct. |
| Hygrometer | Relative humidity | Correct. |
| Barometer | Pressure | Correct; for weather observations, atmospheric pressure. |
| Glass thermometer | Temperature | Correct. |
| Colorimeter or pH scale | Acidity/alkalinity | Incomplete: a pH meter or pH indicator/scale is used to estimate acidity/alkalinity. A colorimeter measures the intensity of colour/absorbance in a solution; it is not a direct pH instrument unless part of a validated colorimetric method. |
| Secchi disc | Turbidity | Acceptable school-level description, but more precisely it measures water transparency/clarity (Secchi depth), which is affected by suspended particles and other optical properties. It is not a direct laboratory turbidity meter. |
| Sweep insect net | Catching insects | Correct as a sampling/collecting tool for insects in vegetation; it does not itself measure population size without a defined sampling method. |

## Corrected student wording
- **Hydrometer:** measures relative density/specific gravity of liquids.
- **Underwater light:** use a suitable underwater light sensor/photometer or quantum sensor, not a hydrometer.
- **pH meter/indicator:** measures or estimates pH.
- **Colorimeter:** measures absorbance/colour intensity of a sample.
- **Secchi disc:** estimates water transparency (Secchi depth); lower visibility generally indicates reduced clarity.
