# Supplementary lesson: Microorganisms in the nitrogen and carbon cycles (SS 1 Biology, Third Term, Week 2)

> The Week 2 general evaluation asks learners to name microorganisms involved in the nitrogen and carbon cycles, but the teaching body does not explain these roles. This supplement provides the missing overview.

## Learning objectives
Describe how microorganisms help recycle nitrogen and carbon in ecosystems and give examples of microbial groups involved.

## 1. Why are cycles important?
Living organisms need elements such as carbon and nitrogen to build cells and tissues. These elements are reused in ecosystems. A **biogeochemical cycle** is the movement of an element between living organisms and the non-living environment.

Microorganisms are important because they decompose dead material, transform chemical compounds and make nutrients available for reuse.

## 2. Nitrogen cycle
Nitrogen is needed to make amino acids, proteins and nucleic acids. Most organisms cannot use atmospheric nitrogen gas (N₂) directly.

### 2.1 Nitrogen fixation
Nitrogen-fixing microorganisms convert atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia/ammonium compounds that can enter biological systems.
- **Rhizobium** bacteria live in root nodules of many legumes.
- Some free-living soil bacteria and cyanobacteria also fix nitrogen.

### 2.2 Nitrification
Nitrifying bacteria oxidise ammonium in stages:
- Ammonia/ammonium → nitrite, commonly by bacteria such as **Nitrosomonas**.
- Nitrite → nitrate, commonly by **Nitrobacter** and related bacteria.

Plants can absorb nitrate and ammonium and use nitrogen to build organic molecules.

### 2.3 Ammonification
Decomposer bacteria and fungi break down nitrogen-containing material in dead organisms and wastes, releasing ammonia/ammonium into the environment.

### 2.4 Denitrification
Denitrifying bacteria convert nitrate into gaseous nitrogen compounds, returning nitrogen to the atmosphere. This often occurs in oxygen-poor soils or sediments.

## 3. Carbon cycle
Carbon is present in carbon dioxide, living organisms, dead organic matter, fossil fuels and dissolved carbon compounds.

Microbial roles include:
- **Decomposition:** bacteria and fungi break down dead organisms and wastes, returning carbon compounds to the environment.
- **Respiration:** microorganisms release carbon dioxide when they respire organic food.
- **Fermentation:** some microbes break down organic material without oxygen, producing products such as carbon dioxide, organic acids or alcohols depending on the organism.
- **Methane production:** methanogenic archaea produce methane in oxygen-free environments such as wetlands and the digestive systems of some animals.
- **Methane consumption:** methanotrophic microorganisms use methane and help return its carbon to other forms.

Plants, algae and cyanobacteria also remove carbon dioxide during photosynthesis. This process is not limited to microorganisms.

## 4. Summary table

| Cycle process | Microbial role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen fixation | Converts N₂ to ammonia/ammonium | Rhizobium |
| Nitrification | Converts ammonium to nitrite and nitrate | Nitrosomonas, Nitrobacter |
| Ammonification | Releases ammonia/ammonium from organic matter | Decomposer bacteria and fungi |
| Denitrification | Converts nitrate to gaseous nitrogen compounds | Denitrifying bacteria |
| Carbon decomposition | Breaks down dead organic matter | Bacteria and fungi |
| Methane production | Produces methane in anaerobic environments | Methanogenic archaea |
| Methane oxidation | Uses methane | Methanotrophic bacteria/archaea |
