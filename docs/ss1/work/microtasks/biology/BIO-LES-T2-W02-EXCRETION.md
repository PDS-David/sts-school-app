# Supplementary lesson: Excretion and excretory structures (SS 1 Biology, Second Term, Week 2)

> The Week 2 scheme names “Excretory Organelles in Living Cells”, but the source teaching body contains no lesson on that topic. This is a source-distinct supplement; it does not claim the original note taught these structures.

## Learning objectives
Define excretion and distinguish it from egestion; identify common metabolic wastes; describe contractile vacuoles, flame cells, nephridia, Malpighian tubules and kidneys.

## 1. Meaning of excretion
**Excretion** is the removal of metabolic waste products produced by cells. These wastes may become harmful if they accumulate.

Do not confuse:
- **Excretion:** removal of wastes made by metabolism, such as carbon dioxide, excess water, salts and nitrogenous wastes.
- **Egestion:** removal of undigested food from the digestive tract.

## 2. Common metabolic wastes
- **Carbon dioxide:** produced during aerobic cellular respiration; removed through gas-exchange surfaces.
- **Excess water and mineral ions:** removed to maintain the correct internal water and salt balance.
- **Nitrogenous wastes:** produced when proteins and nucleic acids are broken down. Ammonia is very toxic; many terrestrial vertebrates convert it to urea or uric acid before excretion.

## 3. Excretion in unicellular organisms
Some unicellular organisms exchange small wastes such as carbon dioxide and ammonia across the cell surface by diffusion. In many freshwater protists, water continually enters the cell by osmosis.

A **contractile vacuole** is a cell organelle that collects excess water and helps expel it from the cell. In organisms such as Amoeba and Paramecium, the vacuole fills, moves towards the cell surface and contracts to discharge fluid. Its major role in freshwater protists is osmoregulation (control of water balance); it can also help remove some dissolved wastes. It is not the same as a food vacuole, which contains food being digested.

## 4. Excretory structures in some invertebrates

| Organism/group | Structure | Main function |
|---|---|---|
| Flatworms such as Planaria | **Flame cells / protonephridia** | Ciliated cells and tubules move fluid and wastes towards excretory pores; help regulate water and salts. |
| Annelids such as earthworms | **Nephridia** | Tubules process body fluid; useful substances are reabsorbed and wastes leave through nephridiopores. |
| Many insects | **Malpighian tubules** | Remove nitrogenous wastes and ions from body fluid; water and useful ions can be reabsorbed, helping conserve water. |

## 5. Excretion in vertebrates
In vertebrates, the **kidneys** filter blood and regulate water and ion balance. Their functional units are **nephrons**. Urine formation involves:
1. **Filtration:** water and small solutes pass from blood into the nephron.
2. **Selective reabsorption:** useful substances and much of the water return to the blood.
3. **Secretion:** additional wastes and ions move from blood into the tubule.

Urine travels from the kidneys through the ureters to the urinary bladder and leaves the body through the urethra. The liver also helps process some harmful substances and converts ammonia to urea in humans.

## 6. Comparison and memory aid
- Contractile vacuole: a single cell's water balance.
- Flame cells: flatworms.
- Nephridia: annelid worms such as earthworms.
- Malpighian tubules: insects and some other arthropods.
- Kidneys/nephrons: vertebrates.
