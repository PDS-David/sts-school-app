## Biological fact findings
1. **Tapeworms (cestodes) are hermaphroditic.** Mature proglottids generally contain both male and female reproductive structures.
2. **Fertilisation is internal.** Sperm and eggs meet within reproductive structures of the tapeworm/proglottid. Many cestodes mainly cross-fertilise between different worms in the same host; some species can also self-fertilise between reproductive units. Avoid teaching that all tapeworms reproduce only by self-fertilisation.
3. Mature/gravid proglottids contain fertilised eggs and detach or release eggs, which pass out of the definitive host. The exact life cycle depends on the cestode species and its intermediate host.
4. A safe introductory answer to “Describe sexual reproduction in tapeworm” should mention hermaphroditic proglottids, cross-fertilisation (with the species qualification above), fertilised eggs, gravid segments and transmission to an intermediate host. It should not claim that every species has exactly the same number of proglottids or identical host cycle.

## Internal-fertilisation multiple-choice item is ambiguous
The Week 3 weekend question asks which of earthworm, tapeworm, butterfly and toad is the exception to internal fertilisation.

- Tapeworm: internal fertilisation within reproductive structures/proglottids.
- Butterfly: internal fertilisation.
- Earthworm: copulation transfers sperm, but fertilisation occurs in a cocoon outside the body after eggs and stored sperm are released; it is not internal fertilisation in the usual school classification.
- Toad: generally external fertilisation in water.

Therefore the printed question has **at least two defensible exceptions: earthworm and toad**. It cannot be given a single correct option as written. The original wording is preserved; do not silently mark only toad or only earthworm as correct.
