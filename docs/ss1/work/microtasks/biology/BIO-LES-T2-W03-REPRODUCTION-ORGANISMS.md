# Supplementary lesson: Reproduction in Amoeba, Paramecium, Spirogyra, Earthworm, Cockroach, Housefly and Snail (SS 1 Biology, Second Term, Week 3)

> This is a separately authored supplement based on the school's Second-Term Week 3 teaching. The original source and its figures remain unchanged; the diagrams registered with this lesson are project-authored redraws.

## Learning objectives
By the end of this lesson, you should be able to describe binary and multiple fission in Amoeba; binary fission and conjugation in Paramecium; fragmentation and conjugation in Spirogyra; reproduction in earthworms and land snails; and reproduction and metamorphosis in cockroaches and houseflies.

## 1. Reproduction in Amoeba
Amoeba reproduces asexually. **Binary fission** occurs when the nucleus divides and the cytoplasm divides to form two daughter amoebae. In **multiple fission**, an amoeba may form a protective cyst during unfavourable conditions. The nucleus divides repeatedly inside it. When conditions improve, the cyst opens and several small amoebae are released. The key difference is one division producing two offspring versus repeated nuclear divisions followed by release of several offspring.

## 2. Reproduction in Paramecium
Paramecium reproduces asexually by **binary fission** and can exchange genetic material by **conjugation**.

### Binary fission
1. The micronucleus divides by mitosis.
2. The macronucleus elongates and divides.
3. The cytoplasm constricts between the nuclear sets.
4. Two daughter Paramecia are formed.

### Conjugation
Conjugation is a temporary pairing and exchange/reorganisation of micronuclear genetic material between two compatible Paramecia. It is not permanent fusion of the whole organisms.
1. Two mature Paramecia come together near their oral grooves.
2. The micronucleus undergoes meiosis, producing haploid micronuclei.
3. Micronuclear material is exchanged between the paired cells.
4. The exchanged haploid micronucleus fuses with the stationary haploid micronucleus in each cell. This fusion is **syngamy**, restoring a diploid micronucleus.
5. Nuclear reorganisation follows; new macronuclear and micronuclear structures develop.
6. The conjugants separate and later divide.

The project-authored diagram follows the source's circular sequence and uses only these visible labels: Macronucleus, Diploid micronucleus, MEIOSIS, Haploid micronuclei, SYNGAMY, New macronuclei and Micronuclei.

## 3. Reproduction in Spirogyra
Spirogyra is a filamentous green alga. It reproduces asexually by **fragmentation** and sexually by **conjugation**.

**Fragmentation:** a filament breaks into pieces; a viable fragment can grow into a new filament.

**Scalariform conjugation:**
1. Two filaments lie side by side.
2. Opposite cells form connecting **conjugation tubes**.
3. The contents of one cell pass through a tube into the opposite cell.
4. The cell contents fuse to form a zygote.
5. The zygote develops a resistant wall and becomes a **zygospore**.
6. After a resting period and favourable conditions, the zygospore germinates and a new filament develops.

The three-panel diagram is read A → B → C: conjugation between filaments; transfer/fusion through a tube to form a zygote; then the resistant zygospore. The labels are Male gametangium, Conjugation process, Conjugation tube, Zygote, Empty male cell, Female cell and Zygospore.

## 4. Reproduction in earthworms
Earthworms are **hermaphrodites**: each mature worm has both male and female reproductive organs. Two worms usually come together and exchange sperm; this is cross-fertilisation.
1. Two worms lie close together with their ventral surfaces touching.
2. Bristles called **chaetae** help hold them in position.
3. They exchange sperm during copulation.
4. Later, the **clitellum** secretes a cocoon.
5. Eggs and stored sperm enter the cocoon, where fertilisation and early development take place.
6. Young worms hatch from the cocoon.

## 5. Reproduction in cockroaches
Cockroaches reproduce sexually and fertilisation is internal.
1. The male transfers sperm to the female.
2. Sperm is stored in the female reproductive system until eggs are released.
3. Fertilised eggs are enclosed in a protective case called an **ootheca** or egg case.
4. Young cockroaches hatch as **nymphs**, which resemble small, wingless adults.
5. Nymphs feed, grow and moult several times before becoming adults.

Cockroaches undergo **incomplete metamorphosis**: egg case → nymph → adult. The source illustration shows two nymph stages to represent successive growth stages: egg case → nymph → nymph → adult → egg case. There is no pupal stage.

## 6. Reproduction in houseflies
Houseflies reproduce sexually and fertilisation is internal. A female lays eggs, commonly in moist organic material. The young pass through four main stages:
1. **Egg:** the embryo develops inside the egg.
2. **Larva (maggot):** the larva feeds, grows and moults.
3. **Pupa:** the larva forms a protective puparium; major reorganisation occurs and it does not feed.
4. **Adult (imago):** the adult fly emerges from the puparium.

Houseflies undergo **complete metamorphosis**: egg → larva → pupa → adult. The adult lays eggs and the cycle repeats.

## 7. Reproduction in land snails
The school source describes land snails as hermaphrodites with internal fertilisation. In many land snails, two individuals exchange sperm; sperm may be transferred in a package called a **spermatophore**. Eggs are later laid and develop outside the parent's body. Details vary among snail species, so the source's structures should not be assumed to describe every snail.

The source also introduces **torsion**, a developmental rotation of the visceral mass in gastropods. Torsion is a body-development process, not fertilisation.

## 8. Cockroach and housefly compared

| Feature | Cockroach | Housefly |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Incomplete metamorphosis | Complete metamorphosis |
| Sequence | Egg/ootheca → nymph → adult | Egg → larva → pupa → adult |
| Immature stage | Nymph resembles a small adult | Larva (maggot) looks very different from adult |
| Pupal stage | Absent | Present |

A cockroach nymph is not a larva, and a cockroach does not have a pupal stage.

## 9. Worked examples
- A single-celled organism forms a cyst, its nucleus divides several times, and many small cells are released: **multiple fission in Amoeba**.
- Two Paramecia pair temporarily, exchange micronuclear material and undergo syngamy: **conjugation in Paramecium**.
- A filamentous alga forms conjugation tubes and a thick-walled zygospore: **conjugation in Spirogyra**.
- An insect hatches as a nymph and becomes an adult without a pupa: **incomplete metamorphosis**, as in cockroach.

## Key points
- Amoeba can reproduce by binary and multiple fission.
- Paramecium reproduces by binary fission and can undergo conjugation; syngamy is fusion of haploid micronuclei.
- Spirogyra reproduces by fragmentation and conjugation; a resistant zygospore forms.
- Earthworms are hermaphrodites but commonly exchange sperm with another worm.
- Cockroach: egg case → nymph → adult; incomplete metamorphosis.
- Housefly: egg → larva → pupa → adult; complete metamorphosis.
- Land snails are hermaphroditic in the school account; details vary by species.
