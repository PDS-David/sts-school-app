# Supplementary lesson: Phases of microbial growth (SS 1 Biology, Third Term, Week 2)

> The school's Week 2 note names four phases of bacterial growth but does not define them. This supplement explains the phases using a standard growth-curve model. It is conceptual only; it does not instruct students to culture or handle microorganisms.

## Learning objectives
Define lag, exponential/logarithmic, stationary and decline/death phases; explain why a microbial population changes through these phases.

## 1. What is microbial growth?
In microbiology, growth usually means an increase in the number of microbial cells in a population, not simply an increase in the size of one cell. Many bacteria reproduce by binary fission. Under suitable conditions, one cell divides into two, and the population can increase rapidly.

A typical closed-batch culture produces a growth curve with four phases. The exact curve depends on the organism and conditions.

## 2. Four phases

### 2.1 Lag phase
Cells adjust to the new environment. They may repair cell components, make enzymes and prepare for division. The number of cells may change little, even though the cells are metabolically active.

### 2.2 Exponential (logarithmic) phase
Cells divide at a relatively constant maximum rate under the available conditions. The population increases rapidly; the number may double at regular intervals called the **generation time** when conditions remain suitable.

### 2.3 Stationary phase
The rate of new cell formation is approximately balanced by the rate of cell death. Nutrients may become limited and waste products may accumulate. The total number of living cells therefore remains roughly stable for a period.

### 2.4 Decline (death) phase
The number of living cells decreases because cells die faster than new cells are produced. This can occur as nutrients become exhausted and harmful waste products accumulate.

## 3. Factors affecting microbial growth
Important factors include:
- availability of nutrients and water;
- temperature;
- pH;
- oxygen or other gaseous requirements;
- salt concentration and other environmental conditions.

Different microorganisms have different optimum conditions. Do not assume every microbe grows best at human body temperature or in the presence of oxygen.

## 4. Worked example
A bacterial population is transferred into a new suitable environment. At first, the cells adjust and few divide (lag). They then divide rapidly (exponential). Later, nutrient shortage and waste accumulation balance new cell production with cell death (stationary). Finally, living cell numbers fall (decline).
