# Supplementary lesson: General control of harmful microorganisms (SS 1 Biology, Third Term, Week 2)

> The Week 2 general evaluation asks for ways to control microorganisms, but the source discusses only scattered examples. This supplement organises the ideas at a safe, introductory level. It does not provide chemical recipes, sterilisation settings, culture methods or instructions for handling pathogens.

## Learning objectives
Explain general principles for preventing the spread of harmful microorganisms and distinguish cleaning, disinfection, sterilisation, vaccination and antibiotic treatment.

## 1. Why control harmful microorganisms?
Some microorganisms cause disease or spoil food. Control measures aim to prevent transmission, reduce contamination, remove microbes from surfaces or materials, or protect people and animals from infection.

## 2. General methods

### 2.1 Personal hygiene
- Wash hands with soap and clean water at appropriate times, especially after using the toilet and before preparing or eating food.
- Cover coughs and sneezes and dispose of tissues safely.
- Avoid sharing items that may carry blood or body fluids.
- Keep cuts covered with clean dressings.

### 2.2 Safe food and water
- Use safe drinking water.
- Wash produce and prepare food on clean surfaces.
- Cook foods thoroughly and store perishable foods safely.
- Cover food to protect it from flies, dust and other sources of contamination.
- Refrigeration slows the growth of many microbes but does not sterilise food.

### 2.3 Sanitation and waste management
- Dispose of refuse and sewage safely.
- Maintain clean toilets, kitchens and water-storage containers.
- Prevent stagnant water and manage vector breeding sites.
- Keep animal housing clean and avoid overcrowding.

### 2.4 Physical control
- **Heat:** cooking, boiling and properly controlled pasteurisation can reduce or kill many microorganisms. Different processes have different purposes; pasteurisation is not the same as sterilisation.
- **Cold:** refrigeration and freezing slow the growth of many microorganisms but may not kill them.
- **Drying:** removes available water and makes growth difficult for many microbes.
- **Filtration:** suitable filters can remove microorganisms from water or air, depending on filter design and pore size.
- **Sterilisation:** a validated process that destroys or removes all forms of microbial life, including bacterial spores. It is normally performed using approved equipment by trained personnel.

### 2.5 Chemical control
- **Antiseptics** are products designed for use on living tissue to reduce microorganisms.
- **Disinfectants** are used on inanimate surfaces and objects; they may damage skin and must be used only according to their labels.
- **Sanitisers** reduce microbial contamination to an acceptable level in the intended setting.
- Never mix cleaning chemicals or prepare stronger solutions than the product label directs.

### 2.6 Medicines and vaccination
- **Antibiotics** treat certain bacterial infections; they do not treat viral infections such as common colds or measles. They should be used only when prescribed by an authorised health professional and exactly as directed.
- **Vaccines** train the immune system to recognise particular pathogens and help prevent specific infections.
- Antiviral and antiparasitic medicines are used for certain viral and parasitic infections under medical guidance.

## 3. Vector control
Vectors such as mosquitoes and tsetse flies can transmit pathogens. General prevention includes reducing breeding sites, using approved protective barriers such as mosquito nets, maintaining clean surroundings and following public-health guidance. Insecticides must be handled only according to official instructions by trained or authorised persons.

## 4. Worked examples
- Covering cooked food helps prevent contamination by flies and dust.
- Refrigerating food slows microbial growth but does not make contaminated food safe indefinitely.
- Antibiotics may treat a susceptible bacterial infection but do not cure a viral infection.
- Vaccination can prevent specific diseases but does not protect against every microorganism.

## 5. Practice
1. State four general methods of controlling harmful microorganisms. (4)
2. Distinguish an antiseptic from a disinfectant. (2)
3. Explain why refrigeration does not sterilise food. (2)
4. Why should antibiotics not be used to treat a common cold? (2)
5. State two ways to reduce transmission by insect vectors. (2)
