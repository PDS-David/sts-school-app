# Supplementary lesson: Completing the five conditions for photosynthesis (SS 1 Biology, First Term, Week 5)

> The school's Week 5 assignment asks for five conditions necessary for photosynthesis. Its teaching note names sunlight, chlorophyll, water and carbon dioxide, but does not name a fifth. This supplement adds the missing scientifically supported condition—a suitable temperature—without changing the original school note. It does not authorise or replace the school's practical procedures.

## Learning objectives
By the end, you should be able to name five conditions or requirements for photosynthesis, explain their roles and explain why a shortage can limit the rate.

## 1. Meaning of photosynthesis
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria use light energy to make organic food from carbon dioxide and water. In green plants, chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs light energy. Oxygen is released.

Simplified word equation:

**carbon dioxide + water —(light energy and chlorophyll)→ carbohydrate + oxygen**

Light supplies energy; chlorophyll is a pigment that absorbs light. Neither should be treated as an ordinary chemical product.

## 2. Five conditions or requirements

| Condition | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| **1. Light** | Supplies energy for the light-dependent reactions. Sunlight is the usual natural source. |
| **2. Chlorophyll / photosynthetic pigments** | Chlorophyll absorbs light energy in green plant chloroplasts. |
| **3. Water** | A raw material used in the light-dependent reactions. Its splitting contributes electrons and protons, and oxygen is released. |
| **4. Carbon dioxide** | Supplies the carbon incorporated into organic molecules during the Calvin cycle. |
| **5. Suitable temperature** | Photosynthesis includes enzyme-controlled reactions. Low temperatures slow reactions; excessive heat can disrupt enzymes and reduce the rate. |

**Important distinction:** light, water and carbon dioxide are inputs; chlorophyll is a pigment; temperature is an environmental condition affecting the rate. School questions often group all five as “conditions necessary for photosynthesis”.

## 3. Limiting factors
A **limiting factor** is a factor in shortest supply, or least favourable, that restricts the rate of a process. A plant may receive plenty of light but have too little water; the shortage can reduce photosynthesis. Increasing one factor does not always increase the rate because another factor may become limiting.

- More light can raise the rate when light is limiting, up to a saturation point.
- More carbon dioxide can raise the rate when carbon dioxide is limiting, up to a point.
- A temperature rise towards a suitable optimum may increase enzyme activity; excessive heat can reduce it.
- Water shortage can reduce photosynthesis and may cause stomata to close, limiting carbon dioxide entry.

## 4. Worked examples
**Example 1:** A green plant receives water and carbon dioxide but is kept in complete darkness. Which requirement is missing?  
**Answer:** Light. Without light energy, the light-dependent reactions cannot proceed normally.

**Example 2:** Two similar plants receive equal light and carbon dioxide. One is severely short of water. Why may it photosynthesise more slowly?  
**Answer:** Water is a raw material. Water shortage can also close stomata and reduce carbon dioxide entry.

**Example 3:** A plant is moved from a very cold place to a warmer place and its photosynthesis rate increases. Does any increase in temperature always increase the rate?  
**Answer:** No. A rise towards a suitable optimum may help, but excessive heat can disrupt enzymes and reduce the rate.

## 5. Safe observation activity
With teacher permission, observe two similar healthy potted plants, one in a well-lit place and one in a shaded place. Keep watering as similar as possible and record the conditions and visible observations over several days. Do not remove leaves, apply chemicals or heat plants. Leaf colour alone is not a quantitative measurement of photosynthesis. This is an observation activity, not a chemical practical.

## 6. Practice
1. List the five conditions or requirements for photosynthesis. (5)
2. State the role of (a) chlorophyll and (b) carbon dioxide. (2)
3. Explain “limiting factor”. (2)
4. A plant has water and carbon dioxide but no light. Explain why photosynthesis cannot proceed normally. (2)
5. Explain why a very high temperature may reduce photosynthesis. (2)

## Key points
The missing fifth condition in the source assignment is a suitable temperature. Photosynthesis also requires light, photosynthetic pigments, water and carbon dioxide. Several factors can limit the rate.
