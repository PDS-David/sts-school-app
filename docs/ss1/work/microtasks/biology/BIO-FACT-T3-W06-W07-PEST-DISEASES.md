## 1. Crop pests

| Source name | Fact finding / correction | Safe student-facing control principle |
|---|---|---|
| Grasshopper | Nymphs and adults of several grasshopper species feed on leaves and shoots; species and damage vary. | Monitor and use integrated pest management, including field hygiene, habitat management and approved biological/cultural options. |
| Yam beetle | “Yam beetle” may refer to *Heteroligus* species (Coleoptera: Dynastinae); adults damage yam tubers. A single species cannot be inferred from the common name alone. | Use healthy planting material, field sanitation, crop rotation/planting practices and locally recommended IPM. |
| Cocoa “myriads/capsids” | The intended group is likely cocoa capsids/mirids (e.g. *Sahlbergella singularis*, *Distantiella theobroma*). They pierce/suck cocoa tissues and damage shoots/pods. | Use monitoring, farm sanitation, shade/canopy management and approved integrated approaches. |
| Mealybug | Cassava mealybug *Phenacoccus manihoti* is a sap-sucking pest that can distort shoot growth. The source confuses mealybug injury with cassava mosaic transmission: cassava mosaic viruses are mainly spread by infected cuttings and whiteflies, not cassava mealybugs. | Use clean planting material, resistant varieties and biological control where locally established. |
| Bean weevil | The common storage bruchid is *Callosobruchus maculatus*; larvae develop inside stored cowpea/bean seeds and reduce quantity and quality. | Dry grain properly, clean storage structures, use hermetic storage and monitor stores. Fumigation is not a student procedure and requires licensed professionals. |
| Cotton stainer | Correct common term is **cotton stainer** (e.g. *Dysdercus* spp.); bugs feed on developing cotton seeds and stain lint. | Use field monitoring, sanitation and locally approved IPM. |
| Mammals and birds | Rodents, monkeys, grasscutters and birds may feed on crops; impact depends on crop, species and locality. | Exclusion, crop protection and humane, locally lawful management; do not prescribe shooting or poisons as general student advice. |

**Chemical-control warning:** the source recommends Gammalin 20 (lindane/gamma-HCH), aldrin, Rogor (dimethoate), DDT and other products. Aldrin and lindane are listed under Annex A of the Stockholm Convention for elimination, while DDT is restricted under Annex B with a limited disease-vector-control acceptable purpose. These are not suitable student recommendations. No product choice, dose, concentration, application method or fumigation instruction is reproduced here.

## 2. Livestock pests and parasites

| Source row | Corrected scientific interpretation |
|---|---|
| Ticks | Ectoparasitic arachnids attach to livestock, feed on blood and may cause irritation, skin damage, anaemia and transmit pathogens. Control belongs to veterinary integrated parasite management. |
| Liver fluke | *Fasciola hepatica* (and the tropical species *F. gigantica*) causes fasciolosis. Snails are intermediate hosts; infective metacercariae encyst on aquatic vegetation or other surfaces and are ingested by grazing animals. The source's “cysts in water” description is incomplete. Copper-sulphate treatment of pools is not a safe general instruction. |
| Roundworm | *Ascaris suum* is primarily the pig roundworm, not a general cattle parasite. It can impair growth and cause intestinal/liver/lung pathology. Use species-specific veterinary diagnosis and parasite control. |
| Lice | Poultry lice include several species; some are chewing lice and some can feed on blood/skin debris. The blanket statement that all lice suck blood is inaccurate. |
| Tsetse fly | Tsetse flies transmit animal trypanosomes (e.g. *Trypanosoma congolense*, *T. vivax*, *T. brucei*) causing **animal trypanosomosis/nagana** in livestock. Human African trypanosomiasis (“sleeping sickness”) is a separate disease caused by human-infective subspecies. |

## 3. Plant diseases

| Source disease | Causative agent / fact correction | Key qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Maize rust | Rust fungi include *Puccinia sorghi* and *Puccinia polysora* (different rust diseases). | Typical symptoms are rust-coloured pustules on leaves; the source's “cob and grains covered with black spores” is not a reliable general description. |
| Groundnut rosette | A disease complex involving Groundnut rosette virus, satellite RNA and Groundnut rosette assistor virus; transmitted by the cowpea aphid *Aphis craccivora*. | It is not accurately described as one unspecified virus alone. Symptoms include rosetting, chlorosis/mosaic and stunting. |
| Rice blast | Fungus *Magnaporthe oryzae* (syn. *Pyricularia oryzae*). | Lesions can affect leaves, nodes and panicles; brown leaf spots alone are an incomplete description. |
| Cassava mosaic | Cassava mosaic begomoviruses, spread by infected stem cuttings and whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*). | Symptoms include mosaic, leaf distortion and stunting. |
| Cocoa black pod | *Phytophthora palmivora* and, in parts of West Africa, *P. megakarya*; these are oomycetes (water moulds), not true fungi. | Brown lesions progress to black pod rot; sanitation and resistant material are important. |
| Cassava wilt / bacterial blight | Cassava bacterial blight is caused by *Xanthomonas* (currently classified as *X. phaseoli* pv. *manihotis*; older sources use *X. axonopodis* pv. *manihotis*). | Symptoms can include water-soaked leaf lesions, wilting, dieback and vascular discoloration; “leaves wilt and fall off” alone is insufficient. |
| Cocoa swollen shoot | Cacao swollen shoot virus (CSSV), a badnavirus, transmitted by mealybugs. | Symptoms include stem swelling, leaf vein clearing/chlorosis and yield decline; there is no curative treatment for infected trees. |

## 4. Animal diseases

| Source disease | Causative agent / fact correction | Key qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Newcastle disease (poultry) | Virulent Newcastle disease virus, commonly classified as avian orthoavulavirus 1 (formerly avian paramyxovirus 1). | Respiratory, digestive and nervous signs can occur; vaccination and biosecurity are central. |
| Rinderpest (cattle) | Rinderpest virus. | **Globally eradicated in 2011.** It must be labelled historical/eradicated, not taught as a currently circulating livestock disease. |
| Anthrax | *Bacillus anthracis*, a spore-forming bacterium. | Spores persist in soil; disease is zoonotic and can cause sudden death. Carcass handling/disposal is a veterinary/public-health matter; no handling procedure is given. |
| Coccidiosis (poultry) | Protozoan parasites of the genus *Eimeria* (species-specific). | Causes intestinal disease, poor growth and sometimes bloody droppings; prevention involves hygiene, litter management, appropriate stocking and veterinary guidance. |
| Trypanosomiasis (cattle) | Animal trypanosomes such as *T. congolense*, *T. vivax* and *T. brucei*. | Transmitted mainly by tsetse flies in affected areas; use the term animal trypanosomosis/nagana, not human sleeping sickness. |

## 5. Disposition of source controls
- Remove aldrin and lindane/Gammalin 20 from student recommendations; aldrin and lindane are Stockholm Convention Annex A chemicals.
- DDT is Annex B restricted, with a limited acceptable purpose for disease vector control; it is not a general crop/livestock pest treatment.
- Dimethoate/Rogor and other pesticide recommendations require current Nigerian registration and safety review; no chemical application instruction is included.
- Fumigation requires authorised trained operators and approved procedures; it must not be a student practical.
- Replace product-based control lists with general IPM principles and teacher/veterinary/extension guidance.
