# Supplementary lesson: Sexual reproduction, gametogenesis and meiosis (SS 1 Biology, Second Term, Week 2)

> This lesson develops the school's Week 2 topics. It distinguishes the source's conjugation account from fertilisation by gamete fusion and explains the terms and meiotic stages required by its evaluation questions.

## Learning objectives
Define sexual reproduction, describe conjugation and gamete fusion, define gametogenesis/spermatogenesis/oogenesis, outline meiosis and explain its importance.

## 1. Sexual reproduction
**Sexual reproduction** involves the combination of genetic material from two haploid gametes, usually by fertilisation. The gametes fuse to form a diploid **zygote**, which can develop into a new organism. The offspring are genetically different from either parent in many respects.

A **haploid** cell has one set of chromosomes (n). A **diploid** cell has two sets (2n). Fertilisation restores the diploid chromosome number after meiosis has reduced it.

## 2. Conjugation
**Conjugation** is a process in which two compatible cells or organisms temporarily join and exchange or combine genetic material. The details differ among organisms.

- In **Spirogyra**, two adjacent filaments form conjugation tubes. The contents of one cell move into a cell of the other filament and fuse with its contents. A thick-walled zygospore forms and can survive unfavourable conditions.
- In **Paramecium**, conjugation involves temporary pairing and exchange of micronuclear genetic material. It is not simply two whole organisms merging into one. The cells separate after the exchange and reorganisation of nuclear material.

Conjugation in these organisms is a sexual process because genetic material is recombined. Do not confuse it with binary fission, which is asexual cell division.

## 3. Fusion of gametes and fertilisation
**Gametes** are specialised reproductive cells. The male gamete is usually smaller; the female gamete is usually larger. **Fertilisation** is the fusion of the nuclei of compatible haploid gametes to form a diploid zygote.

Fertilisation may be:
- **External:** gametes meet outside the bodies of the parents, commonly in water.
- **Internal:** sperm is transferred into the reproductive tract of the female, where fertilisation occurs.

## 4. Gametogenesis
**Gametogenesis** means the production of gametes.

- **Spermatogenesis** is the process of producing sperm cells in the testes. A diploid primary spermatocyte undergoes meiosis I and meiosis II, producing four haploid spermatids; these mature into sperm.
- **Oogenesis** is the process of producing an egg cell (ovum) in the ovary. It involves meiosis and unequal division of cytoplasm, producing one large functional egg and smaller polar bodies in the typical human pattern.

The details and timing of oogenesis differ from spermatogenesis. Do not say that every meiotic product becomes a functional egg.

## 5. Meiosis
**Meiosis** is a type of cell division in which one diploid nucleus undergoes one round of DNA replication followed by two nuclear divisions, producing haploid cells. In many animals, these haploid cells become gametes.

### Before meiosis
During interphase, the cell grows and DNA is copied once. Each chromosome now consists of two sister chromatids joined at a centromere.

### Meiosis I — reduction division
- **Prophase I:** homologous chromosomes pair; crossing over may occur between non-sister chromatids.
- **Metaphase I:** homologous pairs line up at the cell equator.
- **Anaphase I:** homologous chromosomes move to opposite poles; sister chromatids remain joined.
- **Telophase I and cytokinesis:** the cell divides into two haploid cells, each with duplicated chromosomes.

### Meiosis II — separation of sister chromatids
- **Prophase II:** a new spindle forms if needed.
- **Metaphase II:** chromosomes line up individually at the equator.
- **Anaphase II:** sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.
- **Telophase II and cytokinesis:** nuclei reform and cells divide. The usual result from one diploid starting cell is four haploid cells.

### Mitosis compared with meiosis
| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Nuclear divisions | One | Two |
| DNA replication | Once before division | Once before meiosis I; none between I and II |
| Chromosome number | Usually maintained | Halved from diploid to haploid |
| Usual products | Two daughter cells | Four haploid products |
| Genetic similarity | Usually genetically very similar | Products are genetically varied |

## 6. Importance of meiosis
1. It halves the chromosome number to produce haploid cells, so fertilisation restores rather than doubles the species' chromosome number.
2. Crossing over creates new combinations of genetic material.
3. Independent assortment of homologous chromosomes creates further variation.
4. Genetic variation contributes to differences among offspring and can help populations respond to changing environments.
