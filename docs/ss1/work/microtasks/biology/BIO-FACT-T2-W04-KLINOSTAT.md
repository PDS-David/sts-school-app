## Verified finding
A **klinostat** (also commonly spelled **clinostat**) is a device that slowly rotates a plant or plant organ around a horizontal axis. Rotation continually changes the direction of the organ relative to gravity. Over time, this averages the one-sided directional effect of gravity and reduces the usual gravitropic bending response. It is used to investigate the role of gravity in plant growth responses.

A stationary horizontal seedling receives a consistent gravitational direction: roots tend to bend downwards (positive gravitropism) and shoots tend to bend upwards (negative gravitropism). On a slowly rotating horizontal clinostat, the direction of gravity relative to the seedling changes continuously; the root and shoot commonly continue growing without the same consistent one-sided curvature.

**Answer basis for the source question:** A klinostat is used to demonstrate or investigate the effect of gravity on plant growth (geotropism/gravitropism) by averaging the directional effect of gravity through rotation.

## Important qualifications
- A clinostat does **not remove gravity** or create true weightlessness. It averages the direction of the gravity stimulus relative to the rotating specimen.
- Rotation speed, orientation, seedling condition, moisture and light direction matter. A clinostat is not a guarantee of perfectly horizontal growth in every setup.
- If investigating gravity, light should be kept uniform or excluded so phototropism does not confuse the observation.
- This report verifies the concept only. It does not author a practical procedure, rotation rate, apparatus specification or safety instruction for SS1 students.
